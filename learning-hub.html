CBC/CBE explained for Kenyan parents

Competency-Based Education focuses on what a learner can demonstrate, not only what the learner can recall. For parents, this means looking beyond marks to the skills, knowledge, values and application shown in school work. A useful home routine is simple: ask what the learner is working on, ask them to explain the idea in their own words, and encourage them to demonstrate it through a small practical task where appropriate.

Parents can also help by keeping communication open with teachers, reviewing school feedback and creating a regular study routine. The aim is not to replace classroom teaching but to give the learner time, encouragement and a suitable environment for practice.

How to prepare for KJSEA

KJSEA preparation should be based on the prescribed curriculum, regular practice and feedback. Start by identifying subjects and learning areas that need more attention. Use short revision sessions, practice questions, corrections and spaced review instead of waiting until the final weeks.

For Grade 9 learners, preparation should also include understanding the assessment requirements and following instructions from the school. KNEC states that KJSEA is intended to measure competencies and provide feedback that helps guide pathway choices at senior school. The official KNEC regulations should therefore be the reference point for assessment requirements.

How teachers can use CBE assessment

Assessment becomes more useful when it produces information that changes teaching. Teachers can combine classroom observation, tasks, questions, projects and other school-based assessment evidence with feedback and targeted intervention. After an assessment, identify the specific skill or learning outcome that needs support, reteach it in a different way, and give the learner another opportunity to demonstrate improvement.

KNEC guidance emphasizes tracking learner achievement, preparing progress reports and providing feedback to learners and parents or guardians. Keep assessment records organized so that evidence can support decisions about learning support.

CBC resources for Grade 7–9

Good Grade 7–9 resources should be clearly labelled by grade, subject and resource type. Before using a resource, check its learning area, topic or strand, instructions, difficulty level and whether it matches the current curriculum design. For revision, combine concise notes with questions and marking guidance so learners can practise and correct mistakes.

KICD publishes curriculum designs for Grade 7, Grade 8 and Grade 9 learning areas. CBE Nexus resources should complement those official curriculum references rather than replace them.

How parents can support learners at home

A practical home study routine does not need to be complicated. Choose a consistent time, set one or two clear learning goals, reduce distractions and allow short breaks. Ask the learner to explain what they learned instead of only asking whether homework is finished.

When a learner struggles, focus on the specific difficulty rather than comparing them with other learners. Review the teacher's feedback, practise the skill in small steps and communicate with the school when additional support is needed. Encourage reading, problem-solving, practical activities and responsible use of digital learning resources.
