Universities with Letters of Interim Authority: applicant guide

A Letter of Interim Authority (LIA) is a specific regulatory status in Kenya’s university framework. It should not be confused with a university charter or treated as a blanket statement that every programme, campus or qualification is approved. Applicants should check the institution’s latest status before enrolling.

What an interim authority indicates

An LIA is a formal stage in the regulatory pathway for an institution seeking to develop as a university. It is distinct from a charter. Exact permissions and conditions depend on the legal instrument and regulator’s decisions, so applicants should consult current official records.

Verify current institutional status

Institutional status can change after regulatory review. Before applying, check the latest CUE institutional list, notices and any conditions or decisions affecting the institution. Confirm the institution’s name and status exactly as published.

Confirm programme approval separately

Do not assume institutional status validates every advertised course. Ask for evidence that the specific programme, campus, delivery mode and intake are authorised. For professional programmes, also consult the relevant regulator.

Understand the qualification and award

Ask which legal entity registers students, conducts examinations and awards the qualification. Obtain written confirmation of the qualification title and award authority.

Protect your application and fees

Use official admissions portals and institution-controlled payment channels. Read refund policy, fee schedule and student contract before paying. Keep offer letters, receipts and correspondence.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Letter of Interim Authority the same as a charter?

No. They are different regulatory statuses.

Does an LIA mean every course is approved?

No. Verify the particular programme, campus and delivery mode.

Where should I confirm current status?

Check the Commission for University Education’s latest official records and notices.

Official sources and further checks

Use the Commission for University Education (CUE) for university status and quality-assurance information, and KUCCPS for current placement and programme information where applicable. For TVET and technical qualifications, also consult the relevant TVET regulator and the institution’s official admissions office. Always verify current information before applying or paying.
