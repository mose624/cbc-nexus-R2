Grade 10 · Biology · Plant nutrition

Photosynthesis: how green plants make food

Understand the stages and requirements of photosynthesis, interpret the equation, investigate starch formation and explain how light intensity, carbon dioxide and temperature affect the rate.

CBE Nexus original study guide · Process diagram, experiment and revision questions

1. Meaning and importance of photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms use light energy to make organic substances from carbon dioxide and water. In green plants, the process takes place in chloroplasts, which contain chlorophyll to absorb light. The chemical energy stored in the products can later be released during respiration.

Photosynthesis supports food chains because plants make organic molecules that provide food for themselves and other organisms. It also releases oxygen into the atmosphere.

2. Word and chemical equations

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The balanced equation is a simplified overall representation. Light energy, absorbed by chlorophyll, is required for the process. In the equation, carbon dioxide and water are reactants; glucose and oxygen are products.

3. Adaptations of a leaf for photosynthesis

  • A broad, flat lamina provides a large surface area for absorbing light.
  • A thin leaf allows gases to diffuse over a short distance.
  • Many stomata allow carbon dioxide to enter and oxygen to leave; guard cells regulate their opening.
  • Palisade mesophyll cells near the upper surface contain many chloroplasts.
  • Veins deliver water through xylem and transport manufactured sugars through phloem.

4. Factors that affect the rate

A limiting factor is a condition in shortest supply relative to the needs of the process. Increasing a limiting factor can increase the rate until another factor becomes limiting.

Light intensity

When light intensity is low, increasing it usually increases the rate because more energy is available. At high intensity, the rate may level off when carbon dioxide concentration or temperature becomes limiting.

Carbon dioxide concentration

Carbon dioxide is a raw material. Increasing its concentration can increase the rate until another factor limits the process.

Temperature

Photosynthesis involves enzyme-controlled reactions. At low temperatures, reactions are slower. As temperature rises, the rate may increase to an optimum. Above the optimum, enzymes may lose their functional shape and the rate falls.

5. Investigating starch in a leaf

A common test checks whether a leaf contains starch. A leaf is first boiled in water to stop chemical reactions. It is then heated in ethanol in a hot-water bath to remove chlorophyll. The leaf is rinsed in warm water to soften it and placed on a white tile. Iodine solution is added. A blue-black colour indicates starch; a yellow-brown colour indicates that starch was not detected.

Safety: Ethanol is flammable. It must never be heated directly over a flame. Use a hot-water bath and follow the teacher’s laboratory instructions.

6. Worked example: interpreting an investigation

Example

A learner records oxygen bubbles released by pondweed in one minute at different lamp distances.

Question: Describe the trend and suggest why it occurs.

  1. As the lamp is moved farther away, the number of bubbles decreases.
  2. Greater distance reduces light intensity reaching the pondweed.
  3. With less light energy, the rate of photosynthesis falls, so less oxygen is released per minute.
Conclusion: Under the conditions of this investigation, light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis.

7. Variables and fair testing

When investigating one factor, change that independent variable and measure the dependent variable, such as oxygen volume per minute. Keep other conditions constant, including plant species, carbon dioxide supply, water temperature and measurement time. Repeat readings and calculate a mean to improve reliability. Bubble counting is an estimate; collecting gas volume can provide a more direct measurement.

8. Assessment questions

1. State the function of chlorophyll in photosynthesis.
Chlorophyll absorbs light energy that drives photosynthesis.
2. Name the two raw materials used in photosynthesis.
Carbon dioxide and water.
3. Give two adaptations of a leaf for photosynthesis.
Any two explained points: broad lamina for light absorption; thin structure for short diffusion distance; palisade cells with many chloroplasts; stomata for gas exchange; xylem to supply water.
4. Why is ethanol heated in a water bath during the starch test?
Ethanol is flammable, so it must not be heated directly over a flame. A water bath provides safer indirect heating.
5. Explain why the rate of photosynthesis may stop increasing when light intensity is increased further.
Another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, may become limiting. The rate cannot increase until that limiting factor is improved.
Exam technique: When explaining an investigation, identify the trend using data, name the factor changed and connect it to the biological process. Distinguish between raw materials, products and conditions needed for the process.
