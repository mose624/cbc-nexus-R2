Linear equations: solving for an unknown

Learn how to solve linear equations by keeping both sides balanced. Practise equations with brackets, unknowns on both sides and short real-life problems.

CBE Nexus original study guide · Step-by-step methods and self-assessment

1. What is a linear equation?

An equation states that two mathematical expressions have equal values. A linear equation in one unknown has the unknown raised to the first power only. For example, 3x + 5 = 20 is linear because x is not squared or multiplied by another unknown.

Balance principle: An equation behaves like a balanced scale. Whatever operation you perform on one side must also be performed on the other side to preserve equality.

2. Inverse operations

Use inverse operations to isolate the unknown. Addition is undone by subtraction; subtraction is undone by addition; multiplication is undone by division; and division is undone by multiplication. Work in reverse order of operations while maintaining equality.

3. Worked example: a two-step equation

Example 1: Solve 3x + 5 = 20

  1. Subtract 5 from both sides: 3x + 5 − 5 = 20 − 5, so 3x = 15.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 15 ÷ 3.
  3. Therefore x = 5.
Check: Substitute x = 5 into the original equation: 3(5) + 5 = 15 + 5 = 20. Both sides are equal.

4. Equations with brackets

Use the distributive property to multiply the term outside the bracket by every term inside it. For example, 2(x + 4) = 2x + 8. Then collect like terms and isolate the unknown.

Example 2: Solve 2(x + 4) = 18

  1. Expand the bracket: 2x + 8 = 18.
  2. Subtract 8 from both sides: 2x = 10.
  3. Divide by 2: x = 5.
Check: 2(5 + 4) = 2 × 9 = 18.

5. Unknowns on both sides

Example 3: Solve 5x − 7 = 2x + 8

  1. Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x − 7 = 8.
  2. Add 7 to both sides: 3x = 15.
  3. Divide by 3: x = 5.
Check: Left side = 5(5) − 7 = 18. Right side = 2(5) + 8 = 18.

6. Forming an equation from a word problem

Read the problem carefully, identify the unknown, represent it with a letter, translate the relationships into an equation and solve. Finally, interpret the answer in the context of the question.

Example 4: consecutive numbers

The sum of a number and twice the number is 36. Find the number.

  1. Let the number be x.
  2. Form the equation: x + 2x = 36.
  3. Collect like terms: 3x = 36.
  4. Divide by 3: x = 12.
Answer: The number is 12. Check: 12 + 2(12) = 36.

7. Common errors to avoid

  • Changing a sign incorrectly when moving a term; instead, show the same operation on both sides.
  • Expanding a bracket by multiplying only the first term.
  • Combining unlike terms such as 3x and 4.
  • Failing to substitute the answer into the original equation to check it.
  • Giving a numerical answer without explaining what it represents in a word problem.

8. Assessment questions

1. Solve x + 9 = 17.
Subtract 9 from both sides: x = 17 − 9 = 8.
2. Solve 4x − 6 = 18.
Add 6: 4x = 24. Divide by 4: x = 6.
3. Solve 3(x − 2) = 21.
Expand: 3x − 6 = 21. Add 6: 3x = 27. Divide by 3: x = 9.
4. Solve 7x + 2 = 4x + 20.
Subtract 4x: 3x + 2 = 20. Subtract 2: 3x = 18. Therefore x = 6.
5. Three identical exercise books and a pen costing KSh 20 cost KSh 200 altogether. Find the cost of one exercise book.
Let the cost of one book be x. 3x + 20 = 200; 3x = 180; x = KSh 60.
Exam technique: Keep each line of working aligned, perform the same operation on both sides and check your answer in the original equation. In word problems, define the unknown before forming the equation.
