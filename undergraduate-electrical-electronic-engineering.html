Electrical and Electronic Engineering

A four-year undergraduate framework for designing, analysing and maintaining electrical, electronic, communication, control and energy systems.

Global University Courses / Electrical and Electronic Engineering

About the programme

Electrical and electronic engineering applies mathematics, physics, computation and engineering design to systems that generate, convert, distribute, control and use electrical energy and information. Students develop analytical skills through circuit theory, electronic devices, electromagnetic fields, machines, power networks, signals, control and communications. Laboratory work connects theoretical models with measurement, simulation, safety and practical engineering constraints.

This illustrative framework is arranged into 16 broad chapters across four academic years. Actual degree structures, credit loads, laboratory requirements and specialisations vary among universities and accrediting bodies.

Safety guidance: This is an educational course framework, not an accredited curriculum or authority to undertake regulated electrical work. Follow laboratory rules and local engineering registration requirements. High-voltage and mains experiments require properly equipped, supervised facilities.

Year 1 — Mathematical and Physical Foundations

CHAPTER 01 · YEAR 1

Engineering Mathematics and Computational Methods

Calculus, linear algebra, complex numbers, differential equations, vector analysis, numerical methods, probability and computational modelling. Apply mathematical tools to transient response, sinusoidal signals, fields and engineering optimisation.

CHAPTER 02 · YEAR 1

Engineering Physics and Electrical Principles

Charge, current, voltage, energy, electric and magnetic fields, materials, electromagnetic induction, waves and semiconductor foundations. Connect physical laws to devices, sensors, energy conversion and measurement.

CHAPTER 03 · YEAR 1

Electrical Circuit Analysis I

Circuit variables, passive sign convention, resistance, capacitance, inductance, sources, Kirchhoff's laws, series and parallel networks, nodal and mesh analysis, network theorems and first-order transient circuits.

CHAPTER 04 · YEAR 1

Programming, Engineering Graphics and Workshop Practice

Computational problem-solving, technical drawing, CAD fundamentals, engineering documentation, measurement instruments, component identification, prototyping and safe workshop practice.

Year 2 — Core Electrical and Electronic Systems

CHAPTER 05 · YEAR 2

Electrical Circuit Analysis II and Signals

Sinusoidal steady state, phasors, impedance, resonance, frequency response, coupled circuits, three-phase systems, Laplace transforms, transfer functions and signal representations.

CHAPTER 06 · YEAR 2

Analogue Electronics and Semiconductor Devices

Diodes, rectifiers, transistors, biasing, small-signal models, operational amplifiers, amplifiers, feedback, filters, oscillators and regulated power supplies.

CHAPTER 07 · YEAR 2

Digital Electronics and Logic Design

Number systems, Boolean algebra, logic gates, combinational circuits, sequential logic, flip-flops, counters, registers, finite-state machines, timing and programmable logic.

CHAPTER 08 · YEAR 2

Electromagnetic Fields and Electrical Measurements

Electrostatic and magnetostatic field laws, Maxwell-equation foundations, induction, magnetic circuits, measurement principles, uncertainty, sensors, transducers, oscilloscopes and calibration.

Year 3 — Machines, Power, Control and Communications

CHAPTER 09 · YEAR 3

Electrical Machines and Drives

Transformers, DC machines, induction motors, synchronous machines, equivalent circuits, torque-speed characteristics, losses, efficiency, starting and speed control.

CHAPTER 10 · YEAR 3

Power Generation, Transmission and Distribution

Energy sources, generators, transmission lines, distribution networks, load flow, voltage regulation, protection, switchgear, grounding, power quality and renewable integration.

CHAPTER 11 · YEAR 3

Control Systems Engineering

Dynamic-system modelling, block diagrams, feedback, transfer functions, time response, stability, root locus, frequency response, PID control, sensors and actuators.

CHAPTER 12 · YEAR 3

Signals, Systems and Communication Engineering

Continuous and discrete signals, convolution, Fourier analysis, sampling, modulation, noise, filters, transmission media, antennas and digital communication principles.

Year 4 — Integration, Specialisation and Design

CHAPTER 13 · YEAR 4

Microprocessors, Microcontrollers and Embedded Systems

Processor architecture, memory, input/output, interrupts, timers, serial interfaces, embedded programming, real-time constraints, sensors, actuators and hardware-software integration.

CHAPTER 14 · YEAR 4

Power Electronics and Renewable Energy Systems

Controlled rectifiers, DC-DC converters, inverters, switching devices, motor drives, photovoltaic systems, wind-energy conversion, storage interfaces and grid connection.

CHAPTER 15 · YEAR 4

Engineering Design, Reliability, Safety and Professional Practice

Design processes, requirements analysis, simulation, verification, reliability, maintainability, risk assessment, standards, sustainability, project planning and professional responsibility.

CHAPTER 16 · YEAR 4

Research Methods, Integrated Design Project and Industrial Practice

Define an engineering problem, review literature, establish specifications, select methods, build and test a prototype or simulation, analyse results, document limitations and present a final design.

Expected programme learning outcomes

  • Apply mathematics, physics and computational methods to engineering problems.
  • Analyse DC, AC, transient and three-phase electrical circuits.
  • Explain and design basic analogue and digital electronic systems.
  • Use measurement instruments and interpret uncertainty and test results.
  • Analyse electrical machines, power networks and energy conversion systems.
  • Model feedback control and communication systems.
  • Develop embedded-system solutions and integrate hardware with software.
  • Apply safety, standards, sustainability and professional engineering judgement.

Possible career pathways

Depending on accreditation, experience and local registration requirements, graduates may work in electrical power, renewable energy, electronics design, industrial automation, telecommunications, embedded systems, instrumentation, testing and commissioning, maintenance or engineering consulting.

How to use this study guide

Combine mathematical derivations with circuit simulations, laboratory observations and design exercises. Record assumptions, units, uncertainty and safety controls. Verify calculations independently and compare theoretical predictions with measured behaviour. Use the standards and programme handbook prescribed by your institution.

CBE Nexus provides educational guidance. Professional engineering recognition and licensing are determined by relevant accreditation and regulatory bodies.
