Start with the learner's school programme

International schools do not all follow one global syllabus. A school may teach a Cambridge programme, the International Baccalaureate (IB), Pearson Edexcel qualifications, a national curriculum or a combination of pathways. Before selecting notes or past-paper practice, confirm the programme, subject, level, examination board and current syllabus year with the school.

This guide explains the main pathways linked from CBE Nexus. It is a starting point for learners, parents and teachers—not a substitute for the official syllabus or the school's subject choices.

How the pathways differ

The terms below are related, but they are not interchangeable. The school and awarding organisation determine the exact subjects, assessment structure and progression route.

How to choose the right study resource

  1. Check the programme. Ask the school whether the learner follows Cambridge, IB, Pearson Edexcel or a national curriculum.
  2. Confirm the subject and level. Biology, Coordinated Sciences and Combined Science are not automatically equivalent courses.
  3. Match the specification. Check the syllabus or specification code and the year of examination.
  4. Use the right type of practice. Read topic notes first, then attempt worked examples, practical or data questions and timed examination practice.
  5. Check source and status. Use official awarding-body documents for syllabus and assessment rules. Treat independent notes and practice questions as supplementary.

Official curriculum and awarding-body information

  • Cambridge International — programmes, qualifications and syllabuses
  • International Baccalaureate — programme information
  • Pearson Qualifications — specifications and assessment information

Always use the current official document for the learner's examination session. Qualification names, available subjects and assessment arrangements can vary by country and school.

Before you download or begin a revision course

  • Confirm the curriculum and awarding body with the school.
  • Check that the resource matches the learner's current level and subject.
  • Check the syllabus or specification code and its current version.
  • Separate official past papers from independently written practice questions.
  • Use mark schemes and examiner guidance from the relevant awarding body where available.
