What Qualifications Do I Need to Become a Teacher?

Understand common teacher education routes, supervised teaching practice, professional registration, safeguarding and international recognition.

Important: Course availability, admission rules, professional licensing, scholarship benefits and deadlines can change. Verify current requirements with the official provider or regulator before applying or paying.

Requirements depend on the country and school level

There is no single worldwide teaching qualification. Requirements vary by country, age group, subject, school type and whether the role is in a public school, private school, international school or language centre. A qualification accepted in one country may need assessment or additional registration in another. Before enrolling in a teacher-training programme or applying for a job, identify the regulator responsible for the exact role and location.

Complete an approved teacher-education pathway

Common routes include a diploma or degree in education, a bachelor’s degree in a subject followed by an approved postgraduate teacher-education qualification, or another nationally recognised initial teacher education programme. Early-years, primary and secondary teaching may have different requirements. Secondary teachers are often expected to demonstrate strong subject knowledge in the subject they teach. Check that the institution and programme are recognised by the relevant national authority before paying tuition.

Gain supervised classroom practice

Teacher education commonly includes supervised teaching practice or a practicum. This helps future teachers learn lesson planning, classroom management, assessment, inclusive teaching, child development and professional conduct. Keep evidence of the placement, dates, age groups taught, subjects covered and supervisor feedback. Practical experience can be important when a regulator assesses whether an overseas qualification is comparable to local teacher education.

Meet registration, safeguarding and suitability rules

Many jurisdictions require teacher registration, certification, licensing or approval before a person can teach. Authorities may ask for identity documents, academic transcripts, qualification assessments, proof of English or local-language proficiency, police clearance, health checks and evidence of professional standing. Some countries require an examination or continuing professional development. Do not assume that a teaching degree alone gives permission to work; immigration permission and professional approval are separate matters.

Examples of destination-specific checks

In Australia, teacher registration is handled by state and territory authorities; AITSL provides skills assessments for certain migration purposes, which are separate from permission to teach. In the UAE, the government describes minimum degree and licensing requirements for education professionals. In England, overseas applicants must also meet immigration and employer suitability requirements. These examples show why you must check the relevant regulator's current rules rather than rely on a general international checklist.

Prepare a professional evidence file

Keep clear scans of certificates, transcripts, practicum records, employment letters, references, professional registration, police clearance and identity documents. Request official transcripts and certified translations when needed. Make sure names and dates match across documents. Prepare a CV that explains your teaching subjects, grade levels, curriculum experience, assessment skills and professional development. Never alter certificates or exaggerate your experience.

Frequently asked questions

Can I teach with a subject degree only? Some roles may accept a degree plus teacher training, while others require a recognised teaching qualification and licence. Is TEFL the same as a teaching degree? No. TEFL/TESOL certificates are generally designed for teaching English as an additional language and do not automatically qualify someone for all primary or secondary school teaching jobs. Can I use my qualification abroad? Possibly, but the destination regulator must assess recognition and any additional requirements.
