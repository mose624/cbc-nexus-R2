About this physics study hub

Physics develops quantitative models of matter, motion, fields, energy and the structure of the universe. The pathways below progress from applied foundations to mathematical theory, experimental methods and specialist research.

Use the level pathways below to identify the appropriate depth. A diploma module may be practical and introductory; a bachelor's module normally develops theory and independent analysis; postgraduate study expects advanced synthesis, specialist methods and research judgement. These are broad learning guides, not a universal qualification framework.

Diploma Physics pathway

Applied foundations, practical methods and core concepts. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Measurements, units, vectors and uncertainty
  2. Mechanics: motion, forces, work and energy
  3. Properties of matter, fluids and thermal physics
  4. Waves, sound and geometrical optics
  5. Electricity, circuits and magnetism
  6. Introductory electronics and practical instrumentation

Undergraduate Physics pathway

Degree-level theory, analytical methods and disciplinary breadth. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Classical mechanics, oscillations and Lagrangian methods
  2. Electromagnetism and Maxwell's equations
  3. Thermodynamics and statistical physics
  4. Quantum mechanics and atomic physics
  5. Waves, optics and Fourier methods
  6. Solid-state physics and semiconductor principles
  7. Nuclear and particle physics
  8. Mathematical and computational physics

Postgraduate Physics pathway

Advanced specialist study, research methods and critical evaluation. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Advanced quantum theory and quantum information
  2. General relativity, gravitation and cosmology
  3. Condensed matter and many-body physics
  4. Plasma physics and fusion science
  5. Advanced photonics, lasers and spectroscopy
  6. Particle physics, detectors and accelerator methods
  7. Numerical simulation, inverse problems and research design

Core academic and practical skills

Across all levels, develop vector calculus, differential equations, uncertainty analysis, experimental design, dimensional reasoning, numerical modelling and scientific programming. Keep a record of definitions, assumptions, sources, methods and limitations so that your work can be checked and reproduced.

Where this subject is applied

electronics, medical imaging, renewable energy, telecommunications, aerospace, materials science and climate modelling.

Suggested assessment formats

Practice with derivations, multi-step numerical problems, experimental reports, graph analysis, computational investigations and critical literature reviews. Use original practice questions and worked solutions to identify gaps; do not treat these as official institutional examination papers.

Study and research integrity

  • Use current course outlines and primary academic sources.
  • Show working, explain assumptions and cite borrowed ideas or data.
  • For laboratory or field activities, follow institution-approved safety, ethics and supervision requirements.
  • Do not claim professional accreditation or qualification equivalence from these independent learning materials.

CBE Nexus provides independent educational support and is not a university, awarding body or professional regulator.
