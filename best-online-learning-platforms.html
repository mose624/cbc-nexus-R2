What Are the Best Online Learning Platforms?

Compare popular online learning platforms by subject, learning style, cost and certificate options.

The right online learning platform depends on what you want to learn, your budget, the level of support you need and whether you require a recognised qualification or simply practical skills.

Platforms to explore

  • Khan Academy: free school-level lessons and practice in mathematics, science and other subjects.
  • OpenLearn: free courses and learning materials from The Open University.
  • Coursera: university and industry courses, certificates and selected degree pathways.
  • edX: courses from universities and institutions, with different audit and certificate options.
  • FutureLearn: online courses across academic and professional subjects.
  • Alison: free study access to a wide range of career and general learning topics.

How to choose

Check the course syllabus, provider, instructor, assessment, accessibility, language, fees and certificate terms. If you need a qualification for employment or professional registration, confirm acceptance with the relevant employer or regulator before paying.

Match the platform to the learning task

For school subjects and foundational skills, Khan Academy offers structured lessons and practice. For open university materials, MIT OpenCourseWare publishes course content and edX provides courses from universities and organisations. For coding, explore freeCodeCamp and Harvard CS50. For workplace digital skills, compare Google Skillshop, Microsoft Learn, IBM SkillsBuild and HubSpot Academy. For English and teacher development, the British Council offers free resources and online courses.

Compare the real cost

Some platforms offer free lessons but charge for graded work, tutor support or verified certificates. edX audit access generally does not include a certificate. The British Council describes its courses as free with optional paid certificates. Check each course page for current price, access duration and assessment rules before enrolling.

Evaluate quality and accessibility

Review course outcomes, instructor or provider credentials, update date, assessment quality, accessibility, captions, mobile support, language options and bandwidth needs. A platform’s brand alone does not guarantee that every course is current or suitable for your level.

Finish with a practical outcome

Set a weekly study schedule and complete one project, lesson plan, portfolio item or assessment. Record what you can demonstrate, not just the number of courses completed. Keep certificate claims accurate and distinguish completion records from academic credit or professional licensing.
