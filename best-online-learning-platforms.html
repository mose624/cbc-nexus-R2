What Are the Best Online Learning Platforms?

Compare trusted online learning platforms by subject, teaching style, price, accessibility and certificate options so you can choose the right course.

Important: Course availability, admission rules, professional licensing, scholarship benefits and deadlines can change. Verify current requirements with the official provider or regulator before applying or paying.

Start with the skill you want to learn

The best platform is the one that fits your learning goal. Before registering, decide whether you need school support, a university-level subject, a professional skill, language practice, teacher development or a formal qualification. A platform that is excellent for coding may not be the best place to prepare for a national examination. Write down the skill, your current level, the time you can study each week and whether you need a certificate.

Platforms worth comparing

Khan Academy is useful for free school-level lessons and practice in mathematics, science and other subjects. Visit Khan Academy. OpenLearn, from The Open University, offers free short courses across a range of subjects; check each course page for its statement of participation or digital badge terms. Visit OpenLearn. UNESCO Open Learning provides free courses in areas including education, science, culture and media literacy. Visit UNESCO Open Learning. Coursera and edX offer university and industry-partner courses, but free access, graded work and certificates differ by course. Coursera · edX. Alison allows free course study; its certificate and diploma options may involve a purchase, so read the course terms. Visit Alison.

Check the true cost before enrolling

Do not assume that a course labelled free includes a free certificate. On edX, the audit track normally provides free access to course materials but does not include a verified certificate or graded assignments. Alison explains that learning is free while digital or printed certificates may be optional paid products. Coursera course options also vary. Check the enrolment screen for the exact course, your country, certificate fee, access period, assessment rules and refund conditions before you begin. Never enter card details simply to unlock a page that promised a free certificate.

How to judge course quality

Check who created the course, whether the instructor or institution is named, what the syllabus covers, how recently it was updated and whether learners complete meaningful assessments. For professional skills, look for practical projects, case studies or a portfolio task. For academic learning, compare the syllabus with your curriculum or intended university programme. Read the certificate description carefully: a completion certificate is not automatically an accredited qualification, professional licence or university credit.

A simple plan to finish a course

Choose one course rather than enrolling in ten at once. Set two or three weekly study sessions, take notes in your own words and complete every available exercise. At the end, produce evidence of learning: a lesson plan, spreadsheet, project, short essay, classroom resource or solved problem set. Save the course URL, provider, completion date and certificate verification link. Add the strongest evidence to your CV or professional portfolio only when it is relevant to the role you want.

Frequently asked questions

Are online courses recognised by employers? Recognition depends on the provider, course level, assessment and employer. A short course can show initiative and skills, but it does not replace a required degree or licence. Can I learn without paying? Yes. Several platforms provide free learning materials, although certificates and advanced features may cost extra. Which platform is best for teachers? Compare UNESCO Open Learning, OpenLearn and reputable university or education-provider courses, then check whether your employer or regulator recognises the credential.
