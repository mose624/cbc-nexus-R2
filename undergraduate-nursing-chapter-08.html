Principles of adult medical-surgical nursing

Adult health nursing supports people experiencing acute, chronic or complex illness. Care integrates assessment, prevention of complications, symptom management, education, rehabilitation, coordination and respect for patient goals. Nurses consider the person's baseline function, comorbidities, medicines, health literacy, family and social context.

A nursing care plan identifies priority problems or risks, measurable goals, evidence-informed interventions, responsibilities and evaluation criteria. Prioritisation considers immediate threats to life, airway, breathing, circulation, neurological status, severe pain, deterioration and the person's stated priorities. Use local escalation and documentation systems.

Cardiovascular conditions and nursing care

Common cardiovascular conditions include hypertension, coronary artery disease, angina, myocardial infarction, heart failure and dysrhythmias. Mechanisms may involve vascular resistance, atherosclerotic plaque, myocardial oxygen supply-demand imbalance, impaired pump function or abnormal electrical conduction. Symptoms and presentations vary, and some people have atypical or subtle features.

Nursing assessment may include symptom history, vital-sign trends, perfusion, oedema, breathlessness, activity tolerance, weight trends, prescribed investigations and response to treatment. Chest pain, sudden breathlessness, syncope, signs of shock or new neurological deficit require urgent escalation. Medication and fluid plans must follow authorised orders.

Hypertension and vascular health

Hypertension is persistently elevated arterial blood pressure and is associated with increased risk of stroke, coronary disease, heart failure, kidney disease and vascular complications. Diagnosis requires appropriate repeated measurements and clinical assessment, not a single casual reading. Risk is influenced by age, genetics, diet, activity, tobacco, alcohol, body weight, renal disease and other factors.

Nursing support includes accurate measurement, education about prescribed treatment, lifestyle discussion, adherence barriers and monitoring for complications or adverse effects. Avoid giving individual treatment targets from generic notes; thresholds and treatment decisions depend on current guidelines, comorbidities and the treating clinician's plan.

Heart failure and fluid management concepts

Heart failure is a clinical syndrome in which structural or functional cardiac abnormalities impair filling or ejection. It may present with breathlessness, fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, peripheral oedema or pulmonary congestion. Ejection fraction is one part of classification and does not alone describe the person's overall condition.

Observe trends in symptoms, weight, oedema, respiratory status, perfusion, prescribed fluid balance and relevant laboratory results. Support education on the agreed care plan and warning signs. Fluid restriction, diuretics, oxygen and other treatments require individualised authorised instructions; do not independently initiate or adjust them.

Respiratory disorders and assessment

Common respiratory conditions include asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pneumonia, tuberculosis and pulmonary embolism. They affect airflow, gas exchange, pulmonary circulation or respiratory mechanics through different mechanisms. Assessment considers breathlessness, cough, sputum, chest discomfort, respiratory rate, work of breathing, oxygen saturation and relevant history.

Use appropriate infection precautions and follow local oxygen and escalation protocols. Severe respiratory distress, cyanosis, altered consciousness, silent chest in a concerning context or rapidly worsening observations require urgent response. Pulse oximetry must be interpreted alongside clinical appearance and known limitations.

Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

Asthma involves variable respiratory symptoms and variable expiratory airflow limitation, often associated with airway inflammation and hyper-responsiveness. COPD is characterised by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation, commonly associated with exposure to harmful particles or gases. These conditions differ in mechanisms and management, although some symptoms overlap.

Nursing education may include inhaler technique, adherence, trigger awareness, action plans, smoking cessation support and recognition of worsening symptoms. Acute severe attacks are emergencies. Follow the patient's prescribed action plan and local emergency guidance; do not recommend medication changes from study notes.

Pneumonia, tuberculosis and infection-related care

Pneumonia is infection of lung parenchyma and may be caused by bacteria, viruses or fungi. Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and commonly affects the lungs but can involve other organs. Clinical presentation varies with age, immune status and comorbidity.

Nursing responsibilities include assessment, specimen procedures when ordered, respiratory support within protocol, hydration and nutrition support where appropriate, adherence education and infection prevention. Suspected infectious pulmonary disease may require prompt isolation precautions and public-health pathways according to local policy.

Gastrointestinal disorders and nursing care

Gastrointestinal disorders include gastro-oesophageal reflux, peptic ulcer disease, gastroenteritis, inflammatory bowel disease, irritable bowel syndrome, obstruction and gastrointestinal bleeding. Mechanisms include altered motility, mucosal injury, inflammation, infection, immune dysregulation or impaired blood supply.

Assess pain, nausea, vomiting, bowel pattern, hydration, abdominal distension, intake, weight and signs of bleeding. Severe abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, persistent vomiting, haematemesis, melaena, shock features or inability to pass stool and flatus with distension require urgent clinical assessment.

Liver, gallbladder and pancreatic conditions

The liver performs metabolic, synthetic, storage and detoxification functions. Liver disease may impair protein synthesis, bilirubin handling, metabolism and portal circulation. Gallstones can obstruct biliary flow and cause biliary pain or inflammation. Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas and can range from mild to life-threatening.

Monitor for jaundice, altered mental status, bleeding tendency, abdominal pain, fever, fluid accumulation and nutritional problems. Interpretation of liver enzymes, bilirubin, coagulation and pancreatic tests requires clinical context. Escalate severe pain, fever with jaundice, confusion, bleeding or haemodynamic instability.

Renal and urinary disorders

Renal conditions include acute kidney injury, chronic kidney disease, urinary tract infection, glomerular disorders and renal stones. Kidney dysfunction can affect fluid balance, electrolytes, acid-base status, blood pressure, erythropoiesis and medicine clearance. Urinary symptoms should be assessed with age, sex, pregnancy status where relevant and other risk factors in mind.

Nursing monitoring may include urine output, fluid balance when prescribed, blood pressure, oedema, symptoms, laboratory trends and medicine review by the clinical team. Reduced urine output, haematuria, fever with flank pain, acute retention, confusion or signs of sepsis require prompt assessment.

Diabetes and endocrine disorders

Diabetes mellitus is a group of metabolic disorders characterised by hyperglycaemia due to impaired insulin secretion, insulin action or both. Acute complications include hypoglycaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state. Long-term complications can affect eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart and circulation.

Nursing learning includes understanding glucose monitoring, symptom recognition, foot-care education, nutrition support and safe use of prescribed medicines. Hypoglycaemia can cause sweating, tremor, confusion, seizures or unconsciousness. Follow the person's authorised treatment plan and emergency protocol; never improvise insulin doses.

Integrated care planning, rehabilitation and patient education

Integrated adult care coordinates nursing, medical, pharmacy, dietetic, physiotherapy, occupational therapy and social support. Goals should be meaningful, measurable and agreed with the person. Discharge planning begins early and considers medicines reconciliation, equipment, follow-up, warning signs, transport, caregiver capacity and access to services.

Evaluate whether interventions improve symptoms, function, understanding and safety. Use teach-back to check understanding, adapt information to language and health literacy, and include family or carers with the person's permission. Document unresolved risks and ensure a clear handover across care settings.

Integrated clinical-learning case

Scenario: During a supervised placement, a student encounters a patient whose condition or care needs require closer assessment. The patient asks questions and expresses preferences that may affect the proposed plan. The student must listen, gather relevant information, check identity and consent, assess immediate safety, communicate findings and seek supervision.

Discussion prompts: Which information is subjective and which is objective? What additional assessment would be appropriate within the student's competence? Which features require urgent escalation? How can dignity, privacy and shared decision-making be protected? What information should be included in a structured handover?

Learning point: Safe nursing integrates knowledge, observation, communication, ethical conduct and teamwork. Clinical decisions and interventions must follow approved local protocols and qualified supervision.

Examination and revision questions

Answer in complete sentences. For extended responses, define terms, organise the explanation logically, use labelled diagrams where appropriate and connect the science to safe nursing care.

  1. What is the purpose of an adult nursing care plan?

    Show answer guidance

    It organises priority needs, measurable goals, interventions, responsibilities and evaluation for coordinated person-centred care.

  2. Name four common cardiovascular conditions managed in adult nursing.

    Show answer guidance

    Hypertension, coronary artery disease, heart failure and dysrhythmias are examples.

  3. Why must chest pain be assessed urgently?

    Show answer guidance

    It may indicate myocardial ischaemia or another serious condition and requires prompt assessment through local emergency pathways.

  4. List four elements of respiratory assessment.

    Show answer guidance

    Rate, depth, effort, pattern, breathlessness, cough, sputum, chest discomfort and oxygen saturation are examples.

  5. Differentiate asthma from COPD in broad terms.

    Show answer guidance

    Asthma typically involves variable symptoms and variable airflow limitation; COPD involves persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation.

  6. What is pneumonia?

    Show answer guidance

    An infection involving lung parenchyma that may be caused by bacteria, viruses or fungi.

  7. Name three functions of the kidneys relevant to nursing care.

    Show answer guidance

    Fluid and electrolyte regulation, acid-base balance, blood pressure regulation and excretion of waste and medicines.

  8. State two warning signs in a patient with gastrointestinal symptoms.

    Show answer guidance

    Severe abdominal pain, haematemesis, melaena, persistent vomiting, shock features or marked distension require urgent review.

  9. What is a major acute complication of diabetes?

    Show answer guidance

    Hypoglycaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state are important acute complications.

  10. Why are trends in observations important?

    Show answer guidance

    Trends can reveal deterioration or response to treatment earlier than a single isolated measurement.

  11. Give three components of discharge planning.

    Show answer guidance

    Medicines reconciliation, follow-up, equipment, education, transport, home support and clear warning signs.

  12. What is teach-back?

    Show answer guidance

    A method of checking understanding by asking the person to explain the information or plan in their own words.

  13. Why should generic notes not be used to set an individual's blood pressure target?

    Show answer guidance

    Targets depend on current guidelines, comorbidities, risk and the treating clinician's individualised plan.

  14. What is the nurse's role in supporting inhaler use?

    Show answer guidance

    Provide education and check technique using the prescribed device and approved teaching resources.

  15. When should adult health concerns be escalated?

    Show answer guidance

    When symptoms, observations or trends indicate possible deterioration, emergency features or a change from baseline requiring prompt review.
