Academic focus: Reproductive physiology, antenatal care, pregnancy complications, labour and gynaecological disorders. This original teaching unit connects core knowledge with clinical reasoning, assessment practice and professional responsibility.

Core framework

Obstetrics and gynaecology require evidence-based care that respects autonomy, privacy, cultural context and reproductive rights. Pregnancy assessment begins with accurate gestational dating, maternal history, risk assessment and discussion of screening choices. Distinguish screening tests, which estimate risk in an asymptomatic population, from diagnostic tests, which investigate a specific concern. Maternal and fetal findings must be interpreted together, and normal physiological adaptation should not obscure warning signs.

Mechanisms and clinical interpretation

During pregnancy, plasma volume and cardiac output increase, systemic vascular resistance falls, and renal filtration rises; these changes influence interpretation of observations and laboratory values. Placental function involves exchange of gases, nutrients and waste, endocrine signalling and immune interactions. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy include chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, pre-eclampsia and eclampsia; pre-eclampsia is a multisystem disorder associated with new hypertension after 20 weeks and maternal organ dysfunction and/or uteroplacental dysfunction, depending on diagnostic criteria used. Obstetric haemorrhage may occur before, during or after birth; recognition, communication, resuscitation and source control are time-critical. Labour involves coordinated uterine contractions, cervical effacement and dilatation, fetal descent and birth, followed by placental delivery and postpartum uterine contraction. Gynaecological assessment should consider menstrual pattern, pregnancy possibility, infection, structural pathology, endocrine causes, malignancy risk and the patient's priorities. Consent must be ongoing and specific, especially for intimate examinations and procedures.

Integrated clinical case

A pregnant patient in the third trimester presents with severe headache and elevated blood pressure. Explain why this presentation requires urgent obstetric assessment, what maternal and fetal complications are considered, and how the principles of respectful communication and informed consent apply.

Reasoning sequence: identify immediate safety issues; separate reported symptoms from observed findings; form a concise problem representation; explain the mechanism; consider alternatives; identify useful evidence; and state what requires qualified clinical review. A teaching vignette is not a definitive diagnosis or treatment plan.
