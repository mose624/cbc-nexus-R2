Bachelor of Pharmacy

An undergraduate study framework exploring the discovery, formulation, quality, safe use and responsible supply of medicines, combining pharmaceutical sciences with patient-centred care.

Global University Courses / Pharmacy

About the programme

Pharmacy is a health profession and scientific discipline concerned with medicines and their safe, effective and appropriate use. It integrates chemistry, biology, pharmaceutics, pharmacology, microbiology, clinical sciences, therapeutics, public health and professional practice. Students examine how medicines are discovered, designed, manufactured, tested, dispensed and monitored, and how pharmacists support patients and healthcare teams.

This illustrative guide organises core learning into 16 broad chapters across four academic years. Actual programme structures differ by institution and jurisdiction; some universities use a five-year or otherwise extended structure, and clinical placements and professional requirements vary.

Professional and safety notice: This educational outline is not a prescribing or dispensing authority and does not replace an accredited university curriculum, supervised clinical training or national pharmacy regulations. Medication decisions must be made by qualified professionals using current, jurisdiction-specific references and patient information.

Year 1 — Biomedical and Chemical Foundations

CHAPTER 01 · YEAR 1

Foundations of Pharmacy and Professional Practice

Explore the history, scope and responsibilities of pharmacy; medicine-use systems; professional ethics; patient confidentiality; communication; health systems; pharmacy law foundations; and the pharmacist's contribution to safe, equitable healthcare.

CHAPTER 02 · YEAR 1

Human Anatomy, Physiology and Pathophysiology

Study the structure and function of major organ systems, homeostasis, cellular organisation and human development. Introduce mechanisms by which physiological processes become disrupted in disease and explain their relevance to medicines and patient care.

CHAPTER 03 · YEAR 1

General, Organic and Physical Chemistry for Pharmacy

Develop atomic structure, bonding, stoichiometry, equilibria, acids and bases, kinetics, thermodynamics, stereochemistry and organic functional-group chemistry. Apply chemical principles to drug properties, stability, solubility and interactions.

CHAPTER 04 · YEAR 1

Biochemistry, Cell Biology and Pharmaceutical Microbiology

Examine biomolecules, enzymes, metabolism, membranes, genetics, cellular signalling and microorganisms. Introduce microbial growth, pathogenicity, host defence, sterilisation, disinfection and contamination control in medicine production and healthcare.

Year 2 — Core Pharmaceutical Sciences

CHAPTER 05 · YEAR 2

Pharmaceutical Chemistry and Medicinal Chemistry I

Study physicochemical properties of drug molecules, functional groups, acid-base behaviour, partitioning, stereochemistry, structure-activity relationships and introductory drug design. Relate molecular structure to biological activity and formulation considerations.

CHAPTER 06 · YEAR 2

Pharmaceutics I: Dosage Forms and Drug Delivery

Introduce preformulation, powders, granules, tablets, capsules, solutions, suspensions, emulsions, semisolids and modified-release concepts. Examine excipients, manufacturing principles, routes of administration, dissolution and factors affecting drug delivery.

CHAPTER 07 · YEAR 2

Pharmacology I: General and Systemic Pharmacology

Explore pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics, receptors, dose-response relationships, absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and major drug classes. Emphasise mechanisms, therapeutic uses, adverse effects, contraindications and evidence-based interpretation.

CHAPTER 08 · YEAR 2

Pharmacognosy and Natural Products

Study medicinal plants, natural-product chemistry, extraction, isolation, identification, quality control and standardisation. Consider traditional knowledge, evidence evaluation, contamination, adulteration, sustainability and safety of herbal medicines.

Year 3 — Medicines, Therapeutics and Quality

CHAPTER 09 · YEAR 3

Medicinal Chemistry II and Drug Discovery

Examine advanced structure-activity relationships, lead optimisation, prodrugs, drug metabolism, molecular targets, rational design and the drug-development pipeline. Introduce analytical evidence used to characterise compounds and assess pharmaceutical quality.

CHAPTER 10 · YEAR 3

Pharmaceutics II: Biopharmaceutics and Pharmacokinetics

Develop bioavailability, bioequivalence, dissolution, permeability, compartment models, clearance, volume of distribution, half-life, dosing intervals and therapeutic drug monitoring concepts. Practise calculations with explicit units, assumptions and clinically safe interpretation.

CHAPTER 11 · YEAR 3

Pharmacology II: Therapeutic Drug Classes

Study major medicines used in cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrine, gastrointestinal, neurological, infectious and inflammatory conditions. Compare mechanisms, therapeutic goals, safety profiles, interactions and monitoring principles using current authoritative references.

CHAPTER 12 · YEAR 3

Pharmaceutical Analysis, Quality Assurance and Manufacturing

Explore analytical methods, spectroscopy, chromatography, assay validation, impurities, stability testing, good manufacturing practice, quality risk management, packaging, storage and regulatory quality systems.

Year 4 — Clinical Pharmacy and Professional Integration

CHAPTER 13 · YEAR 4

Clinical Pharmacy and Evidence-Based Therapeutics

Develop patient assessment, medication history, treatment goals, medicine optimisation, clinical reasoning, guideline appraisal, shared decision-making and documentation. Use case-based learning to identify medicine-related problems and evaluate therapeutic options.

CHAPTER 14 · YEAR 4

Pharmacy Practice, Communication and Medicines Safety

Study dispensing systems, prescription assessment, counselling, adherence support, medication reconciliation, error prevention, pharmacovigilance, adverse drug reaction reporting and interprofessional communication. Apply professional standards and patient-centred principles.

CHAPTER 15 · YEAR 4

Special Populations, Public Health and Pharmacy Law

Consider medicines use in children, older adults, pregnancy, renal or hepatic impairment and other complex circumstances. Explore vaccination and health promotion roles where permitted, antimicrobial stewardship, controlled medicines, regulation, ethics and equitable access.

CHAPTER 16 · YEAR 4

Research Methods, Clinical Placement and Final-Year Project

Introduce research questions, literature review, study design, biostatistics, ethics approval, data integrity and scientific communication. Integrate supervised practice experiences, reflective learning, case presentations and a research or service-improvement project.

Expected programme learning outcomes

  • Explain chemical, biological and physicochemical principles underlying medicines.
  • Describe drug discovery, formulation, manufacturing, quality assurance and regulatory oversight.
  • Explain pharmacological mechanisms, pharmacokinetics and therapeutic principles.
  • Interpret basic pharmaceutical calculations and analytical results accurately.
  • Recognise medication-related problems, interactions and safety risks using appropriate references.
  • Communicate medicine information clearly and respectfully with patients and healthcare teams.
  • Apply evidence-based reasoning, professional ethics and patient confidentiality.
  • Understand pharmacovigilance, antimicrobial stewardship and public health responsibilities.
  • Conduct supervised research and reflect critically on professional practice.

Potential career pathways

Subject to programme accreditation, supervised training and local registration rules, pharmacy graduates may pursue community pharmacy, hospital pharmacy, clinical pharmacy, pharmaceutical industry, manufacturing, quality assurance, regulatory affairs, pharmacovigilance, medicines information, research, academia, public health and postgraduate specialisation.

How to study pharmacy effectively

Connect molecular chemistry to drug properties, formulation and biological action. Practise calculations carefully and show units at every stage. Use case studies to integrate pharmacology, pathophysiology and patient factors. Learn to evaluate the quality and currency of medicine information, and never treat a classroom example as an individual treatment instruction. Laboratory and clinical activities must follow approved protocols and supervision.
