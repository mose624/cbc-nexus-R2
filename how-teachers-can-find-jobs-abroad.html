International standards: expanded guidance

Define your profile and target role

Record your teaching qualification, professional registration, subject specialism, age groups, years of experience and curricula taught. Decide whether you are seeking a licensed school position, international-school role, language-teaching job, assistant programme or leadership post. Each has different eligibility, salary and visa rules.

Research destination requirements before applying

Compare licensing, credential recognition, language proficiency, police checks and work-permit rules. Teacher regulation may be national, state, provincial or regional. Confirm requirements on the destination regulator’s official site and do not assume a qualification is automatically portable.

Prepare a focused application

Write a concise CV showing dates, school names, subjects, grade levels, curriculum experience and evidence of impact. Prepare a tailored cover letter, degree certificates, transcripts, teaching licence, references and other requested documents. Keep sensitive identity records secure and share them only through verified channels.

Search official school careers pages and reputable recruiters

Use school websites, recognised recruitment platforms and official government programmes. Confirm that each vacancy is current and appears on the employer’s own site. Keep a tracker of the job title, closing date, contact details, application status and follow-up date.

Verify the employer, contract and visa

Check the school’s legal identity, campus, curriculum authorisation and safeguarding policies. Read salary, tax, housing, flights, insurance, leave, working hours, probation, termination and dependant benefits. Confirm who sponsors the work permit. Avoid anyone demanding payment for a guaranteed job or visa.

Additional practical guidance - CBE Nexus

Build a focused international job-search strategy

Start by listing the subjects, grade levels and curricula you are qualified to teach. International schools may recruit teachers for early years, primary, secondary, special education, language learning and specialist subjects. Match your experience to the actual vacancy rather than applying indiscriminately. Search phrases such as “secondary chemistry teacher”, “Cambridge mathematics teacher” or “primary teacher” together with the destination country.

Check teacher registration and immigration rules before applying

Identify the official education regulator and immigration authority for the destination. Confirm whether your qualification must be assessed, whether a local teaching licence is mandatory, whether an English-language test is required and whether the employer can sponsor your work visa. Requirements can differ between public schools, private schools and international schools, and may also vary by state or province. For example, Australia separates teacher registration from migration skills assessment; the UAE has its own professional licensing requirements. Always check the regulator's current instructions for the exact role and location.

Prepare a strong application portfolio

Use a clear, concise CV that highlights teaching qualifications, subject expertise, curriculum experience, classroom results, assessment methods, technology skills and professional development. Add a tailored cover letter explaining why your experience matches the school's needs. Prepare certified copies of qualifications, transcripts, teaching-practice records, references and professional registration where requested. Keep dates and job titles accurate. Do not claim a licence, qualification or experience you do not have.

Search reliable sources and verify recruiters

Prioritise school career pages, official government recruitment portals and recognised international-school recruitment services. Record the employer, source, date posted, deadline and application link. Check the school's legal name, registration status, physical address and official domain independently. Be cautious of recruiters who request payment to secure an interview, job offer or visa. A legitimate employer should provide a written contract and clear details about the recruitment process.

Review the offer before relocating

Compare salary and currency, tax, housing, flights, health insurance, teaching load, working hours, leave, probation, contract duration and termination conditions. Ask who pays for visa processing, medical checks, document attestation and relocation. Confirm the work authorisation and start date before resigning or purchasing non-refundable travel. During interviews, prepare examples of lesson planning, differentiation, safeguarding, assessment and communication with families.

Official checks: AITSL skills assessment process; UAE teacher qualification and licensing information; UK Government guidance for recruiting teachers from overseas.
