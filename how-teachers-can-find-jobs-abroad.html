How Can Teachers Find Jobs Abroad?

Choose destinations where your qualifications and experience may meet the requirements. Research licensing, visa eligibility, subject demand and living costs. Prepare a tailored CV, cover letter, certificates, transcripts and professional references. Search school career pages and established recruitment channels rather than relying on unverified social media adverts.

International standards: expanded guidance

Define your profile and target role

Record your teaching qualification, professional registration, subject specialism, age groups, years of experience and curricula taught. Decide whether you are seeking a licensed school position, international-school role, language-teaching job, assistant programme or leadership post. Each has different eligibility, salary and visa rules.

Research destination requirements before applying

Compare licensing, credential recognition, language proficiency, police checks and work-permit rules. Teacher regulation may be national, state, provincial or regional. Confirm requirements on the destination regulator’s official site and do not assume a qualification is automatically portable.

Prepare a focused application

Write a concise CV showing dates, school names, subjects, grade levels, curriculum experience and evidence of impact. Prepare a tailored cover letter, degree certificates, transcripts, teaching licence, references and other requested documents. Keep sensitive identity records secure and share them only through verified channels.

Search official school careers pages and reputable recruiters

Use school websites, recognised recruitment platforms and official government programmes. Confirm that each vacancy is current and appears on the employer’s own site. Keep a tracker of the job title, closing date, contact details, application status and follow-up date.

Verify the employer, contract and visa

Check the school’s legal identity, campus, curriculum authorisation and safeguarding policies. Read salary, tax, housing, flights, insurance, leave, working hours, probation, termination and dependant benefits. Confirm who sponsors the work permit. Avoid anyone demanding payment for a guaranteed job or visa.
