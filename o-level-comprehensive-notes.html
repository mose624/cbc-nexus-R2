Biology: comprehensive revision notes

1. Core ideas

Biology studies living organisms and the processes that sustain life. Organise revision around cell structure, movement of substances, nutrition, transport, respiration, excretion, coordination, reproduction, inheritance and ecology only where these appear in your syllabus.

2. Structure and key terms

Cells are the basic structural and functional units of living things. A plant cell commonly has a cellulose cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and a large permanent vacuole; chloroplasts occur in photosynthetic tissues. Animal cells lack a cell wall and chloroplasts.

3. Processes and relationships

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration. Osmosis is net water movement through a partially permeable membrane from a more dilute solution to a more concentrated solution. Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient using energy.

4. Applying the concept

Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in organic molecules. The word equation is carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen. Chlorophyll absorbs light; light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature can limit the rate.

5. Exam skills

Respiration releases energy from organic molecules. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases more energy than anaerobic pathways. In humans, gas exchange occurs across thin, moist alveolar surfaces with a large surface area and rich blood supply.

6. Interpreting evidence

Inheritance involves DNA, genes, chromosomes and alleles. A genotype describes alleles; a phenotype is the observable characteristic produced through genotype and environmental influence. Use a genetic cross to show possible allele combinations and probabilities.

7. Revision focus

Ecology examines relationships between organisms and their environment. Energy flows through food chains and food webs; decomposers recycle nutrients. Population size can be affected by food, disease, predation, competition and habitat conditions.

Concept map

Sunlight → producer (plant) → primary consumer → secondary consumer → decomposers → nutrients returned to soil

Biology: topical practice questions

  1. Name two structures found in plant cells but not typical animal cells.

    Show answer and explanation

    Cellulose cell wall and chloroplasts (in photosynthetic cells).

  2. State the function of the nucleus.

    Show answer and explanation

    It contains genetic material and helps control cell activities.

  3. Define diffusion.

    Show answer and explanation

    Net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration.

  4. What makes a membrane partially permeable?

    Show answer and explanation

    It allows some substances to pass through but restricts others.

  5. Explain why alveoli are efficient gas-exchange surfaces.

    Show answer and explanation

    They provide a large surface area, thin moist walls and a good blood supply.

  6. Write the word equation for photosynthesis.

    Show answer and explanation

    Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light and chlorophyll are required).

  7. What is the role of chlorophyll?

    Show answer and explanation

    It absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.

  8. Distinguish genotype from phenotype.

    Show answer and explanation

    Genotype is the allele combination; phenotype is the observable characteristic.

  9. What is a producer in a food chain?

    Show answer and explanation

    An organism, usually a green plant or alga, that makes organic food.

  10. Why are decomposers important?

    Show answer and explanation

    They break down dead material and recycle nutrients.

  11. Give one reason a population may decrease.

    Show answer and explanation

    Examples include disease, predation, competition, food shortage or habitat loss.

Chemistry: comprehensive revision notes

1. Core ideas

Chemistry explains the composition, structure and changes of matter. Classify substances as elements, compounds or mixtures. An element contains one type of atom; a compound contains chemically bonded elements in fixed proportions; mixtures can be separated physically.

2. Structure and key terms

The atom contains a tiny nucleus of protons and neutrons with electrons in shells or energy levels. Atomic number is proton number; mass number is proton plus neutron number. Isotopes have the same proton number but different neutron numbers.

3. Processes and relationships

Ionic bonding involves electron transfer and electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. Covalent bonding involves shared pairs of electrons. Metallic bonding is attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons.

4. Applying the concept

Write chemical formulae using ion charges and balance equations by changing coefficients, never subscripts. The law of conservation of mass means total mass is conserved in a closed system during a chemical reaction.

5. Exam skills

The mole links particle number to measurable mass. Amount in moles = mass in grams ÷ molar mass in grams per mole. In reacting-mass calculations, first balance the equation, convert known quantities to moles, use the mole ratio, then convert to the required unit.

6. Interpreting evidence

Acids produce hydrogen ions in aqueous solution; alkalis are soluble bases that produce hydroxide ions. Neutralisation forms salt and water. Indicators change colour over a pH range; universal indicator estimates pH.

7. Revision focus

Rates of reaction can be increased by higher temperature, greater concentration or pressure for gases, greater surface area of solids, or a catalyst. Explain collision theory in terms of collision frequency and the fraction of particles with sufficient energy.

Reactants → effective particle collisions → bonds break and form → products

Chemistry: topical practice questions

  1. Define an element.

    Show answer and explanation

    A pure substance containing only one type of atom.

  2. What is the atomic number?

    Show answer and explanation

    The number of protons in the nucleus.

  3. How many neutrons are in an atom with mass number 23 and atomic number 11?

    Show answer and explanation

    23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

  4. Describe ionic bonding.

    Show answer and explanation

    Electrons are transferred and oppositely charged ions attract electrostatically.

  5. What is a covalent bond?

    Show answer and explanation

    A shared pair of electrons between atoms.

  6. Why must a chemical equation be balanced?

    Show answer and explanation

    To show conservation of atoms and mass.

  7. Balance: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O.

    Show answer and explanation

    2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.

  8. Calculate moles in 12 g of carbon (Ar = 12).

    Show answer and explanation

    12 ÷ 12 = 1 mol.

  9. What is neutralisation?

    Show answer and explanation

    Reaction of an acid with a base/alkali to form salt and water.

  10. Name one factor that increases reaction rate.

    Show answer and explanation

    Higher temperature, concentration/pressure, surface area or a suitable catalyst.

  11. Explain how a catalyst affects activation energy.

    Show answer and explanation

    It provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy and is not used up overall.

Mathematics: comprehensive revision notes

1. Core ideas

Use place value, factors, multiples, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio and proportion accurately. Convert between forms before comparing quantities. Keep units consistent and estimate answers to detect unreasonable results.

2. Structure and key terms

Algebra uses symbols to represent numbers. Collect like terms, expand brackets using the distributive law, factorise common factors and solve linear equations by applying inverse operations to both sides.

3. Processes and relationships

For a straight line y = mx + c, m is the gradient and c is the vertical intercept. Gradient = change in y ÷ change in x. Parallel lines have equal gradients; perpendicular gradients multiply to −1 when both are defined.

4. Applying the concept

Geometry requires accurate properties and reasons. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°, around a point sum to 360°, and angles in a triangle sum to 180°. Use congruence and similarity conditions where appropriate.

5. Exam skills

For a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c². Trigonometric ratios are sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = opposite/adjacent. Check calculator mode and round only at the end.

6. Interpreting evidence

Area and volume formulae must match the shape. For a prism, volume = cross-sectional area × length. For a cylinder, volume = πr²h. Surface area counts all exposed faces; use square units for area and cubic units for volume.

7. Revision focus

Statistics summarises data. Mean = total ÷ number of values; median is the middle ordered value; mode is most frequent. Probability lies from 0 to 1; for equally likely outcomes, probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total outcomes.

Physics: comprehensive revision notes

1. Core ideas

Physics describes matter, energy and interactions. Record measurements with units and suitable precision. For a measuring instrument, read the scale at eye level and identify the smallest division.

2. Structure and key terms

Speed = distance ÷ time. Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time. On a distance–time graph, gradient represents speed; on a velocity–time graph, gradient represents acceleration and area under the graph represents displacement.

3. Processes and relationships

A resultant force changes motion. Newton's second law is resultant force = mass × acceleration. Weight = mass × gravitational field strength. Distinguish mass, measured in kilograms, from weight, measured in newtons.

4. Applying the concept

Energy is transferred between stores and dissipated to less useful forms, often by heating or sound. Efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input × 100%. Power = energy transferred ÷ time.

5. Exam skills

Pressure = force ÷ area. In a liquid, pressure increases with depth and density. Density = mass ÷ volume. Use SI units consistently and convert cm³ to m³ or g/cm³ to kg/m³ when required.

6. Interpreting evidence

Waves transfer energy without overall transfer of matter. Wave speed = frequency × wavelength. Transverse oscillations are perpendicular to direction of travel; longitudinal oscillations are parallel.

7. Revision focus

In electric circuits, current is rate of flow of charge. Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge. For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, V = IR. In series, current is the same; in parallel, potential difference is the same across branches.

Physics: topical practice questions

  1. Define speed.

    Show answer and explanation

    Distance travelled per unit time.

  2. A runner travels 150 m in 30 s. Calculate average speed.

    Show answer and explanation

    150 ÷ 30 = 5 m/s.

  3. What does the gradient of a distance–time graph represent?

    Show answer and explanation

    Speed.

  4. A 4 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s². Find the resultant force.

    Show answer and explanation

    F = ma = 4 × 3 = 12 N.

  5. Distinguish mass from weight.

    Show answer and explanation

    Mass is amount of matter/inertia in kg; weight is gravitational force in N.

  6. Calculate density if 200 g occupies 50 cm³.

    Show answer and explanation

    200 ÷ 50 = 4 g/cm³.

  7. State the principle of conservation of energy.

    Show answer and explanation

    Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it is transferred or transformed.

  8. A device transfers 600 J in 20 s. Find its power.

    Show answer and explanation

    P = E/t = 600/20 = 30 W.

  9. A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find speed.

    Show answer and explanation

    v = fλ = 5 × 2 = 10 m/s.

  10. State Ohm's law.

    Show answer and explanation

    For a conductor at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to potential difference.

  11. A 6 Ω resistor has 12 V across it. Calculate current.

    Show answer and explanation

    I = V/R = 12/6 = 2 A.

English: comprehensive revision notes

1. Core ideas

Read the task carefully and identify audience, purpose, form and tone. A formal report, letter, article, speech and narrative require different conventions. Plan before writing and keep every paragraph relevant to the task.

2. Structure and key terms

For comprehension, distinguish explicit information from inference. Select concise evidence, explain what it suggests and avoid copying large sections. When asked about language, identify a feature and explain its effect in context.

3. Processes and relationships

A strong paragraph develops one central idea. Use a topic sentence, supporting detail or evidence, explanation and a link to the task. Logical sequencing helps the reader follow an argument.

4. Applying the concept

Argumentative writing presents a clear position, reasons and relevant evidence. Acknowledge a counterargument fairly, respond to it and finish with a conclusion that follows from the discussion.

5. Exam skills

Narrative writing needs a purposeful setting, believable characters, conflict and controlled sequence. Use sensory details selectively; vary sentence length and avoid overloading every sentence with adjectives.

6. Interpreting evidence

Grammar supports clarity. Check subject–verb agreement, tense consistency, pronoun reference, punctuation, sentence boundaries and paragraphing. Use a colon to introduce an explanation or list and a semicolon to link closely related independent clauses.

7. Revision focus

Editing is a separate stage. Read once for meaning and structure, then for sentence accuracy, spelling and punctuation. Replace vague words, remove repetition and check that each sentence contributes to the intended effect.

Plan purpose + audience → organise ideas → draft paragraphs → revise meaning → edit accuracy → final proofread

English: topical practice questions

  1. Identify the audience and purpose in a school newsletter article inviting students to join a reading club.

    Show answer and explanation

    Audience: school community/students; purpose: inform and persuade them to join.

  2. What is an inference?

    Show answer and explanation

    A conclusion drawn from clues or evidence rather than stated directly.

  3. Give one feature of formal register.

    Show answer and explanation

    Standard grammar, respectful tone, precise vocabulary and avoidance of slang.

  4. Write a topic sentence for a paragraph about the benefits of exercise.

    Show answer and explanation

    Example: Regular exercise improves physical health and supports mental wellbeing.

  5. What is a counterargument?

    Show answer and explanation

    A viewpoint that challenges or opposes the main argument.

  6. Why should a writer vary sentence length?

    Show answer and explanation

    To control pace, emphasis and rhythm and maintain reader interest.

  7. Correct the agreement error: 'The list of topics are long.'

    Show answer and explanation

    The list of topics is long.

  8. Name two features of a persuasive speech.

    Show answer and explanation

    Direct address, rhetorical questions, repetition, emotive language, evidence or a clear call to action.

  9. What should a conclusion do in an argumentative essay?

    Show answer and explanation

    Summarise the reasoning and reinforce the position without introducing an unrelated new point.

  10. Explain the effect of a short sentence after a long description.

    Show answer and explanation

    It can create emphasis, contrast, urgency or a sudden pause, depending on context.

  11. Give two checks to make when proofreading.

    Show answer and explanation

    Check spelling and punctuation; also check tense, agreement, sentence boundaries and paragraph order.
