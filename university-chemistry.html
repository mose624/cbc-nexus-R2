About this chemistry study hub

Chemistry explains the composition, structure, properties and transformation of matter. These learning pathways connect foundational chemical principles to laboratory practice, quantitative analysis, materials, energy and current research.

Use the level pathways below to identify the appropriate depth. A diploma module may be practical and introductory; a bachelor's module normally develops theory and independent analysis; postgraduate study expects advanced synthesis, specialist methods and research judgement. These are broad learning guides, not a universal qualification framework.

Diploma Chemistry pathway

Applied foundations, practical methods and core concepts. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Atomic structure, periodicity and chemical bonding
  2. Stoichiometry, solutions and chemical calculations
  3. Acids, bases, buffers and introductory equilibrium
  4. Organic functional groups and reaction principles
  5. Thermochemistry, kinetics and introductory electrochemistry
  6. Laboratory safety, measurement and analytical techniques

Undergraduate Chemistry pathway

Degree-level theory, analytical methods and disciplinary breadth. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Quantum chemistry, molecular structure and spectroscopy
  2. Chemical thermodynamics and phase equilibria
  3. Advanced kinetics and reaction mechanisms
  4. Organic synthesis, stereochemistry and spectroscopy
  5. Inorganic chemistry, coordination compounds and organometallics
  6. Analytical chemistry, chromatography and electrochemical methods
  7. Physical chemistry, statistical mechanics and computational methods

Postgraduate Chemistry pathway

Advanced specialist study, research methods and critical evaluation. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Advanced synthesis, catalysis and reaction design
  2. Materials chemistry, nanochemistry and surface science
  3. Advanced spectroscopy, mass spectrometry and structural analysis
  4. Electrochemical energy storage and conversion
  5. Environmental, atmospheric and sustainable chemistry
  6. Research methods, reproducibility, chemical data and scientific writing

Core academic and practical skills

Across all levels, develop dimensional analysis, uncertainty propagation, safe experimental design, interpretation of spectra and chromatograms, mechanism reasoning and evidence-based reporting. Keep a record of definitions, assumptions, sources, methods and limitations so that your work can be checked and reproduced.

Where this subject is applied

pharmaceutical development, clean energy, water treatment, materials engineering, food analysis and environmental monitoring.

Suggested assessment formats

Practice with balanced chemical equations, calculation problems, reaction-mechanism explanations, laboratory reports, data interpretation and research critiques. Use original practice questions and worked solutions to identify gaps; do not treat these as official institutional examination papers.

Study and research integrity

  • Use current course outlines and primary academic sources.
  • Show working, explain assumptions and cite borrowed ideas or data.
  • For laboratory or field activities, follow institution-approved safety, ethics and supervision requirements.
  • Do not claim professional accreditation or qualification equivalence from these independent learning materials.

CBE Nexus provides independent educational support and is not a university, awarding body or professional regulator.
