WORKED EXAMPLE

Using Ohm’s law

Potential difference = current × resistance: V = I × R.

For a lamp with resistance 6 Ω and current 2 A:

  1. Formula: V = I × R
  2. Substitute: V = 2 × 6
  3. Calculate: V = 12 V

Answer: 12 volts.

Check your understanding

A 4 Ω resistor carries a current of 3 A. What is the potential difference?

Reveal answer and method

V = I × R = 3 × 4 = 12 V.

MEDICINE · ANATOMY

Human cardiovascular system

The heart works as a muscular pump. The right side sends blood to the lungs for gas exchange, while the left side supplies oxygenated blood to the body.

Key pointArteries carry blood away from the heart; veins return blood to the heart.
Quick knowledge check

Question: Which chamber pumps oxygenated blood into the aorta?

Answer: The left ventricle.

CHEMISTRY · ORGANIC

Understanding functional groups

Functional groups are specific arrangements of atoms that give organic compounds characteristic reactions. Common examples include hydroxyl, carbonyl and carboxyl groups.

Key pointAn alcohol contains a hydroxyl group (–OH) bonded to a carbon atom.
Quick knowledge check

Question: Which functional group defines a carboxylic acid?

Answer: The carboxyl group (–COOH).

BIOCHEMISTRY · ENZYMES

How enzymes catalyse reactions

Enzymes are biological catalysts. They bind specific substrates at an active site and provide a pathway with lower activation energy, increasing reaction rate.

Key pointEnzyme activity is affected by temperature, pH and substrate concentration.
Quick knowledge check

Question: What may happen when an enzyme is exposed to very high temperatures?

Answer: Its structure may denature, changing the active site.

NURSING & HEALTH SCIENCES

Pharmacology: dose and response

Pharmacology examines how medicines interact with living systems. Safe practice considers the prescribed dose, route, timing, patient factors and possible adverse effects.

Key pointMedication decisions must follow qualified clinical guidance and the applicable prescription.
Quick knowledge check

Question: Why is the route of administration important?

Answer: It affects how a medicine is delivered, absorbed and acts in the body.
