CBE NEXUS EDUCATION GUIDE · TEACHING TIPS

How Can I Improve My English?

You can improve your English by practising listening, speaking, reading and writing consistently. Set a clear goal, choose material at your level, learn vocabulary in context and ask for feedback. You do not need expensive lessons to begin: free resources and a realistic daily routine can help you make steady progress.

1. Identify your goal and current level

Decide why you need English: school examinations, university, employment, interviews, travel or everyday communication. Your goal determines what to practise. A learner preparing for an examination may need grammar, comprehension and essay writing, while someone preparing for an interview should practise explaining experience and answering questions clearly. Use a reputable placement test, such as the British Council's free level test where available, to choose suitable materials. Set a measurable target, such as reading one article and summarising it or speaking for two minutes on a familiar topic.

2. Improve listening every day

Listen to short podcasts, news reports, interviews, educational videos and graded audio. First listen for the main idea without stopping. Listen again and write down key points. Replay difficult sections and compare what you heard with a transcript if one is provided. Repeat useful sentences aloud to practise pronunciation, rhythm and stress. Choose content you can mostly understand; material that is far too difficult can become discouraging.

3. Practise speaking with confidence

Speak every day, even if you do not have a partner. Describe your day, explain a science concept, retell a story or give a short opinion. Record yourself and listen for unclear words, repeated expressions and incomplete sentences. Practise with a teacher, classmate or language partner and ask for specific feedback. Learn useful phrases for asking questions, giving reasons, agreeing politely and clarifying meaning. Aim for clear communication rather than copying a particular accent.

4. Read and build vocabulary in context

Read graded stories, articles, textbooks and material connected to your interests. Try to understand unfamiliar words from context before checking a dictionary. Record each useful word with its meaning, pronunciation and an example sentence. Learn common word combinations such as “make a decision” and “carry out an experiment”, not just isolated vocabulary. After reading, summarise the main idea in three to five sentences to check comprehension.

5. Strengthen writing and grammar

Write a short paragraph each day: a diary entry, email, report, explanation or opinion. Check that each sentence has a clear subject and verb, tenses are consistent and punctuation makes meaning clear. Use a grammar reference to investigate repeated mistakes, then rewrite the paragraph. Ask a teacher or proficient speaker to review a sample regularly. Do not rely only on automatic correction; understand why a correction is needed so you can use the rule next time.

6. Follow a simple weekly routine

Try a 30-minute routine: ten minutes listening, ten minutes reading and ten minutes speaking or writing. On another day, focus on grammar and vocabulary. Once a week, complete a longer task such as a presentation, reading test or timed writing exercise. Keep examples of your work and compare them after four weeks. Consistency and feedback matter more than occasional long study sessions.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to become fluent? There is no fixed timeline. Progress depends on your starting level, practice time, feedback and exposure. Should I study grammar first? Learn grammar alongside all four language skills so that you can use it in real communication. Where can I practise for free? The British Council LearnEnglish website provides free learning materials and interactive activities for different levels.
