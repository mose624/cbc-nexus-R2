About this history study hub

History examines change and continuity through the critical study of evidence, context, interpretation and historical argument. These pathways encourage comparison across regions and periods rather than treating one national narrative as universal.

Use the level pathways below to identify the appropriate depth. A diploma module may be practical and introductory; a bachelor's module normally develops theory and independent analysis; postgraduate study expects advanced synthesis, specialist methods and research judgement. These are broad learning guides, not a universal qualification framework.

Diploma History pathway

Applied foundations, practical methods and core concepts. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Introduction to historical study and chronology
  2. Ancient and classical societies across world regions
  3. Medieval and early modern transformations
  4. Modern revolutions, industrialisation and social change
  5. African, Asian, European and American historical surveys
  6. Historical sources, evidence and introductory research

Undergraduate History pathway

Degree-level theory, analytical methods and disciplinary breadth. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Historiography, historical theory and methods
  2. African history: states, societies and transformations
  3. World history, migration and global connections
  4. Colonialism, resistance and decolonisation
  5. Political, social, economic and cultural history
  6. War, diplomacy and international relations in historical context
  7. Gender, labour, environment and intellectual history
  8. Archives, oral history and material culture

Postgraduate History pathway

Advanced specialist study, research methods and critical evaluation. Module names, credit values and prerequisites differ by institution; use your programme handbook as the final authority.

  1. Advanced historiography and philosophy of history
  2. Comparative empire, colonialism and postcolonial studies
  3. Transnational and global history
  4. Digital humanities, historical databases and spatial methods
  5. Oral history, memory studies and public history
  6. Advanced archival research and source criticism
  7. Research proposal, thesis design and scholarly publication

Core academic and practical skills

Across all levels, develop source evaluation, contextualisation, chronology, comparative analysis, archival literacy, oral-history ethics and evidence-led academic writing. Keep a record of definitions, assumptions, sources, methods and limitations so that your work can be checked and reproduced.

Where this subject is applied

education, archives, museums, heritage, public policy, journalism, diplomacy and cultural institutions.

Suggested assessment formats

Practice with primary-source analysis, historiographical essays, annotated bibliographies, archival projects, seminar papers and dissertations. Use original practice questions and worked solutions to identify gaps; do not treat these as official institutional examination papers.

Study and research integrity

  • Use current course outlines and primary academic sources.
  • Show working, explain assumptions and cite borrowed ideas or data.
  • For laboratory or field activities, follow institution-approved safety, ethics and supervision requirements.
  • Do not claim professional accreditation or qualification equivalence from these independent learning materials.

CBE Nexus provides independent educational support and is not a university, awarding body or professional regulator.
