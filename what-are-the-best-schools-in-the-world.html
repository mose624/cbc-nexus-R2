What Are the Best Schools in the World?

There is no single school that is best for every learner. Compare curriculum, recognition, teaching quality, class size, safeguarding, learning support, facilities, fees and location. Check whether the school is registered or accredited by the responsible education authority and whether its curriculum matches the learner's future plans.

How to compare schools

Visit the school where possible, request current policies and fee schedules, and ask about student wellbeing and academic support. Check official inspection or accreditation reports and speak with families. Treat rankings as one source of information, not a substitute for checking accreditation and fit.

International standards: expanded guidance

Define the right school for the learner

There is no single global list that identifies the best primary and secondary school for every child. Decide what matters: academic progress, inclusive support, arts, sport, languages, boarding, special education, university preparation or affordability. The right choice depends on the learner’s age, strengths, language, wellbeing and future plans.

Verify registration and curriculum authorisation

Check the school against the local education authority and, where relevant, the curriculum provider’s official directory. For example, use the International Baccalaureate’s Find an IB World School directory to verify IB authorisation. Ask for the accrediting body’s name and current status rather than relying on a logo in a brochure.

Compare teaching and learner support

Review teacher qualifications, class size, curriculum, assessment, safeguarding, counselling, special-needs provision, facilities, extracurricular activities and communication with families. Examination results are only one indicator and may reflect student selection or other contextual factors. Ask how the school supports learners who need additional help.

Calculate total cost and visit where possible

Include tuition, registration, uniforms, meals, transport, devices, boarding, examinations and annual increases. Ask about scholarships and refund terms. Arrange a visit or virtual tour, review child-protection policies and speak with families where possible. A ranking should be read with its methodology and should never replace direct checks.

Official directories: IB World School finder and Council of International Schools. University rankings such as QS and THE do not rank primary and secondary schools.
