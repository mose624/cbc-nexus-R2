How Can Kenyan Teachers Find Legitimate International Teaching Jobs?

A safe step-by-step guide for Kenyan teachers searching for overseas school jobs, checking qualifications, preparing applications and avoiding recruitment scams.

Kenyan teachers interested in working overseas should approach the search as a professional recruitment process. Requirements differ by country, school, curriculum and role. A vacancy that is suitable for one teacher may not be suitable for another, so verify each employer's requirements and never assume that a Kenyan teaching qualification automatically satisfies every destination's licensing rules.

1. Choose target countries and school types

Research countries where your subject and experience may be relevant. Compare public schools, private schools, international schools and language-teaching roles carefully because their hiring standards and employment conditions differ.

2. Check qualifications and registration

Review the destination's official teacher licensing or credential-recognition requirements. Employers may ask for a recognised teaching degree, subject qualification, teaching registration, experience, police clearance and references. Check whether document authentication or credential evaluation is required.

3. Prepare a strong international teacher CV

Use a clear, accurate CV showing subjects, grade levels, curricula taught, qualifications, employment dates and measurable achievements. Include relevant professional development and safeguarding experience. Tailor the CV to each vacancy and do not claim credentials you do not hold.

4. Search through legitimate channels

Start with official school career pages and established recruitment platforms. Check that the school exists, the vacancy is current and the application route matches the employer's own website. Keep a record of the job title, location, contact person and closing date.

5. Evaluate the offer and contract

Read salary, currency, tax treatment, accommodation, medical insurance, flights, leave, working hours, probation, contract duration and visa sponsorship terms. Ask questions in writing before accepting. Consider the cost of living and whether dependants are covered.

6. Protect yourself from recruitment fraud

Be cautious of guaranteed-job promises, pressure to pay personal accounts, requests for sensitive documents before an employer is verified and interviews conducted only through informal messaging. Confirm the recruiter and employer independently. Never travel on the assumption that a work visa will be arranged later.

7. Keep applications organised

Track applications, interviews, references, document requests and follow-ups. If you receive a rejection, use any feedback to improve the next application rather than sending the same generic CV everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Do Kenyan teachers need experience to work abroad?

Many schools request classroom experience, but requirements vary by role and country. Read each vacancy carefully.

Can I apply before getting a teaching licence abroad?

Some employers guide successful candidates through local registration; others require eligibility or registration before hiring. Confirm the rule with the destination authority and employer.

Where can I search for vacancies?

Browse the CBE Nexus international teaching jobs page and verify each opportunity with the hiring school or authorised recruiter.
