Unit guide and mathematical standard

These notes are intended for undergraduate study. Work through the derivations before memorising results. Unless stated otherwise, use SI units, state assumptions, retain units through calculations and round final values to a precision justified by the data. Check limiting cases and dimensions as part of every solution.

Method for numerical problems: identify known and unknown quantities; draw a labelled diagram where useful; select a physical model; state assumptions; derive or rearrange the governing relation; substitute values with units; calculate; then interpret and check the answer.

1. Temperature and thermal equilibrium

Temperature determines the direction of spontaneous thermal energy transfer. The zeroth law states that if A is in thermal equilibrium with C and B is in thermal equilibrium with C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium. Temperature differences drive heat transfer; heat is energy in transit, not a substance stored in a body.

2. Thermal expansion

For a small temperature change, linear expansion is ΔL=αL₀ΔT, where α is the linear expansion coefficient. For isotropic solids, volume expansion coefficient β≈3α. These approximations assume coefficients are nearly constant over the temperature interval.

3. Ideal gas equation and kinetic theory

For n moles, pV=nRT. Microscopically, pV=(1/3)Nm⟨c²⟩, giving average translational kinetic energy per molecule ⟨K⟩=(3/2)k_BT. The root-mean-square speed is c_rms=√(3k_BT/m)=√(3RT/M).

4. Heat capacity and calorimetry

Heat required for a temperature change is Q=mcΔT when specific heat capacity c is constant. During a phase change, Q=mL, where L is specific latent heat. In an insulated calorimeter, heat lost by hot components equals heat gained by cold components and the calorimeter, subject to the chosen sign convention.

5. First law of thermodynamics

Using the convention that W is work done by the system, ΔU=Q−W. For quasi-static pressure-volume work, W=∫p dV. At constant volume W=0, so Q_V=ΔU. For an ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature.

6. Heat transfer

Conduction through a plane slab follows rate Q̇=kA(T₁−T₂)/L. Radiation from an ideal blackbody follows P=σAT⁴; a real surface radiates approximately εσAT⁴. Convection depends on fluid motion and is often modelled by Q̇=hA(T_s−T_f).

7. Worked calculation: thermal expansion

A steel rod of length 2.00 m has α=12×10⁻⁶ K⁻¹ and is heated by 80.0 K. ΔL=αL₀ΔT=(12×10⁻⁶)(2.00)(80.0)=1.92×10⁻³ m=1.92 mm. The final length is approximately 2.00192 m.

8. Worked calculation: ideal gas

A 0.0200 mol sample of ideal gas occupies 5.00×10⁻⁴ m³ at 300 K. p=nRT/V=(0.0200×8.314×300)/(5.00×10⁻⁴)=9.98×10⁴ Pa. The result is approximately atmospheric pressure. Use absolute temperature and SI units.

9. Worked calculation: calorimetry

A 0.20 kg metal sample at 100°C is placed in 0.30 kg water at 20°C in an ideal insulated calorimeter. If the metal specific heat is 450 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ and water is 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, energy balance gives 0.20(450)(100−T)=0.30(4180)(T−20). Thus 9000−90T=1254T−25080; T=34080/1344=25.36°C.

10. Laboratory application: specific heat capacity

Use an electrical heater immersed in a known mass of water or solid. Electrical energy supplied is E=VIt. Correct for heat losses where possible, measure temperature against time, and estimate c=E/(mΔT). A realistic report discusses heater and vessel heat capacity, thermal losses, sensor lag and uncertainty in V, I, t, m and ΔT.

Expanded concept-by-concept explanations and worked examples

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

For nitrogen M=0.0280 kg mol⁻¹ at 300 K, c_rms=√(3RT/M)=517 m s⁻¹.

5. Specific heat capacity and calorimetry

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

A 0.20 kg sample with c=400 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ cools 30 K. Heat released=mcΔT=2400 J.

6. Latent heat and phase change

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

Melting 0.10 kg ice at 0°C requires Q=mL=0.10×334000=33.4 kJ.

7. First law and pressure-volume work

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

A gas expands at 1.0×10⁵ Pa from 0.010 to 0.016 m³. W=pΔV=600 J. If Q=1000 J, ΔU=Q−W=400 J.

8. Molar heat capacities

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

For 2.0 mol monatomic gas heated 10 K at constant volume, Q=n(3R/2)ΔT=249 J.

9. Conduction, convection and radiation

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

For k=0.80 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, A=10 m², ΔT=15 K and L=0.20 m, Q̇=kAΔT/L=600 W.

10. Thermal measurement and uncertainty

Worked example — step-by-step calculation

20.0±0.2°C to 35.0±0.2°C gives ΔT=15.0°C and σΔT=√(0.2²+0.2²)=0.28°C.

Structured revision questions with model answers

  1. State the central physical principle behind the unit and give one condition under which a commonly used model fails.

    Model answer: State the relevant conservation law or governing equation, define its variables and identify assumptions such as negligible friction, small oscillations, ideal-gas behaviour or a uniform field. Explain how relaxing the assumption changes the model.

  2. Starting from a fundamental law, derive one equation used in this unit. Define every symbol and state the boundary or initial conditions.

    Model answer: Begin with the stated law, substitute the constitutive relation, separate variables or integrate as appropriate, apply the initial/boundary conditions, and check dimensions.

  3. A measured quantity is reported with uncertainty. Explain how the uncertainty should be propagated through a calculated result.

    Model answer: For independent uncertainties, use first-order propagation: σ_f²≈Σ(∂f/∂xᵢ)²σᵢ². For products and powers, fractional uncertainties combine in quadrature after multiplying each fractional uncertainty by the absolute power.

  4. Design a laboratory experiment to test one relationship from this unit. Identify apparatus, independent and dependent variables, controls, safety issues and graph expected.

    Model answer: Describe a repeatable method, collect repeated measurements over a useful range, control confounding variables, plot the linearised relationship, obtain the physical parameter from the gradient and report uncertainty and limitations.

  5. Explain how the relevant theory connects to at least one other undergraduate Physics unit.

    Model answer: Identify the shared mathematical structure or conservation principle and explain how the variables or physical interpretation differ in the second context.
