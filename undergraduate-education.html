Bachelor of Education (B.Ed.)

A four-year undergraduate study framework for future teachers, education specialists and learning leaders. Explore educational theory, child and adolescent development, curriculum, effective pedagogy, assessment, inclusion, research and professional teaching practice.

Global University Courses / Bachelor of Education

About the programme

A Bachelor of Education develops the knowledge, professional judgement and practical skills required to support learning across different ages, subjects and educational settings. Students examine how people learn, how curricula are designed, how progress is assessed and how safe, inclusive learning environments are created. The programme combines academic study with lesson planning, microteaching, school observation and supervised practicum.

The framework below is organised into 16 broad chapters across four academic years. Universities may distribute topics differently, require particular subject specialisations or prescribe additional school placements.

Academic and professional guidance: This is an illustrative global course framework, not an official syllabus or guarantee of teacher registration. Check the current programme handbook, accreditation status, subject specialisation, practicum requirements and teacher-registration rules in the country where you intend to study or teach.

Year 1 — Foundations of Education and Learners

CHAPTER 01 · YEAR 1

Foundations, History and Philosophy of Education

Explore the meaning and purposes of education, major philosophical traditions, historical development of schooling, educational aims, citizenship, equity and the relationship between education and society. Compare perennialist, essentialist, progressivist, social reconstructionist and learner-centred perspectives and consider how each influences classroom decisions.

CHAPTER 02 · YEAR 1

Educational Psychology and Human Development I

Study foundational theories of learning and development, including behaviourist, cognitive, constructivist and social learning perspectives. Examine motivation, memory, attention, language development, individual differences and the influence of home, peers and community on learning.

CHAPTER 03 · YEAR 1

Sociology of Education and Comparative Education

Analyse the relationship between schools and society, socialisation, culture, identity, inequality, gender, language, family, social mobility and education policy. Compare education systems internationally while considering context, access, participation and outcomes.

CHAPTER 04 · YEAR 1

Communication, Digital Literacy and Academic Study Skills

Develop academic reading, writing, referencing, oral presentation, collaboration, information literacy and responsible digital communication. Practise evaluating sources, avoiding plagiarism, using learning platforms and communicating professionally with learners, families and colleagues.

Year 2 — Curriculum, Teaching and Assessment

CHAPTER 05 · YEAR 2

Curriculum Theory, Design and Development

Examine curriculum purposes, models, sequencing, scope and progression, curriculum mapping, learning outcomes, competency-based approaches, hidden curriculum, localisation and curriculum evaluation. Learn to align intended outcomes, teaching activities and assessment evidence.

CHAPTER 06 · YEAR 2

Teaching Methods, Pedagogy and Lesson Planning

Develop learner-centred, inquiry-based, cooperative, direct-instruction, problem-based and project-based approaches. Construct measurable objectives, lesson sequences, teaching resources, questioning strategies, differentiation plans and reflective lesson evaluations.

CHAPTER 07 · YEAR 2

Educational Assessment, Measurement and Evaluation

Study formative and summative assessment, diagnostic assessment, validity, reliability, fairness, rubrics, observation, portfolios, feedback, grading and interpretation of learning evidence. Practise designing assessment tasks aligned with intended learning outcomes.

CHAPTER 08 · YEAR 2

Classroom Management, Guidance and Learner Wellbeing

Explore positive behaviour support, classroom routines, relationships, conflict resolution, safeguarding, referral pathways, guidance and counselling boundaries, learner wellbeing and safe learning environments. Consider restorative approaches and culturally responsive classroom practice.

Year 3 — Inclusion, Technology and Professional Practice

CHAPTER 09 · YEAR 3

Inclusive Education and Special Educational Needs

Examine barriers to learning and participation, disability inclusion, universal design for learning, reasonable accommodations, differentiated instruction, assistive technologies and collaboration with families and specialist services. Plan accessible lessons without lowering meaningful learning expectations.

CHAPTER 10 · YEAR 3

Educational Technology and Digital Learning Design

Evaluate learning management systems, multimedia, online and blended learning, digital assessment, accessible content, educational media and responsible use of artificial intelligence. Address privacy, cybersecurity, digital citizenship, academic integrity and the digital divide.

CHAPTER 11 · YEAR 3

Subject Specialisation and Methods of Teaching

Deepen subject knowledge and learn discipline-specific pedagogy. Explore how learners develop concepts, common misconceptions, practical or field-based learning, subject vocabulary, inquiry, demonstrations and assessment within the chosen teaching specialisation.

CHAPTER 12 · YEAR 3

Educational Research Methods and Data Literacy

Introduce research questions, literature reviews, qualitative and quantitative designs, sampling, instruments, ethical approval, data collection, descriptive statistics, thematic analysis and evidence-informed improvement. Develop the ability to interpret research critically.

Year 4 — Leadership, Policy and Teaching Practice

CHAPTER 13 · YEAR 4

Education Policy, Governance and Quality Assurance

Study education systems, governance structures, policy development, school accountability, inspection, quality assurance, financing, education rights and international comparisons. Analyse how policy is translated into institutional and classroom practice.

CHAPTER 14 · YEAR 4

Educational Leadership, Management and Professional Ethics

Examine leadership theories, school improvement, teamwork, staff development, resource management, professional standards, confidentiality, duty of care, teacher conduct and ethical decision-making. Practise responding to realistic professional dilemmas.

CHAPTER 15 · YEAR 4

School-Based Teaching Practicum and Reflective Practice

Apply professional knowledge in supervised school placements. Plan and teach lessons, assess learner progress, manage classrooms, maintain professional records, collaborate with mentors and reflect systematically on evidence from teaching experiences.

CHAPTER 16 · YEAR 4

Education Research Project and Professional Portfolio

Complete an ethically conducted research or school-improvement project. Define a problem, review evidence, select an appropriate method, analyse findings, draw defensible conclusions and present recommendations. Compile a portfolio demonstrating planning, teaching, assessment and professional growth.

Expected programme learning outcomes

Depending on the institution and specialisation, graduates should be able to:

  • Explain major theories of education, learning, development and curriculum.
  • Plan coherent lessons with measurable outcomes and suitable learning activities.
  • Use fair assessment methods and constructive feedback to support progress.
  • Design inclusive learning experiences and identify barriers to participation.
  • Use educational technologies responsibly and evaluate digital resources.
  • Apply safeguarding principles, professional ethics and appropriate referral procedures.
  • Interpret educational research and use evidence to improve teaching.
  • Demonstrate developing competence through supervised teaching practice.

Possible career pathways

Depending on subject qualifications, experience and local registration rules, graduates may pursue primary or secondary teaching, early-years education, learning support, curriculum development, education administration, instructional design, education research, training and development, or education programme coordination.

How to use this study guide

Use each chapter as a study unit: begin with its key concepts, consult the prescribed readings and local curriculum documents, create lesson or assessment examples, and test your understanding with structured questions. During practicum, follow your institution's supervision, safeguarding and assessment requirements. The chapter headings provide a broad framework and do not replace the official handbook of an accredited institution.
