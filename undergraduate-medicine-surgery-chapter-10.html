Core framework and conceptual foundations

Internal medicine requires synthesis of symptoms, examination, investigations and mechanisms. Start with the patient's stability and identify time-critical threats before constructing a differential diagnosis. For each condition, organise knowledge into definition, epidemiology and risk factors, pathophysiology, clinical presentation, differential diagnosis, investigations, complications, management principles, prevention and follow-up. Distinguish screening from diagnostic testing and a risk factor from a confirmed disease.

Mechanisms, structure and clinical interpretation

In hypertension, sustained elevation of arterial pressure reflects interacting renal, vascular, neurohormonal and environmental influences. Assessment includes accurate repeated measurement, cardiovascular risk, possible secondary causes and target-organ effects. Acute coronary syndromes arise from myocardial oxygen supply-demand imbalance, commonly due to coronary atherothrombosis; ECG findings and serial cardiac biomarkers are interpreted with symptoms and timing. Heart failure is a clinical syndrome resulting from structural or functional cardiac abnormality, with congestion and/or reduced perfusion; classify by ejection fraction and phenotype rather than using one mechanism for all patients. Asthma is characterised by variable respiratory symptoms and variable expiratory airflow limitation; COPD involves persistent airflow limitation in an appropriate exposure and clinical context. Pneumonia requires integration of symptoms, signs and imaging where indicated; tuberculosis evaluation depends on symptoms, exposure, imaging and microbiological testing, with infection control and public-health measures. Acute kidney injury is identified by changes in serum creatinine and/or urine output; chronic kidney disease requires persistence and evidence of kidney abnormality, not a single isolated result. Interpret electrolytes and acid-base status alongside volume state, medication exposures and renal function.

Integrated clinical case

A patient with diabetes presents with breathlessness, bilateral ankle swelling and reduced exercise tolerance. Develop a structured differential spanning heart failure, renal disease, anaemia and pulmonary causes. Explain which history, examination and investigations would help distinguish them, and identify red flags requiring urgent escalation.

How to reason through the case: identify the immediate safety issue; separate reported symptoms, observed signs and test results; construct a concise problem representation; explain the relevant mechanism; consider at least two alternatives; identify the evidence that would discriminate between them; and state what requires prompt review by a qualified clinician. Do not infer a definitive diagnosis or treatment plan from a teaching vignette alone.
