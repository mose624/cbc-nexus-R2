How Can I Become an International Teacher?

Understand qualifications, licensing, experience, documents and the application process for teaching overseas.

Becoming an international teacher usually involves meeting the destination country's teacher-registration rules and the hiring school's requirements. There is no single qualification that guarantees eligibility in every country.

1. Complete recognised teacher education

Earn a teaching qualification appropriate to the role, such as a Bachelor of Education or a relevant degree with a recognised teaching qualification. Check whether your subject specialism and teaching level match the vacancy.

2. Check registration and licensing

Some destinations require teacher registration, credential evaluation, police clearance, health checks or proof of English proficiency. For example, review the relevant authority's rules in the country where you intend to work. Do not assume that a licence from one country automatically transfers to another.

3. Build classroom experience

Keep evidence of your teaching experience, subjects taught, age groups, curriculum experience, assessment practice and professional development. International schools may ask for references from school leaders.

4. Prepare an international teacher CV

Use a clear CV with qualifications, teaching experience, measurable achievements, curriculum knowledge, safeguarding awareness and referees. Tailor it to each vacancy and never exaggerate your credentials.

5. Apply through legitimate employers

Use school career pages and established recruitment channels. Verify the school, contract, salary, visa arrangements and any recruitment fees before accepting an offer.
