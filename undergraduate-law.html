Bachelor of Laws (LL.B.)

A four-year undergraduate study framework in legal systems, rights, obligations, justice, dispute resolution, legal reasoning, research and professional responsibility.

Global University Courses / Law

About the programme

A Bachelor of Laws introduces students to the institutions, principles, rules and methods through which societies create, interpret and apply law. Students learn to identify legal issues, locate and interpret authoritative sources, construct reasoned arguments, evaluate evidence and communicate advice clearly. Study normally combines doctrinal subjects with legal research, writing, advocacy, negotiation and practical problem-solving.

This illustrative framework is organised into 16 broad chapters over four academic years. The actual degree length, subject sequence, electives and professional pathway differ between countries and universities.

Jurisdiction warning: Law is country- and territory-specific. This page is an educational overview, not legal advice or an official syllabus. Constitutional provisions, statutes, cases, court structures, terminology and professional admission rules differ. Always use current primary legal sources and the approved curriculum of the relevant institution.

Year 1 — Legal Foundations and Institutions

CHAPTER 01 · YEAR 1

Introduction to Law and Legal Systems

Examine the nature, functions and sources of law; the distinction between public and private law; civil, common, customary, religious and mixed legal traditions; legislation, precedent and delegated lawmaking; legal institutions; and methods of legal reasoning. Compare how different systems identify authoritative legal sources.

CHAPTER 02 · YEAR 1

Legal Methods, Research and Writing

Develop case reading, statutory interpretation, issue identification, rule synthesis, legal citation, research planning and structured legal writing. Practise case briefs, problem questions, memoranda and reasoned arguments, while distinguishing binding authority from persuasive commentary.

CHAPTER 03 · YEAR 1

Constitutional Law and Government

Study constitutionalism, separation of powers, legislative and executive authority, judicial independence, federal and devolved arrangements, constitutional interpretation, amendment procedures and constitutional remedies. Use the constitution and leading decisions of the relevant jurisdiction as primary sources.

CHAPTER 04 · YEAR 1

Law of Contract

Explore agreement, offer and acceptance, consideration or equivalent doctrines, intention, capacity, terms, vitiating factors, performance, breach, remedies and third-party rights. Apply contract principles to commercial and everyday fact patterns while noting jurisdictional differences.

Year 2 — Core Substantive Law

CHAPTER 05 · YEAR 2

Criminal Law

Examine the purposes and principles of criminal liability, actus reus, mens rea, causation, participation, inchoate offences, general defences and selected offences against persons and property. Consider legality, proportionality, culpability and the relationship between criminal law and human rights.

CHAPTER 06 · YEAR 2

Law of Tort and Civil Liability

Study negligence, duty and standard of care, causation, remoteness, occupiers' liability, nuisance, defamation, trespass, vicarious liability and available remedies. Analyse how courts balance compensation, deterrence, responsibility and public policy.

CHAPTER 07 · YEAR 2

Land, Property and Trusts

Introduce ownership, possession, estates and interests in land, registration, transfers, leases, co-ownership, security interests, equitable principles and trusts. Examine how property rights are created, protected, limited and transferred under local law.

CHAPTER 08 · YEAR 2

Administrative Law and Human Rights

Explore public authority powers, legality, procedural fairness, legitimate expectations, reasonableness or proportionality review, access to remedies, fundamental rights and regional or international human-rights instruments. Apply principles to public decision-making and accountability.

Year 3 — Procedure, Evidence and Specialist Areas

CHAPTER 09 · YEAR 3

Evidence Law

Study relevance, admissibility, burdens and standards of proof, witness competence, examination and cross-examination, documentary and electronic evidence, expert opinion, hearsay and exclusionary rules. Recognise that evidentiary rules and exceptions are jurisdiction-dependent.

CHAPTER 10 · YEAR 3

Civil Litigation and Dispute Resolution

Examine jurisdiction, pleadings, limitation periods, interim remedies, disclosure, trial preparation, judgments, costs and enforcement. Compare litigation with negotiation, mediation, arbitration and other appropriate dispute-resolution processes.

CHAPTER 11 · YEAR 3

Criminal Procedure and Justice Administration

Follow the criminal process from investigation and charging through bail, plea, trial, sentencing and appeal. Consider fair-trial rights, disclosure, legal representation, police powers, vulnerable participants and safeguards against wrongful conviction.

CHAPTER 12 · YEAR 3

Commercial, Company and Business Law

Study business organisations, separate legal personality, directors' duties, shareholder rights, corporate governance, insolvency concepts, commercial transactions and selected consumer or competition principles. Use current local legislation and regulatory materials.

Year 4 — Advanced Law and Professional Preparation

CHAPTER 13 · YEAR 4

Public International Law and International Organisations

Explore sources of international law, treaties, custom, state responsibility, jurisdiction, immunities, peaceful dispute settlement, international organisations and the relationship between international and domestic law. Consider the role and limits of international courts and tribunals.

CHAPTER 14 · YEAR 4

Jurisprudence, Legal Theory and Ethics

Compare natural law, positivism, legal realism, interpretivism, critical legal studies and theories of justice. Examine professional independence, conflicts of interest, confidentiality, duties to clients and courts, access to justice and ethical decision-making.

CHAPTER 15 · YEAR 4

Advocacy, Negotiation and Clinical Legal Education

Develop oral submissions, interviewing, client communication, negotiation, mediation, drafting, file management and reflective practice through supervised simulations or clinics. Observe confidentiality, informed consent, competence and institutional supervision requirements.

CHAPTER 16 · YEAR 4

Legal Research Project and Professional Portfolio

Formulate a focused research question, review primary and secondary sources, select doctrinal or empirical methods, address research ethics, analyse legal developments and present defensible conclusions. Compile a portfolio of research, writing and practical skills.

Expected programme learning outcomes

  • Identify legal issues and locate relevant primary and secondary authorities.
  • Interpret legislation and analyse judicial decisions using appropriate legal methods.
  • Construct clear, evidence-based legal arguments and written opinions.
  • Explain core principles in public, private, criminal and international law.
  • Understand procedural fairness, evidence and dispute-resolution pathways.
  • Apply professional ethics, confidentiality and responsible client communication.
  • Conduct independent legal research and communicate findings accurately.
  • Recognise the limits of general legal information and verify jurisdiction-specific rules.

Possible career and further-study pathways

Depending on jurisdiction, additional vocational training, examinations, supervised practice and admission requirements, graduates may pursue pathways in legal practice, public service, compliance, policy, corporate governance, legal research, advocacy organisations, international development or further postgraduate study. An LL.B. alone does not automatically confer a licence to practise.

How to study law effectively

Read cases and legislation in full where required; distinguish facts, issues, holdings and reasoning; practise applying rules to new facts; support propositions with current authority; and state uncertainty where the law is unsettled. Keep track of amendments and appellate decisions. For assessed work, follow the citation style and academic-integrity rules prescribed by your institution.
