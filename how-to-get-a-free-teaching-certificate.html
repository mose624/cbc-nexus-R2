How Can I Get a Free Teaching Certificate?

Some organisations provide free teacher-development courses with a completion certificate or statement of participation. Explore OpenLearn, UNICEF learning resources and selected courses on edX or Coursera. Certificate availability and fees vary by course, so read the terms before enrolling.

Check recognition before relying on it

A course certificate is not automatically a teaching licence, teaching degree or professional registration. If you need it for employment, promotion or licence renewal, ask the employer or regulator whether the course is accepted. Record the provider, course title, date and learning hours accurately on your CV.

International standards: expanded guidance

Identify whether you need professional development or a teaching licence

A course completion certificate can document learning, but it is not automatically a teaching degree, government licence or accredited professional qualification. If you need permission to teach in a regulated school, check the teacher regulator’s approved route. If you want to strengthen practice or document CPD, a short online course may be useful.

Find credible free teacher-development courses

The British Council offers free online courses for teachers and learners, with optional paid certificates on some courses. OpenLearn provides free learning and selected statements of participation or digital badges. UNICEF and other international organisations publish professional learning resources, but certificate availability depends on the course. Confirm terms on the specific provider page.

Check certificate costs and conditions before starting

Confirm whether assessments are free, whether a certificate is included, what completion score is needed, and whether the credential has a public verification link. edX’s free audit track generally does not award a certificate. Alison allows free study and a learner record, while many certificates are optional paid products; selected courses offer free digital certificates.

Choose learning that improves classroom practice

Prioritise formative assessment, inclusive education, safeguarding, literacy and numeracy instruction, digital pedagogy, subject knowledge and responsible AI use. Keep the course description and certificate with your CPD record, and note how you applied the learning in your classroom.

Verify recognition with your employer or regulator

Ask whether the course provider, hours and certificate format meet your employer’s or regulator’s CPD requirements. Do not advertise a completion certificate as “internationally accredited” unless the named accrediting body confirms it.

Official course sources: British Council courses, OpenLearn, Alison certificate guidance.

Distinguish a course certificate from a teaching licence

A certificate of completion confirms that you finished an online course. A recognised teacher-education qualification prepares a person for the profession. A government teaching licence or registration gives permission to teach in a particular jurisdiction. These are not interchangeable. If you need legal permission to teach in a school, ask the relevant regulator which qualifications, supervised practice and registration steps are required before paying for a course.

Find credible free teacher-development courses

Start with organisations that publish their course content, learning outcomes and credential terms clearly. UNESCO Open Learning offers free courses across education and other fields. OpenLearn from The Open University provides free short courses and may offer a statement of participation or digital badge depending on the course. UNICEF and other recognised education organisations also publish training resources. Check each course page; not every course includes a free certificate.

Check whether the certificate itself is free

Some platforms provide course materials at no cost but charge for a verified certificate. edX explains that its free audit track does not include a certificate. Alison allows free course study and provides a learner record, while optional digital or printed certificates may be paid products. A learner record, badge, statement of participation and accredited professional qualification have different meanings. Read the provider's terms before enrolling and check the exact credential you will receive.

Choose training that improves classroom practice

Useful topics include inclusive education, child safeguarding, assessment for learning, classroom management, digital learning, lesson planning, subject pedagogy, special educational needs, literacy, numeracy and education technology. Choose a course that addresses a real development need. Review the instructor's expertise, syllabus, duration, language, assessment method and update date.

Keep evidence and confirm recognition

Save the course title, provider, completion date, certificate or learner-record link and a short description of the skill gained. Apply one strategy in your classroom and reflect on the result. If your employer or regulator requires professional-development hours, ask whether the specific course counts before including it in your record. Be cautious of adverts promising an instant international teaching licence after a short online course unless the regulator confirms the claim.

Official course information: UNESCO Open Learning; OpenLearn; edX audit-track rules; Alison certificate information.
