Bachelor of Public Health

A four-year undergraduate framework for understanding and improving health at population level through prevention, evidence, policy, community action and equitable health systems.

Global University Courses / Public Health

About the programme

Public health focuses on protecting and improving the health of populations rather than treating illness only at the level of an individual patient. It combines epidemiology, statistics, environmental science, social science, health promotion, policy, management and research to understand patterns of health and disease and to design effective prevention programmes. Students learn to interpret population data, identify health inequalities, evaluate interventions and work with communities and health institutions.

This illustrative programme is arranged into 16 broad chapters across four academic years. Universities may vary in course sequence, credit requirements, field placements, specialisations and accreditation.

Scope and practice note: This is an educational course framework, not a clinical qualification or official syllabus. Public health practice, professional registration, fieldwork permissions and data-protection obligations vary by country. Follow current local public health guidance and institutional ethics requirements.

Year 1 — Foundations of Population Health

CHAPTER 01 · YEAR 1

Introduction to Public Health and Health Determinants

Define health, wellbeing, population health and public health practice. Examine social, economic, cultural, biological, behavioural and environmental determinants; health equity; prevention levels; population approaches; and the historical development of public health institutions.

CHAPTER 02 · YEAR 1

Human Biology, Anatomy and Physiology for Public Health

Review major body systems, homeostasis, human development, nutrition, immunity and the biological basis of health and disease. Relate individual biological processes to population-level risks while distinguishing public health education from clinical diagnosis.

CHAPTER 03 · YEAR 1

Introduction to Epidemiology

Study disease occurrence and distribution, incidence, prevalence, mortality, morbidity, risk, rates, ratios, outbreak concepts and descriptive epidemiology. Introduce person-place-time analysis and the interpretation of population health indicators.

CHAPTER 04 · YEAR 1

Biostatistics and Health Data Literacy I

Develop data types, sampling, tables, graphs, measures of central tendency and dispersion, probability foundations, distributions and introductory statistical reasoning. Learn to interpret health datasets carefully and communicate uncertainty.

Year 2 — Disease Prevention and Healthy Environments

CHAPTER 05 · YEAR 2

Infectious Disease Epidemiology and Control

Examine infectious agents, transmission pathways, reservoirs, immunity, surveillance, outbreak investigation, contact patterns, vaccination programmes and prevention strategies. Interpret epidemic curves and assess interventions using current public health evidence.

CHAPTER 06 · YEAR 2

Non-Communicable Diseases and Injury Prevention

Study cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancers, chronic respiratory conditions, mental health burdens and injuries. Analyse behavioural and structural risk factors, screening principles, prevention policies and integrated population strategies.

CHAPTER 07 · YEAR 2

Environmental and Occupational Health

Explore water quality, sanitation, air pollution, waste management, food safety, housing, climate-related health risks and workplace hazards. Introduce exposure assessment, risk communication, occupational safety and environmental health regulation.

CHAPTER 08 · YEAR 2

Health Promotion, Behaviour and Community Engagement

Examine health education, behaviour-change theories, community participation, communication planning, culturally appropriate interventions and the design of health promotion campaigns. Evaluate reach, uptake, equity and sustainability.

Year 3 — Health Systems, Policy and Applied Methods

CHAPTER 09 · YEAR 3

Maternal, Child, Adolescent and Reproductive Health

Study population approaches to maternal and newborn health, child survival, adolescent wellbeing, reproductive health, nutrition, immunisation and prevention of avoidable illness. Consider access, rights, gender and continuity of care.

CHAPTER 10 · YEAR 3

Global Health and Health Inequalities

Analyse global patterns of disease, migration, urbanisation, humanitarian emergencies, health equity, international health cooperation and the influence of trade, conflict and climate. Compare health challenges across settings without assuming one model fits all countries.

CHAPTER 11 · YEAR 3

Health Systems, Policy, Economics and Financing

Examine health-system building blocks, primary health care, governance, service delivery, workforce, medicines, information systems, health financing, universal health coverage, policy cycles and economic evaluation concepts.

CHAPTER 12 · YEAR 3

Biostatistics II and Applied Epidemiological Study Designs

Develop confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, association measures, confounding, bias, cohort and case-control studies, cross-sectional surveys, trials and introductory regression. Emphasise interpretation, assumptions and limits of causal inference.

Year 4 — Leadership, Research and Field Practice

CHAPTER 13 · YEAR 4

Public Health Programme Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation

Conduct needs assessments, stakeholder analysis, logic modelling, objective setting, indicator selection, budgeting, implementation planning and evaluation. Distinguish inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes and impact, and use findings to improve programmes.

CHAPTER 14 · YEAR 4

Public Health Leadership, Ethics, Law and Emergency Preparedness

Study public health governance, ethical principles, privacy, informed consent, health law, risk communication, emergency coordination, disaster preparedness and leadership. Explore proportionality, transparency, community trust and accountability during health emergencies.

CHAPTER 15 · YEAR 4

Public Health Research Methods and Scientific Communication

Develop research questions, literature reviews, qualitative and quantitative designs, sampling, instruments, ethics review, data management, analysis, scientific writing, poster presentation and responsible interpretation of evidence.

CHAPTER 16 · YEAR 4

Community Field Practicum and Final-Year Research Project

Apply learning through supervised community assessment, programme observation or field placement. Identify a defined public health problem, engage stakeholders, collect appropriate data, analyse findings, recommend feasible action and present a documented project.

Expected programme learning outcomes

  • Explain major determinants of health and the principles of population-level prevention.
  • Describe and interpret core epidemiological measures and study designs.
  • Use basic biostatistical methods to summarise and interpret health data.
  • Identify infectious, non-communicable, environmental and occupational health risks.
  • Design culturally appropriate health promotion and community engagement activities.
  • Analyse health systems, policy, equity and financing challenges.
  • Plan and evaluate public health programmes using measurable indicators.
  • Apply research ethics, data protection and responsible scientific communication.
  • Contribute effectively to supervised fieldwork and public health projects.

Possible career pathways

Depending on the degree's accreditation, experience and local requirements, graduates may pursue roles in health promotion, community health programmes, public health project coordination, surveillance support, research assistance, health policy, monitoring and evaluation, environmental health programmes, non-governmental organisations and further postgraduate study. Some regulated roles require additional qualifications or registration.

How to use this study guide

Connect each topic to credible local and international public health evidence. Practise calculating epidemiological measures, interpreting charts, critiquing study designs and building programme logic models. During field activities, respect community participation, informed consent, confidentiality, safety and institutional supervision. Check current guidance from relevant national health authorities and recognised public health organisations.
