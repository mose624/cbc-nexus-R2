Biology revision notes

Study the key ideas, redraw the concept map and then attempt the practice questions. Match every topic to your own subject guide and course level.

1. Cells and membranes

Cells are the basic units of life. Eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Membranes are selectively permeable phospholipid bilayers with proteins. Diffusion, osmosis and active transport explain movement across membranes.

2. Molecular biology

Water is polar and supports biochemical reactions. Carbohydrates provide energy and structure; lipids store energy and form membranes; proteins perform structural, transport and catalytic roles. Enzymes lower activation energy and are affected by temperature, pH and substrate concentration.

3. Genetics

DNA stores hereditary information in nucleotide sequences. Genes code for functional products. Replication copies DNA; transcription produces RNA; translation uses ribosomes to assemble polypeptides. Alleles and gene expression contribute to inherited variation.

4. Ecology and evolution

Energy flows through trophic levels while matter is recycled. Populations interact through competition, predation and mutualism. Natural selection changes allele frequencies when heritable variation affects reproductive success.

5. Human and plant physiology

Transport systems move materials around organisms. Gas exchange depends on diffusion gradients and specialised exchange surfaces. Photosynthesis captures light energy; cellular respiration transfers chemical energy to ATP.

Biology: 11 self-check questions

  1. What is the function of the plasma membrane?
    Reveal answer

    It regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell.

  2. Distinguish diffusion from osmosis.
    Reveal answer

    Diffusion is net particle movement down a concentration gradient; osmosis is water movement across a selectively permeable membrane.

  3. Why do enzymes show substrate specificity?
    Reveal answer

    Their active-site shape and chemical properties complement particular substrates.

  4. What is the role of ATP?
    Reveal answer

    It transfers usable energy for cellular processes.

  5. Name the monomers of proteins.
    Reveal answer

    Amino acids.

  6. What is DNA replication?
    Reveal answer

    The process of copying DNA before cell division.

  7. Define transcription.
    Reveal answer

    Synthesis of RNA using a DNA template.

  8. What is natural selection?
    Reveal answer

    Differential survival and reproduction associated with heritable variation.

  9. Why is biodiversity important?
    Reveal answer

    It supports ecosystem stability, resilience and useful biological resources.

  10. What is a limiting factor in photosynthesis?
    Reveal answer

    A factor such as light, carbon dioxide or temperature that restricts the rate.

  11. State one adaptation of alveoli.
    Reveal answer

    Large surface area, thin moist walls or rich capillary supply.

Chemistry revision notes

Study the key ideas, redraw the concept map and then attempt the practice questions. Match every topic to your own subject guide and course level.

1. Atomic structure and periodicity

Atoms contain protons and neutrons in a nucleus with electrons in quantised energy levels. Proton number defines an element. Electron configuration helps explain periodic trends and chemical reactivity.

2. Bonding and structure

Ionic bonding is electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions; covalent bonding involves shared electron pairs; metallic bonding involves positive ions and delocalised electrons. Structure determines melting point, conductivity and solubility.

3. Stoichiometry

Balanced equations conserve atoms. Convert mass to moles using n=m/M, use coefficients as mole ratios, then convert to the requested quantity. Identify limiting reactants and calculate percentage yield where required.

4. Energetics and kinetics

Enthalpy change describes heat transfer at constant pressure. Bond enthalpies estimate reaction enthalpy. Reaction rate depends on effective collision frequency and activation energy; catalysts provide an alternative pathway.

5. Equilibrium, acids and redox

Dynamic equilibrium occurs in a closed system when forward and reverse rates are equal. Le Chatelier's principle predicts shifts. Acids donate protons; redox involves electron transfer and oxidation-state changes.

Chemistry: 11 self-check questions

  1. What does atomic number represent?
    Reveal answer

    The number of protons in the nucleus.

  2. Define an isotope.
    Reveal answer

    Atoms of the same element with different neutron numbers.

  3. Why do ionic compounds conduct when molten?
    Reveal answer

    Their ions are free to move and carry charge.

  4. What is a mole?
    Reveal answer

    The amount containing Avogadro's number of specified entities.

  5. Calculate moles in 10 g of a substance with molar mass 20 g mol⁻¹.
    Reveal answer

    n=m/M=10/20=0.50 mol.

  6. What is activation energy?
    Reveal answer

    The minimum energy needed for a successful reaction event.

  7. How does a catalyst affect reaction rate?
    Reveal answer

    It provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy.

  8. Define dynamic equilibrium.
    Reveal answer

    Forward and reverse reaction rates are equal in a closed system.

  9. What is oxidation in electron terms?
    Reveal answer

    Loss of electrons.

  10. What is a Brønsted–Lowry acid?
    Reveal answer

    A proton donor.

  11. Why must equations be balanced?
    Reveal answer

    To conserve the number of each type of atom.

Mathematics revision notes

Study the key ideas, redraw the concept map and then attempt the practice questions. Match every topic to your own subject guide and course level.

1. Algebra and functions

Use algebraic manipulation to solve equations and inequalities. Functions map each permitted input to one output. Domain, range, inverse and composite functions describe their behaviour.

2. Sequences and series

Arithmetic sequences have a constant difference; geometric sequences have a constant ratio. Identify the pattern, define the nth term and select the appropriate finite or infinite sum formula.

3. Geometry and trigonometry

Use angle properties, similarity, congruence and coordinate geometry. In right triangles apply sine, cosine and tangent; for general triangles use sine and cosine rules and area formulae when appropriate.

4. Calculus

The derivative represents instantaneous rate of change and gradient. Integration reverses differentiation and can calculate signed area or accumulated change. Interpret stationary points using first or second derivatives.

5. Statistics and probability

Describe distributions using appropriate measures of centre and spread. Probability rules include complements, addition and multiplication. Use conditional probability and statistical models only as prescribed by the selected course.

Physics revision notes

Study the key ideas, redraw the concept map and then attempt the practice questions. Match every topic to your own subject guide and course level.

1. Measurements and mechanics

Use SI units, uncertainty and significant figures. Displacement is a vector; speed is scalar. Interpret motion graphs and apply Newton's laws, momentum conservation and energy methods.

2. Waves and fields

Waves transfer energy. Use v=fλ and distinguish transverse from longitudinal waves. Superposition explains interference and standing waves. Fields describe forces acting at a distance.

3. Thermal physics

Temperature relates to average particle kinetic energy; internal energy includes microscopic kinetic and potential energy. Heating can change temperature or state; apply energy conservation to thermal processes.

4. Electricity and magnetism

Current is charge flow per time; potential difference is energy transferred per charge. Apply V=IR to ohmic components and analyse series and parallel circuits. Magnetic fields exert forces on currents and moving charges.

5. Atomic and nuclear physics

Atomic models explain scattering and spectra. Radioactive decay is random and characterised by half-life. Nuclear reactions involve mass-energy changes and require careful treatment of radiation risk and shielding.

Physics: 11 self-check questions

  1. Define acceleration.
    Reveal answer

    Rate of change of velocity with time.

  2. A car travels 120 m in 6 s. Find average speed.
    Reveal answer

    20 m s⁻¹.

  3. State Newton's second law.
    Reveal answer

    Resultant force equals rate of change of momentum; for constant mass F=ma.

  4. What is momentum?
    Reveal answer

    Mass multiplied by velocity.

  5. State conservation of energy.
    Reveal answer

    Energy is not created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed.

  6. A 12 V supply drives 3 A. Find resistance.
    Reveal answer

    R=V/I=4 Ω.

  7. Define frequency.
    Reveal answer

    Number of complete oscillations per unit time.

  8. A wave has f=4 Hz and λ=3 m. Find speed.
    Reveal answer

    v=fλ=12 m s⁻¹.

  9. What is half-life?
    Reveal answer

    Time for the activity or number of undecayed nuclei in a sample to halve.

  10. Why is a thermistor useful?
    Reveal answer

    Its resistance changes with temperature.

  11. What is the SI unit of power?
    Reveal answer

    Watt (W).

English revision notes

Study the key ideas, redraw the concept map and then attempt the practice questions. Match every topic to your own subject guide and course level.

1. Reading and analysis

Identify audience, purpose, context and genre. Support interpretations with concise textual evidence. Analyse how diction, imagery, structure and tone shape meaning rather than merely naming techniques.

2. Argument and rhetoric

Build a defensible thesis, sequence reasons logically and support claims with relevant evidence. Address counterarguments fairly and use rhetorical choices suited to audience and purpose.

3. Literary analysis

Explore character, setting, conflict, voice, form and structure. Explain how a writer's choices create effects and connect details to a coherent interpretation.

4. Writing craft

Plan for form and audience. Use coherent paragraphs, varied sentence structures, precise vocabulary and controlled register. Edit for clarity, cohesion, grammar and punctuation.

5. Research and academic integrity

Distinguish primary and secondary sources, evaluate reliability and bias, keep accurate notes and cite sources consistently. Paraphrase genuinely and acknowledge borrowed ideas.

English: 11 self-check questions

  1. What is a thesis statement?
    Reveal answer

    A concise central claim that guides an argument or analysis.

  2. What is an inference?
    Reveal answer

    A reasoned conclusion drawn from textual clues.

  3. Why use textual evidence?
    Reveal answer

    It supports and makes an interpretation verifiable.

  4. Define tone.
    Reveal answer

    The writer's or speaker's attitude conveyed through language.

  5. What is a counterargument?
    Reveal answer

    A reasoned opposing position that an argument may address.

  6. Name two features of formal register.
    Reveal answer

    Standard grammar and precise, respectful vocabulary.

  7. What is cohesion?
    Reveal answer

    The way ideas and sentences connect logically.

  8. What is a primary source?
    Reveal answer

    Direct or first-hand evidence from the period or subject studied.

  9. Why cite sources?
    Reveal answer

    To acknowledge ideas and allow readers to trace evidence.

  10. What does an effective conclusion do?
    Reveal answer

    Synthesises the main reasoning and gives the writing a clear ending.

  11. Name two editing checks.
    Reveal answer

    Sentence boundaries and punctuation; also grammar, spelling and consistency.
