Unit guide and mathematical standard

These notes are intended for undergraduate study. Work through the derivations before memorising results. Unless stated otherwise, use SI units, state assumptions, retain units through calculations and round final values to a precision justified by the data. Check limiting cases and dimensions as part of every solution.

Method for numerical problems: identify known and unknown quantities; draw a labelled diagram where useful; select a physical model; state assumptions; derive or rearrange the governing relation; substitute values with units; calculate; then interpret and check the answer.

1. Electric charge and Coulomb's law

Two point charges exert forces along the line joining them. In vacuum, F=(1/4πε₀)|q₁q₂|/r². Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. The principle of superposition means the net force is the vector sum of individual forces.

2. Electric field and Gauss's law

Electric field E=F/q for a positive test charge. A point charge produces E=(1/4πε₀)(q/r²) r̂. Electric flux is Φ_E=∫E·dA. Gauss's law is ∮E·dA=Q_enclosed/ε₀ and is especially useful for spherical, cylindrical and planar symmetry.

3. Electric potential and energy

Potential difference is work per unit charge: ΔV=−∫E·dl. For a point charge V=(1/4πε₀)q/r, taking zero potential at infinity. Potential energy of two point charges is U=(1/4πε₀)q₁q₂/r. The electric field points in the direction of decreasing potential.

4. Capacitance and dielectrics

Capacitance C=Q/V. For parallel plates in vacuum C=ε₀A/d; a dielectric of relative permittivity ε_r gives C=ε_rε₀A/d when it fills the gap. Stored energy U=½CV²=½QV=Q²/(2C). Series capacitors satisfy 1/C_eq=Σ1/C_i; parallel capacitors satisfy C_eq=ΣC_i.

5. Direct-current circuits

Ohm's law V=IR applies to ohmic components at constant physical conditions. Kirchhoff's current law follows charge conservation at a junction; Kirchhoff's voltage law follows energy conservation around a closed loop. Electrical power P=VI=I²R=V²/R.

6. Magnetic force and induction

A charge q moving with velocity v in magnetic field B experiences F=qv×B. A straight wire experiences F=BIL sinθ. Magnetic flux Φ_B=∫B·dA. Faraday's law gives induced emf ℰ=−dΦ_B/dt; the minus sign expresses Lenz's law, opposing the change in flux.

7. Worked calculation: Coulomb force

Two charges +2.0 μC and −3.0 μC are separated by 0.20 m in vacuum. Magnitude F=k|q₁q₂|/r²=(8.99×10⁹)(2.0×10⁻⁶×3.0×10⁻⁶)/(0.20²)=1.35 N. The force is attractive because the charges have opposite signs.

8. Worked calculation: parallel-plate capacitor

A capacitor has plate area 0.020 m² and separation 1.0 mm in air, approximated as vacuum. C=ε₀A/d=(8.854×10⁻¹²)(0.020)/(1.0×10⁻³)=1.77×10⁻¹⁰ F=177 pF. At 12 V, Q=CV=2.13 nC and U=½CV²=12.8 nJ.

9. Worked calculation: series DC circuit

A 12 V source is connected to 4 Ω and 8 Ω resistors in series. R_eq=4+8=12 Ω; I=V/R_eq=12/12=1.00 A. Voltage drops are V₄=IR=4.0 V and V₈=8.0 V; their sum is 12.0 V, consistent with Kirchhoff's voltage law. Total power is VI=12 W.

10. Laboratory application: verify Ohm's law

Connect a resistor, ammeter in series and voltmeter in parallel. Vary the supply voltage within the resistor's safe power rating and record paired V and I readings. Plot V against I; the gradient is R for an ohmic resistor. Use repeated readings, avoid heating, check meter ranges and discuss internal resistance.

Structured revision questions with model answers

  1. State the central physical principle behind the unit and give one condition under which a commonly used model fails.

    Model answer: State the relevant conservation law or governing equation, define its variables and identify assumptions such as negligible friction, small oscillations, ideal-gas behaviour or a uniform field. Explain how relaxing the assumption changes the model.

  2. Starting from a fundamental law, derive one equation used in this unit. Define every symbol and state the boundary or initial conditions.

    Model answer: Begin with the stated law, substitute the constitutive relation, separate variables or integrate as appropriate, apply the initial/boundary conditions, and check dimensions.

  3. A measured quantity is reported with uncertainty. Explain how the uncertainty should be propagated through a calculated result.

    Model answer: For independent uncertainties, use first-order propagation: σ_f²≈Σ(∂f/∂xᵢ)²σᵢ². For products and powers, fractional uncertainties combine in quadrature after multiplying each fractional uncertainty by the absolute power.

  4. Design a laboratory experiment to test one relationship from this unit. Identify apparatus, independent and dependent variables, controls, safety issues and graph expected.

    Model answer: Describe a repeatable method, collect repeated measurements over a useful range, control confounding variables, plot the linearised relationship, obtain the physical parameter from the gradient and report uncertainty and limitations.

  5. Explain how the relevant theory connects to at least one other undergraduate Physics unit.

    Model answer: Identify the shared mathematical structure or conservation principle and explain how the variables or physical interpretation differ in the second context.
