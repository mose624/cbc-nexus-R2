Bachelor of Civil Engineering

An undergraduate study framework for planning, analysing, designing, constructing and maintaining safe, resilient and sustainable infrastructure for communities.

Global University Courses / Civil Engineering

About the programme

Civil engineering applies mathematics, physical sciences, engineering analysis and design to the built and natural environment. It encompasses buildings, bridges, roads, railways, airports, water supply, sanitation, drainage, dams, foundations and other infrastructure. Students learn to understand loads and material behaviour, investigate ground conditions, manage water, plan transport systems, assess construction methods and design infrastructure that meets safety, serviceability, environmental and economic requirements.

This illustrative guide arranges core areas into 16 chapters across four academic years. Actual degree structures, programme duration, design standards, laboratory requirements, industrial placements and accreditation differ among universities and jurisdictions.

Engineering safety notice: This is an educational course framework, not an approved design manual. Real projects require current local building codes, relevant engineering standards, site-specific investigations, independent checking and appropriately licensed professionals. Worked learning examples must not be used as construction instructions.

Year 1 — Mathematical and Scientific Foundations

CHAPTER 01 · YEAR 1

Engineering Mathematics and Computational Methods

Develop algebra, trigonometry, vectors, calculus, differential equations, matrices, numerical methods, statistics and introductory computational modelling. Apply mathematical tools to engineering quantities, physical systems, optimisation and the interpretation of experimental data.

CHAPTER 02 · YEAR 1

Engineering Physics and Mechanics

Study units, measurement, forces, equilibrium, motion, work, energy, momentum, material response, heat and basic fluid behaviour. Introduce free-body diagrams, vector resolution and the physical principles used in engineering analysis.

CHAPTER 03 · YEAR 1

Engineering Graphics, CAD and Technical Communication

Learn engineering drawing conventions, orthographic projection, sections, dimensioning, scales, technical reports and computer-aided design. Introduce digital modelling, drawing interpretation, revision control and clear communication of design intent.

CHAPTER 04 · YEAR 1

Engineering Materials, Chemistry and Geology

Examine cementitious materials, metals, polymers, timber, aggregates and composites; corrosion and durability; basic construction chemistry; mineral and rock properties; geological structures; weathering and the relationship between geology and infrastructure performance.

Year 2 — Core Civil Engineering Analysis

CHAPTER 05 · YEAR 2

Statics, Strength of Materials and Structural Mechanics

Develop equilibrium of structures, support reactions, shear-force and bending-moment concepts, stress and strain, elastic behaviour, torsion, deflection, stability and introductory failure theories. Practise idealisation and transparent assumptions in structural calculations.

CHAPTER 06 · YEAR 2

Surveying, Geomatics and Engineering Measurement

Study levelling, traversing, angles, distances, coordinate systems, setting out, contouring, error analysis, total stations, GNSS and introductory geographic information systems. Consider field notes, measurement uncertainty and responsible handling of spatial data.

CHAPTER 07 · YEAR 2

Fluid Mechanics and Hydraulics

Examine fluid properties, pressure, hydrostatics, continuity, energy and momentum equations, pipe flow, losses, pumps, open-channel flow and hydraulic structures. Relate analytical methods to water distribution, drainage and flood-management problems.

CHAPTER 08 · YEAR 2

Geotechnical Engineering I: Soil Mechanics

Explore soil formation, index properties, classification, phase relationships, compaction, permeability, seepage, effective stress, consolidation and shear strength. Introduce laboratory and field investigation methods and the uncertainty inherent in subsurface conditions.

Year 3 — Infrastructure Design and Systems

CHAPTER 09 · YEAR 3

Structural Analysis and Design of Concrete Structures

Study determinate and indeterminate structural systems, influence concepts, load paths, serviceability and reinforced-concrete behaviour. Introduce design philosophy, durability, detailing and the use of applicable structural codes under qualified supervision.

CHAPTER 10 · YEAR 3

Steel, Timber and Composite Structural Design

Examine structural steel members, connections, buckling, tension and compression behaviour, timber properties, engineered wood and composite action. Compare material selection, fabrication, durability, fire considerations and structural efficiency.

CHAPTER 11 · YEAR 3

Geotechnical Engineering II: Foundations and Earth Structures

Develop bearing capacity, settlement, shallow and deep foundation concepts, retaining structures, slope stability, earth pressure and ground improvement. Interpret site investigation information and understand how ground uncertainty affects design decisions.

CHAPTER 12 · YEAR 3

Transportation and Highway Engineering

Study transport planning, traffic flow, road geometry, pavement materials, pavement design principles, intersections, drainage, road safety, public transport and sustainable mobility. Introduce transport data, network performance and life-cycle considerations.

Year 4 — Water, Construction and Integrated Practice

CHAPTER 13 · YEAR 4

Hydrology, Water Resources and Environmental Engineering

Explore the hydrological cycle, rainfall analysis, runoff, catchments, floods, drought, reservoirs, water treatment, wastewater, sanitation, solid waste and environmental impact assessment. Consider integrated water-resource management and climate resilience.

CHAPTER 14 · YEAR 4

Construction Engineering, Project Planning and Cost Management

Study construction methods, work breakdown structures, scheduling, estimating, procurement, contracts, site organisation, quality assurance, occupational safety, risk management and project controls. Introduce sustainable construction and whole-life cost thinking.

CHAPTER 15 · YEAR 4

Infrastructure Asset Management, Sustainability and Professional Ethics

Examine inspection, maintenance, rehabilitation, resilience, life-cycle assessment, carbon impacts, climate adaptation, inclusive infrastructure, engineering law, professional conduct and public-interest responsibilities.

CHAPTER 16 · YEAR 4

Research Methods, Industrial Attachment and Capstone Design

Develop research questions, literature reviews, data collection, modelling, technical writing and presentation. Integrate multidisciplinary design, site or laboratory evidence, feasibility, risk, sustainability, design review and supervised industrial experience.

Expected programme learning outcomes

  • Apply mathematics, physics and computational tools to civil engineering problems.
  • Interpret engineering drawings, survey measurements and geotechnical information.
  • Explain the mechanical behaviour and durability of common construction materials.
  • Analyse basic structural, soil and fluid systems using appropriate assumptions.
  • Understand design principles for buildings, foundations, transport and water infrastructure.
  • Evaluate construction methods, project schedules, costs, quality and safety risks.
  • Consider environmental effects, climate resilience, accessibility and sustainability.
  • Communicate technical findings and work effectively in multidisciplinary teams.
  • Recognise professional limits and the need for codes, review and licensed engineering judgement.

Potential career pathways

Depending on degree accreditation, supervised experience and local registration requirements, graduates may pursue structural, geotechnical, transportation, water resources, environmental, construction, materials, municipal infrastructure, project engineering, consulting, research or further postgraduate study. Professional titles and independent design responsibilities are regulated differently across countries.

How to study civil engineering effectively

Build strong foundations in calculus, vectors, mechanics and material behaviour. Draw clear free-body diagrams, state assumptions, maintain consistent units and check whether calculated results are physically plausible. Connect classroom models with laboratory testing, field measurements and engineering case studies. Learn to read standards critically and understand that codes are jurisdiction-specific and periodically revised.
