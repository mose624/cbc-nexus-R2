General academic concepts

Evidence

Information used to support, challenge or evaluate a claim.

Hypothesis

Testable proposed explanation or prediction.

Mechanism

Sequence of processes or interactions through which an outcome occurs.

Model

Simplified representation used to describe, explain or predict a system.

Reliability

Consistency of a measurement, procedure or result under comparable conditions.

Validity

Extent to which a method or measure assesses what it is intended to assess.

Variable

Characteristic or quantity that can take different values.

Correlation

Statistical association between variables; it does not alone establish causation.

Research question

Focused, answerable question defining the issue a study will examine.

Operational definition

Precise description of how a concept or variable is measured or identified in a study.

Sampling

Process of selecting units or participants from a population for study.

Population

Complete group of people, objects or events to which a study relates.

Inference

Conclusion drawn from evidence, observations or premises using stated reasoning.

Ethics

Principles guiding responsible conduct, including respect, fairness, integrity and avoidance of harm.

Framework

Organised set of concepts or relationships used to structure analysis or interpretation.

Theory

Coherent, evidence-informed explanation of phenomena that can generate questions and predictions.

Critical evaluation

Judgement of strengths, limitations, assumptions and implications of evidence or an argument.

Sustainability

Meeting present needs while considering long-term environmental, social and economic consequences.

Health, nursing and anatomy

Anatomy

Study of body structures and their relationships.

Physiology

Study of normal functions and processes in living organisms.

Homeostasis

Regulation of internal conditions within ranges compatible with normal function.

Perfusion

Passage of blood through tissue capillaries.

Osmosis

Net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane down its water-potential gradient.

Diffusion

Net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration due to random motion.

Asepsis

Practices intended to prevent contamination by pathogenic microorganisms.

Pathogen

Biological agent capable of causing disease in a host.

Inflammation

Coordinated tissue response to harmful stimuli involving vascular and cellular changes.

Pharmacokinetics

How the body absorbs, distributes, metabolises and eliminates a drug.

Pharmacodynamics

Biochemical and physiological effects of drugs and their mechanisms of action.

Incidence

Number of new cases in a population during a specified period.

Prevalence

Number or proportion of people in a population who have a condition at a specified time or period.

Sensitivity

Proportion of people with a condition who test positive.

Specificity

Proportion of people without a condition who test negative.

Chemistry

Mole

SI unit for amount of substance; contains exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ specified entities.

Molarity

Amount concentration in moles of solute per litre of solution.

Oxidation

Loss of electrons, with an increase in oxidation state.

Reduction

Gain of electrons, with a decrease in oxidation state.

Catalyst

Substance that increases reaction rate by providing an alternative pathway and is regenerated overall.

Equilibrium

Dynamic state where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.

Enthalpy

Thermodynamic state function H = U + pV.

pH

Measure related to hydrogen-ion activity; often approximated as −log₁₀[H⁺] in dilute solutions.

Physics

Force

Interaction that can change motion; in Newtonian mechanics F = ma for constant mass.

Work

Energy transferred when a force causes displacement; W = F·s for constant force.

Power

Rate of energy transfer or work done.

Momentum

Product of mass and velocity; a vector quantity.

Current

Rate of flow of electric charge.

Resistance

Opposition to electric current; R = V/I for an ohmic conductor under specified conditions.

Computing

Algorithm

Finite, well-defined sequence of steps for solving a problem or performing a computation.

Data structure

Method of organising and storing data to support efficient access and modification.

Database

Organised collection of data managed for retrieval, update and controlled use.

Complexity

Computational resources, often time or memory, required as input size grows.

Accounting and finance

Liquidity

Ability to meet short-term obligations or convert an asset to cash with limited delay or loss.

Asset

Resource controlled by an entity from which future economic benefits are expected under the applicable framework.

Liability

Present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events.

Depreciation

Systematic allocation of an asset's depreciable amount over its useful life.

Materiality

Significance of information such that omission or misstatement could influence users' decisions.

Architecture

Design brief

Statement of a design problem, requirements, constraints and intended outcomes.

Load path

Route by which structural loads transfer through connected elements to foundations and ground.

Building envelope

Physical boundary separating interior and exterior environments, including walls, roofs and windows.

Education

Curriculum

Planned learning content, experiences, outcomes and assessment within an educational programme.

Pedagogy

Theory and practice of teaching, including how learning is facilitated and assessed.

Formative assessment

Assessment during learning that provides feedback to inform teaching and learner improvement.

Summative assessment

Assessment used to evaluate learning at the end of a defined period or unit.

Psychology

Cognition

Mental processes involved in acquiring, processing, storing and using information.

Reinforcement

Consequence that increases the likelihood of a behaviour occurring again in a given context.

Law

Contract

Agreement that creates legally enforceable obligations when applicable legal requirements are met.

Jurisdiction

Legal authority of a court or body over persons, territory or subject matter.

Precedent

Previous judicial decision that may guide or bind later decisions under the applicable legal system.

Business and management

Governance

Systems by which organisations are directed, controlled and held accountable.

Strategy

Coordinated set of choices and actions intended to achieve longer-term objectives.

Engineering

Project scope

Work and deliverables required to produce a project's agreed outcomes.

Tolerance

Permitted variation in a specified dimension, property or process parameter.

Using this reference responsibly

  • Definitions are concise study aids, not replacements for full textbook explanations.
  • For clinical, legal, engineering and financial practice, check current local legislation, standards, regulator guidance and institutional procedures.
  • For calculations, identify every symbol, convert units consistently and state assumptions.
  • Definitions are reviewed as part of the wider CBE Nexus undergraduate content quality programme.
