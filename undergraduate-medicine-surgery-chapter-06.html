Chapter overview and learning outcomes

This chapter develops an integrated understanding of medical microbiology, immunology and parasitology. Learners should explain concepts accurately, connect mechanisms to other medical disciplines, interpret evidence critically and recognise the limits of simplified models. Patient-related activities require appropriate consent, qualified supervision and adherence to institutional and local standards.

  • Define principal terms and classifications.
  • Explain important structures, mechanisms and relationships.
  • Integrate knowledge across biomedical and clinical disciplines.
  • Analyse educational cases while acknowledging uncertainty.
  • Apply professional, ethical and patient-safety principles.

1. Foundations of medical microbiology and microbial classification

Microorganisms are classified by cellular organisation, morphology, genetic material, metabolism and evolutionary relationships. Classification supports identification and understanding of transmission, but clinical interpretation requires validated laboratory methods and context.
