Unit guide and mathematical standard

These notes are intended for undergraduate study. Work through the derivations before memorising results. Unless stated otherwise, use SI units, state assumptions, retain units through calculations and round final values to a precision justified by the data. Check limiting cases and dimensions as part of every solution.

Method for numerical problems: identify known and unknown quantities; draw a labelled diagram where useful; select a physical model; state assumptions; derive or rearrange the governing relation; substitute values with units; calculate; then interpret and check the answer.

1. Kinematics in one dimension

For constant acceleration, v=u+at, s=ut+½at² and v²=u²+2as. These equations follow from a=dv/dt and v=ds/dt when a is constant. Choose a positive direction before substituting signed quantities; displacement is not the same as distance.

2. Projectile motion

Neglecting air resistance, horizontal acceleration is zero and vertical acceleration is −g. For launch speed u at angle θ: x=u cosθ·t and y=u sinθ·t−½gt². On level ground, flight time T=2u sinθ/g, range R=u²sin2θ/g and maximum height H=u²sin²θ/(2g).

3. Newton's laws and free-body diagrams

The second law is ΣF=ma in an inertial frame. Draw each force acting on the chosen body: weight mg, normal reaction, tension, friction and applied forces. Resolve forces along convenient axes. Static friction adjusts up to fₛ≤μₛN; kinetic friction is approximately fₖ=μₖN.

4. Work, energy and power

For constant force, W=F·s=Fs cosθ. For variable force, W=∫F·ds. The work-energy theorem states W_net=ΔK, where K=½mv². Near Earth's surface gravitational potential energy is U=mgh. Power P=dW/dt=F·v.

5. Momentum and collisions

Linear momentum p=mv. Impulse J=∫Fdt=Δp. For an isolated system, total momentum is conserved. In a perfectly elastic collision, both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved; in an inelastic collision momentum remains conserved but kinetic energy does not.

6. Circular and rotational motion

Angular speed ω=dθ/dt, tangential speed v=rω and centripetal acceleration a_c=v²/r=rω². Torque τ=r×F. Rotational dynamics about a fixed axis follows Στ=Iα, where I=∫r²dm. Rotational kinetic energy is ½Iω².

7. Worked calculation: projectile

A ball is projected at 20.0 m s⁻¹ at 30.0° above level ground. Taking g=9.81 m s⁻², T=2u sinθ/g=2(20)(0.5)/9.81=2.04 s. Range R=u²sin60°/g=400(0.8660)/9.81=35.3 m. Maximum height H=u²sin²30°/(2g)=400(0.25)/19.62=5.10 m. Air resistance is neglected.

8. Worked calculation: friction on an incline

A 5.0 kg block slides down a 30° incline with μₖ=0.20. Along the slope, mg sinθ−fₖ=ma and fₖ=μₖmg cosθ. Therefore a=g(sinθ−μₖcosθ)=9.81(0.500−0.20×0.866)=3.21 m s⁻² down the slope. The mass cancels, as expected for this ideal model.

9. Worked calculation: energy and spring

A 0.50 kg mass moving at 4.0 m s⁻¹ compresses an ideal spring of constant 200 N m⁻¹ on a frictionless surface. Conservation of energy gives ½mv²=½kx². Hence x=v√(m/k)=4.0√(0.50/200)=0.200 m. This assumes all initial kinetic energy becomes spring potential energy.

10. Laboratory application: determine g

Use a ticker timer or photogate to measure displacement and time for a falling object. From s=½gt² for release from rest, plot s against t²; gradient is g/2. Avoid large aerodynamic drag, measure the release point consistently and propagate timing and distance uncertainties.

Structured revision questions with model answers

  1. State the central physical principle behind the unit and give one condition under which a commonly used model fails.

    Model answer: State the relevant conservation law or governing equation, define its variables and identify assumptions such as negligible friction, small oscillations, ideal-gas behaviour or a uniform field. Explain how relaxing the assumption changes the model.

  2. Starting from a fundamental law, derive one equation used in this unit. Define every symbol and state the boundary or initial conditions.

    Model answer: Begin with the stated law, substitute the constitutive relation, separate variables or integrate as appropriate, apply the initial/boundary conditions, and check dimensions.

  3. A measured quantity is reported with uncertainty. Explain how the uncertainty should be propagated through a calculated result.

    Model answer: For independent uncertainties, use first-order propagation: σ_f²≈Σ(∂f/∂xᵢ)²σᵢ². For products and powers, fractional uncertainties combine in quadrature after multiplying each fractional uncertainty by the absolute power.

  4. Design a laboratory experiment to test one relationship from this unit. Identify apparatus, independent and dependent variables, controls, safety issues and graph expected.

    Model answer: Describe a repeatable method, collect repeated measurements over a useful range, control confounding variables, plot the linearised relationship, obtain the physical parameter from the gradient and report uncertainty and limitations.

  5. Explain how the relevant theory connects to at least one other undergraduate Physics unit.

    Model answer: Identify the shared mathematical structure or conservation principle and explain how the variables or physical interpretation differ in the second context.
