CBE NEXUS • GLOBAL MATHEMATICS

Applied and Specialist Mathematics

Two progressive pathways covering engineering, finance, actuarial science, economics, computing, data science, mathematical physics, control systems, decision science and environmental or biological modelling.

How the two levels differ

Undergraduate: develops mathematical foundations, standard analytical and computational techniques, model formulation, interpretation and applied problem solving.

Advanced postgraduate: develops deeper theory, proof-based reasoning, stochastic and optimisation methods, advanced computation, research literature skills and independent model evaluation.

These are broad international topic frameworks, not a claim that every university teaches every topic in this exact order. Institutions differ in prerequisites, course titles, depth, software and assessment. Match formal study to the current programme handbook and module descriptors.

Part C — Undergraduate applied mathematics problem bank

These are original, calculation-led university practice problems. Show equations, units, assumptions and interpretation. Unless a question states otherwise, give exact values before rounding and justify every modelling choice.

Engineering, calculus and linear algebra

  1. Dimensional analysis (10 marks). A pendulum period is modelled as T = kLagb, where L is length and g is gravitational acceleration. Use dimensions to determine a and b, and state the dimensions of k. Explain what dimensional analysis cannot determine about k.
  2. Engineering optimisation (10 marks). An open-top rectangular tank must hold 12 m3. Its base has length twice its width. Express the surface area in terms of the width w, differentiate, and determine the dimensions that minimise material area. Verify that the stationary point is a minimum.
  3. Linear systems and least squares (10 marks). Solve the overdetermined system represented by A = [[1,0],[1,1],[1,2]] and b = [1,2,2]T in the least-squares sense. Calculate the residual vector and explain its orthogonality to the column space of A.
  4. Eigenvalue stability (10 marks). For x′ = Ax with A = [[-2,1],[0,-3]], find the eigenvalues and general solution. Classify the equilibrium at the origin and explain the long-term behaviour of each component.
  5. Forced differential equation (10 marks). A damped oscillator satisfies y″ + 4y′ + 13y = 10cos(2t), with y(0)=0 and y′(0)=0. Find the complementary solution, construct a steady-state particular solution, and use the initial conditions to determine the constants.
  6. Numerical root finding (8 marks). Apply Newton's method to f(x)=x3−2x−5, starting at x0=2. Perform three iterations, tabulate xn and f(xn), and estimate the absolute error using the last two iterates.

Statistics, optimisation and finance

  1. Regression and inference (10 marks). For observations (1,2), (2,3), (3,5), (4,4), fit y = a + bx by least squares. Calculate a, b and R2; interpret the slope and discuss why a high R2 does not establish causation.
  2. Linear programming (10 marks). Maximise P = 5x + 4y subject to 2x+y≤8, x+2y≤8, x≥0, y≥0. Find all feasible vertices, evaluate P at each, identify the optimum and state which constraints are binding.
  3. Compound interest and amortisation (8 marks). A loan of KSh 480,000 is repaid by equal monthly payments over 36 months at a nominal annual rate of 12% compounded monthly. Derive the monthly payment formula, calculate the payment, and determine the total interest paid.
  4. Portfolio risk (8 marks). Two assets have expected returns 8% and 13%, standard deviations 10% and 20%, and correlation 0.25. For a portfolio investing 60% in asset 1 and 40% in asset 2, calculate expected return and standard deviation. Explain the contribution of covariance to portfolio risk.

Computing, networks and mathematical modelling

  1. Algorithmic complexity (8 marks). A nested algorithm performs, for each i=1,…,n, an inner loop of j=1,…,i. Derive the exact number of inner-loop executions and its asymptotic order. Compare it with an algorithm performing n log2n operations.
  2. Graph theory and shortest paths (8 marks). A directed network has edges A→B (4), A→C (2), C→B (1), B→D (5), C→D (8), and D→E (2). Use Dijkstra's algorithm from A to find the shortest path and distance to E. Show the settled-node order.
  3. Modular arithmetic (8 marks). Solve 7x ≡ 5 (mod 26). Find the multiplicative inverse used, give the least non-negative solution and verify it by substitution.
  4. Population dynamics (10 marks). A population follows dP/dt = 0.4P(1−P/1000). Solve the logistic equation for P(0)=100, determine the time at which P reaches 500, and interpret the carrying capacity.
  5. Compartment model (10 marks). A two-compartment tracer model is x′=−3x+y, y′=2x−2y, with x(0)=1 and y(0)=0. Find the eigenvalues, determine whether total tracer x+y is conserved, and explain the physical meaning of the result.

Part D — Advanced postgraduate applied and specialist problem bank

These problems require proof, derivation, computational design or critical model evaluation at Master's/doctoral level. State regularity and feasibility assumptions explicitly; numerical evidence alone is not a proof.

Advanced analysis, computation and optimisation

  1. Perturbation analysis (12 marks). For εy″+y′+y=0 on 0≤t≤1 with y(0)=1 and y′(0)=0, derive the outer expansion through O(ε). Identify the boundary layer needed to satisfy the derivative condition and construct a uniformly valid first-order approximation.
  2. Conditioning and SVD (12 marks). Let A = [[1,1],[1,1+δ]], δ>0. Derive det(A), explain why the system becomes ill-conditioned as δ→0, and obtain the leading-order behaviour of the smallest singular value. Discuss the effect on least-squares parameter uncertainty.
  3. Finite-difference stability (12 marks). Apply forward Euler in time and centred differences in space to ut=κuxx. Use a Fourier-mode analysis to derive the stability restriction on r=κΔt/(Δx)2. Explain why consistency alone is insufficient for convergence.
  4. Constrained optimisation (12 marks). Minimise f(x,y)=x2+2y2 subject to x+y=3 and x≥0,y≥0. Derive the KKT conditions, solve the problem, and verify constraint qualification and complementary slackness.
  5. Integer programming (12 marks). A facility may be opened at either site A or B, with binary variables zA,zB. Opening costs are 40 and 30; each unit of demand costs 5 from A and 8 from B. Formulate a mixed-integer model for demand 10 with exactly one facility open, capacity 12 at either site, and prove which facility is optimal.

Stochastic systems, finance and actuarial science

  1. Markov decision process (12 marks). A two-state discounted decision process has actions with specified one-step costs c(s,a) and transition matrix Pa. Write the Bellman optimality equations for discount factor β∈(0,1), state conditions for a contraction, and outline a policy-iteration algorithm. Explain how you would verify the computed policy.
  2. Option pricing (12 marks). In a one-period binomial model, S0=100, u=1.2, d=0.9, strike K=105 and risk-free gross return R=1.05. Compute the risk-neutral probability, call payoff in each state and no-arbitrage call value. Check the no-arbitrage condition d<R<u.
  3. Ruin probability (12 marks). In a classical risk model with initial reserve u, premium rate c and claim process N(t), derive the surplus process. State the net profit condition and formulate the integro-differential equation for ultimate ruin probability under an explicitly stated claim-size distribution.
  4. Bayesian experimental design (12 marks). Let observations satisfy Y|θ ~ Normal(θ,σ²) with known σ² and prior θ~Normal(μ₀,τ₀²). Derive the posterior distribution after n independent observations and determine how posterior variance changes with n. Propose a design criterion for selecting the next observation precision.
  5. Time-series econometrics (12 marks). For yt=φyt−1+εt, with white-noise innovations of variance σ², derive the stationarity condition, unconditional variance and autocorrelation function. Explain the consequences of fitting this stationary model when |φ|≥1.

Control, networks and scientific modelling

  1. Lyapunov stability (12 marks). For x′=Ax with A=[[-1,2],[-3,-4]], determine whether A is Hurwitz. Solve ATP+PA=−I for symmetric P and use V(x)=xTPx to establish asymptotic stability.
  2. Game theory (10 marks). Two firms choose high or low output. Their payoff matrix is [[(8,8),(2,10)],[(10,2),(4,4)]], with rows and columns ordered High, Low. Identify best responses, pure Nash equilibria and whether either firm has a dominant strategy.
  3. Reaction-diffusion stability (12 marks). For ut=D uxx+f(u), linearise about a spatially uniform steady state u*. Derive the growth rate of a Fourier perturbation and state the condition under which diffusion stabilises or destabilises a mode.
  4. Inverse problem and identifiability (12 marks). Observations satisfy y(t)=a exp(−kt)+b. Determine which parameters are structurally identifiable from ideal continuous noiseless data, identify degenerate cases, and propose a sensitivity-based experiment to distinguish a from b.
  5. Uncertainty quantification (12 marks). A model output is Q=g(X₁,X₂) with correlated inputs. Derive a first-order variance approximation using the gradient and covariance matrix. Explain when a Monte Carlo estimate is preferable and design a convergence diagnostic.

Study and assessment expectations

  • State assumptions, define variables and use consistent units.
  • Show derivations and justify mathematical methods, not just final answers.
  • Interpret results in the engineering, financial, scientific or computational context.
  • Validate models with error analysis, sensitivity checks or suitable data.
  • At postgraduate level, evaluate limitations, compare methods and support claims with scholarly sources.
