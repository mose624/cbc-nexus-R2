Applied and Specialist Mathematics

Two progressive pathways covering engineering, finance, actuarial science, economics, computing, data science, mathematical physics, control systems, decision science and environmental or biological modelling.

How the two levels differ

Undergraduate: develops mathematical foundations, standard analytical and computational techniques, model formulation, interpretation and applied problem solving.

Advanced postgraduate: develops deeper theory, proof-based reasoning, stochastic and optimisation methods, advanced computation, research literature skills and independent model evaluation.

These are broad international topic frameworks, not a claim that every university teaches every topic in this exact order. Institutions differ in prerequisites, course titles, depth, software and assessment. Match formal study to the current programme handbook and module descriptors.

Part A — Undergraduate Applied and Specialist Mathematics

20 core areas for bachelor's degree study and applied specialisation

01

Engineering mathematics and dimensional analysis

Units, dimensions, vector quantities, scaling, error propagation and dimensional consistency in engineering models.

02

Calculus for engineering systems

Differentiation and integration for rates, work, energy, optimisation, transient response and engineering design.

03

Linear algebra for data and engineering

Vectors, matrices, eigenvalues, least squares, systems of equations and matrix methods in engineering and data analysis.

04

Differential equations in physical systems

First- and second-order ODEs, coupled systems, initial/boundary conditions and introductory PDE models.

05

Numerical methods and simulation

Root finding, interpolation, numerical differentiation/integration, ODE solvers, stability, error and computational simulation.

06

Statistics for quality control and experiments

Descriptive and inferential statistics, design of experiments, regression, control charts, process capability and reliability.

07

Operations research and decision science

Model formulation, resource allocation, networks, inventory, queuing, scheduling and decision models.

08

Linear, integer and nonlinear programming

Feasible regions, simplex concepts, integer models, nonlinear constraints, sensitivity and optimisation software.

09

Financial mathematics and compound interest

Simple and compound interest, discounting, annuities, amortisation, effective rates and time value of money.

10

Investment mathematics and cash-flow analysis

Net present value, internal rate of return, bond and equity measures, portfolio returns and project cash flows.

11

Actuarial mathematics and risk

Survival models, life tables, premiums, loss distributions, reserves, risk pooling and introductory ruin concepts.

12

Economics mathematics and econometrics

Optimisation in economics, elasticity, comparative statics, regression, time series and causal inference foundations.

13

Computer science mathematics and algorithms

Asymptotic analysis, recurrence relations, algorithm correctness, complexity and mathematical foundations of computation.

14

Discrete structures, logic and graph algorithms

Sets, relations, combinatorics, Boolean logic, trees, networks, graph traversal and shortest-path methods.

15

Cryptography and elementary number theory

Divisibility, congruences, modular arithmetic, primes, Euclidean algorithm, RSA foundations and secure computation concepts.

16

Data science, probability and statistical learning

Random variables, distributions, estimation, regression, classification, validation, uncertainty and statistical learning foundations.

17

Mathematical physics and modelling

Vector calculus, conservation laws, dimensional models, oscillations, waves, fields and mathematical formulation of physical systems.

18

Control theory and systems mathematics

Linear systems, state-space models, feedback, stability, transfer functions and introductory controller design.

19

Game theory and decision analysis

Strategic-form games, Nash equilibrium, sequential decisions, utility, uncertainty, bargaining and multi-criteria decisions.

20

Environmental, biological and population modelling

Compartment models, population growth, epidemiological dynamics, ecological interactions, parameter estimation and sensitivity.

Part B — Advanced Postgraduate Applied and Specialist Mathematics

20 advanced extensions for Master's, doctoral coursework and research preparation

01

Advanced engineering mathematics and asymptotic methods

Advanced dimensional analysis, perturbation methods, asymptotic expansions, tensor methods and model reduction for coupled engineering systems.

02

Advanced calculus and variational methods for engineering

Vector and tensor calculus, integral theorems, calculus of variations, constrained functionals and optimal design.

03

Advanced computational linear algebra

Spectral theory for matrices, conditioning, sparse systems, Krylov methods, SVD, matrix perturbation and large-scale inverse problems.

04

Advanced differential equations and mathematical physics

Weak formulations, boundary-value problems, Green functions, nonlinear systems, bifurcation and analytical properties of PDE models.

05

Numerical analysis, high-performance simulation and uncertainty

Finite difference/element/volume methods, convergence and stability proofs, adaptive meshes, Monte Carlo simulation and uncertainty quantification.

06

Advanced statistical quality engineering and experimental design

Multivariate process control, sequential monitoring, robust design, Bayesian experiments, reliability growth and industrial data streams.

07

Stochastic operations research and decision science

Markov decision processes, stochastic programming, queueing networks, robust decisions and dynamic optimisation.

08

Advanced optimisation: convex, integer and nonlinear methods

Duality, KKT conditions, decomposition, branch-and-bound, cutting planes, nonconvex optimisation and convergence analysis.

09

Quantitative finance and advanced interest-rate models

Term-structure mathematics, duration/convexity, stochastic discounting, arbitrage-free pricing and interest-rate dynamics.

10

Investment theory, portfolio optimisation and derivatives

Mean-variance theory, factor models, CAPM foundations, option pricing, risk-neutral valuation and cash-flow optimisation.

11

Actuarial risk theory and insurance mathematics

Collective risk models, credibility, survival analysis, reserving, ruin probabilities, reinsurance and solvency capital.

12

Advanced econometrics and mathematical economics

Identification, simultaneous equations, panel data, time-series processes, causal inference and equilibrium modelling.

13

Algorithmic mathematics and theoretical computer science

Randomised algorithms, complexity classes, approximation, optimisation algorithms, formal verification and computational number theory.

14

Advanced discrete mathematics and graph optimisation

Extremal and algebraic graph theory, network flows, matching, matroids, spectral graphs and approximation algorithms.

15

Modern cryptography and computational number theory

Finite fields, elliptic curves, lattices, complexity assumptions, public-key constructions and cryptographic proof techniques.

16

Statistical learning theory and advanced data science

Generalisation bounds, regularisation, Bayesian learning, high-dimensional statistics, causal machine learning and model uncertainty.

17

Advanced mathematical physics and inverse modelling

Operator methods, spectral problems, nonlinear waves, inverse problems, PDE-constrained inference and parameter identifiability.

18

Nonlinear control and dynamical systems

Lyapunov stability, nonlinear feedback, controllability, observability, optimal control and robust/adaptive control.

19

Advanced game theory and mechanism design

Bayesian and evolutionary games, repeated games, auctions, mechanism design, information asymmetry and equilibrium refinements.

20

Advanced environmental, biological and population dynamics

Reaction-diffusion systems, spatial ecology, infectious-disease dynamics, optimal harvesting, climate-risk models and parameter inference.

Study and assessment expectations

  • State assumptions, define variables and use consistent units.
  • Show derivations and justify mathematical methods, not just final answers.
  • Interpret results in the engineering, financial, scientific or computational context.
  • Validate models with error analysis, sensitivity checks or suitable data.
  • At postgraduate level, evaluate limitations, compare methods and support claims with scholarly sources.
