# CBE Nexus undergraduate chapter quality checklist

Use this checklist when writing or reviewing each undergraduate chapter. It is a quality standard, not a word-count target. A chapter should be as long as the subject requires, with no filler. Do not impose arbitrary 5,000- or 10,000-word targets: completeness, accuracy, clarity and demonstrated learning take priority over length.

## 0. Learning design and outcomes

- State clear, assessable learning outcomes at the beginning of the chapter.
- Organise the chapter in a logical sequence from foundational knowledge to advanced application.
- Connect explanations, diagrams, worked examples and assessment questions directly to the stated outcomes.
- Include prerequisite knowledge and explain important links to subsequent topics.
- Use accessible language without sacrificing correct university-level terminology; explain specialist language when first introduced.

## 1. Definitions and glossary

- Include approximately 10–20 chapter-relevant technical terms when appropriate.
- Define each term accurately in the context of the discipline; distinguish similar terms.
- Expand abbreviations at first use and define symbols, units and notation.
- For medical and nursing topics, use accepted anatomical, physiological and clinical terminology and identify jurisdiction-dependent practice terms.
- Link to the [Undergraduate Academic Glossary and Formula Library](https://cbenexus.com/undergraduate-glossary.html), but do not substitute a generic glossary for chapter-specific definitions.

## 2. University-level explanations

- Explain the underlying principles, mechanisms, theories and assumptions.
- Distinguish established evidence, models, hypotheses and areas of uncertainty.
- Explain why a concept matters and how it connects to prior and later topics.
- Use discipline-appropriate examples and applications.
- Avoid unsupported claims, superficial paraphrase and padding.

## 3. Diagrams and illustrations

- Use original, accurate, labelled diagrams where they materially improve understanding.
- Provide captions that explain what the learner should notice.
- Add meaningful alt text; do not use keyword-stuffed image descriptions.
- Keep labels legible on mobile and in print.
- Identify schematic diagrams as schematic; do not imply that a teaching diagram is a diagnostic image or engineering drawing.

## 4. Worked examples and applications

- Show each step and explain why it is taken.
- Define symbols and units, state assumptions, and check dimensions or plausibility.
- Include practical applications, cases or worked calculations relevant to the subject.
- For clinical, legal, financial and engineering cases, clearly distinguish educational scenarios from professional advice or operational instructions.

## 5. Examination preparation

- Aim for at least 15–20 substantial questions per chapter where appropriate.
- Include a range of recall, explanation, application, calculation, comparison, analysis and evaluation tasks.
- Provide accurate answer guidance, model calculations or marking points.
- Ensure questions assess the material actually taught in that chapter.
- Where interactive self-testing is provided, derive it from the chapter's authored questions and answer guidance; do not fabricate distractors or unsupported marking.

## 6. References and AI answer-readiness

- Cite authoritative textbooks, peer-reviewed research, professional standards and official sources at the point of use.
- Prefer primary sources and current regulator or institution guidance for changing requirements.
- Record publication or access dates where useful; never invent citations, authors, credentials or dates.
- Use question-based headings with a concise direct answer followed by the full explanation.
- Name the discipline, units, population, standard and jurisdiction where they affect interpretation.
- Preserve caveats when content is summarised by search engines or AI systems.

## 7. Internal links and learning progression

- Link to the parent course and relevant prerequisite chapters.
- Link to the next chapter and genuinely related topics.
- Recommend only existing course pages with a clear academic relationship.
- Use descriptive anchor text and check every destination for a working page.
- Avoid repeated sitewide link blocks that do not help the learner.

## Shared learning tools

CBE Nexus undergraduate chapter pages can provide:

- A searchable glossary with subject-specific terms and a link to the complete academic glossary.
- A health-science terminology and anatomy reference on relevant chapters.
- A formula and equations library on relevant science, engineering and finance chapters.
- Interactive self-tests using existing chapter questions and their answer guidance.
- Print-friendly chapter revision checklists and the browser's print-to-PDF function.
- Related undergraduate course recommendations.

The global glossary currently provides an interdisciplinary reference set and a formula collection. These are starter resources and should be expanded through academic review; they are not yet a complete, independently reviewed glossary for every degree programme.

## Editorial review before publication

A reviewer should confirm:
- The chapter teaches every stated learning outcome at an appropriate undergraduate level; no section exists merely to increase word count.
- Terminology, equations, diagrams, units and examples are correct.
- References exist, are relevant and support the claims cited.
- Questions and answers align with the chapter learning outcomes.
- Clinical/legal/financial/engineering safety and jurisdictional caveats are present where needed.
- Page title, description, canonical URL, structured data and internal links match the visible page.
- The page is accessible, mobile-friendly, printable and free of broken links.

Do not claim that a page is officially endorsed, accredited, guaranteed to rank, or academically reviewed unless that status has been verified and documented.
