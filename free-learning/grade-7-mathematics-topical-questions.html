Grade 7 Mathematics Practice: Fractions and Operations

Prepared by the CBE Nexus Education Content Team · Updated 14 November 2026 · Read online at no cost

Free Grade 7 Mathematics revision notes on equivalent fractions, comparing fractions and the four operations, with worked examples and ten questions with answers.

1. What is a fraction?

A fraction represents part of a whole or part of a group. In 3/5, the numerator 3 tells how many equal parts are selected and the denominator 5 tells how many equal parts make the whole. The denominator cannot be zero.

Worked example

A cake is divided into 8 equal pieces. If 3 pieces are eaten, the fraction eaten is 3/8 and the fraction remaining is 5/8.

2. Equivalent fractions

Equivalent fractions have the same value even though their numerators and denominators differ. Multiply or divide both parts by the same non-zero number.

Example: Find a fraction equivalent to 2/3 with denominator 12.

Since 3 × 4 = 12, multiply the numerator by 4 as well: 2 × 4 = 8. Therefore, 2/3 = 8/12.

3. Adding and subtracting fractions

When denominators are equal, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator. When denominators differ, first find a common denominator.

Example A: 2/7 + 3/7

(2 + 3)/7 = 5/7.

Example B: 1/4 + 1/2

Convert 1/2 to 2/4. Then 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.

Example C: 5/6 − 1/3

Convert 1/3 to 2/6. Then 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.

4. Multiplying and dividing fractions

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

Example A: 2/3 × 3/5

(2 × 3)/(3 × 5) = 6/15 = 2/5.

Example B: 3/4 ÷ 2/5

3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8.

5. Simplifying fractions

Divide the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor. For example, 12/18 can be divided by 6 to give 2/3. Check whether the final numerator and denominator have a common factor greater than 1.

Topical practice questions

  1. Write an equivalent fraction to 3/4 with denominator 20.
  2. Simplify 15/25.
  3. Which is greater: 2/3 or 3/5?
  4. Calculate 4/9 + 2/9.
  5. Calculate 1/3 + 1/6.
  6. Calculate 7/8 − 3/8.
  7. Calculate 5/6 − 1/2.
  8. Calculate 3/5 × 10/9 and simplify.
  9. Calculate 2/3 ÷ 4/5.
  10. A water container is 3/4 full. A learner uses 1/4 of the container's full capacity. What fraction of its capacity remains?
Answers with brief working
  1. 15/20 (multiply top and bottom by 5).
  2. 3/5 (divide by 5).
  3. 2/3 is greater: 2/3 = 10/15 and 3/5 = 9/15.
  4. 6/9 = 2/3.
  5. 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.
  6. 4/8 = 1/2.
  7. 5/6 − 3/6 = 2/6 = 1/3.
  8. 30/45 = 2/3.
  9. 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6.
  10. 3/4 − 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.

Study tip

Show each step, simplify your final answer and check that your result is reasonable. Use diagrams or fraction strips when you are unsure how the parts fit together.
