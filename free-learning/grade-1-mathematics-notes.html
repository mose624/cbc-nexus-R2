Grade 1 Mathematics Starter Notes: Counting and Number Operations

Prepared by the CBE Nexus Education Content Team · Updated 14 November 2026 · Read online at no cost

Free Grade 1 Mathematics notes with counting, comparing numbers, addition, subtraction, worked examples and practice questions for Kenyan learners.

1. Counting and reading numbers

Numbers tell us how many objects we have. Count one object at a time and say the number names in order. A number can be shown using objects, drawings, words or numerals.

Example: Count the objects

● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●

There are eight dots. The numeral is 8 and the number word is eight.

Practise counting forwards from 0 to 20 and backwards from 20 to 0. Use bottle tops, beans or sticks to represent each number.

2. Comparing numbers

Count each group. The group with the greater number has more objects. The group with the smaller number has fewer objects. Equal groups have the same number.

Worked example

Group A has 6 counters. Group B has 9 counters. Group B has 3 more counters than Group A.

3. Addition means putting together

Addition combines groups to find the total. The plus sign is + and the equals sign is =.

Worked example: 7 + 5
  1. Start with 7.
  2. Count on five steps: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12.
  3. Therefore, 7 + 5 = 12.

Another way is to make ten: 7 + 3 = 10, then add the remaining 2 to get 12.

4. Subtraction means taking away

Subtraction finds how many remain or the difference between two numbers. The minus sign is −.

Worked example: 13 − 4

Count backwards four steps from 13: 12, 11, 10, 9. Therefore, 13 − 4 = 9.

5. Patterns, shapes and measurement

A pattern repeats a rule, for example red, blue, red, blue. A triangle has three straight sides; a square has four equal sides; a rectangle has four sides with opposite sides equal. Compare length using longer, shorter and equal. Compare mass using heavier and lighter.

Check your understanding

  1. Write the number word for 14.
  2. Which is greater: 8 or 5?
  3. Complete: 2, 4, 6, __, __.
  4. Find 6 + 3.
  5. Find 9 + 5.
  6. Find 12 − 2.
  7. Find 15 − 6.
  8. How many sides does a triangle have?
  9. Which is longer: a 10 cm ribbon or a 7 cm ribbon?
  10. There are 8 oranges. 3 are eaten. How many remain?
Answers

1. Fourteen. 2. 8. 3. 8, 10. 4. 9. 5. 14. 6. 10. 7. 9. 8. Three. 9. The 10 cm ribbon. 10. 5 oranges.

Home practice

Count 15 household objects and arrange them in groups of five. Make two addition and two subtraction stories using the objects. Explain how you found each answer.
