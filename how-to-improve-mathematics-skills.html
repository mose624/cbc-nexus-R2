How Can I Improve My Mathematics Skills?

Learn how to strengthen mathematics foundations, solve math problems step by step, practise effectively and overcome common mistakes.

Important: Course availability, admission rules, professional licensing, scholarship benefits and deadlines can change. Verify current requirements with the official provider or regulator before applying or paying.

How to solve math problems and identify the exact topic causing difficulty

Mathematics becomes easier when you identify the missing skill instead of saying “I am bad at maths”. List topics such as whole numbers, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio, algebra, geometry, measurement, graphs, probability or statistics. Try a short set of questions and mark each topic as secure, developing or not yet understood. When you make an error, identify whether it came from a concept you do not understand, a forgotten rule, a calculation slip or misreading the question.

Rebuild the foundations before moving ahead

Many advanced topics depend on earlier skills. If algebra is difficult, review operations with negative numbers, fractions and order of operations. If percentages are confusing, practise fractions and decimals first. Use number lines, diagrams, counters, tables and graphs to make abstract ideas visible. Explain what a formula means and why it works instead of memorising it without understanding. Ask a teacher to show a worked example and explain each step.

Use a learn–practise–check cycle

Study one concept, examine a worked example, then solve a similar question without looking at the solution. Check your answer and correct the first step where your reasoning went wrong. After that, try a question with different numbers or a new context. This gradual change helps you learn to select a method rather than copy a pattern. Keep a mistake log with the question type, the error, the correct reasoning and a similar question to retry later.

How to solve word problems

Read the whole problem twice. On the first reading, identify what is happening. On the second, underline the quantities, units and the exact question. Draw a diagram or make a table if useful. Decide which mathematical relationship applies, write the calculation before using a calculator, and show each step. Finally, check whether the answer is reasonable and whether the unit is correct. Do not assume that a word such as “more” always means addition; understand the relationship described.

Practise consistently and use reliable resources

Short, focused practice on most days is usually more manageable than cramming. Begin with a few questions you can solve, then attempt increasingly challenging examples. Khan Academy offers free lessons and practice in many mathematics topics; select the level and topic that match your needs. Use your school syllabus and teacher-provided materials as the main guide for what you are expected to learn. If you are preparing for an examination, practise questions in the format and time limits set by the examination authority.

A simple 4-week improvement plan

Week 1: identify gaps and revise number skills. Week 2: focus on fractions, decimals, percentages and ratio. Week 3: work on algebra, geometry or the topics required by your class. Week 4: mix topics, complete timed practice and review your mistake log. At the end of each week, redo questions you previously missed. Ask a teacher, tutor or study partner to explain any mistake you cannot resolve.

Frequently asked questions

How many maths problems should I solve daily? Quality matters more than a fixed number. Start with a manageable set, check every answer and understand each mistake. Can I improve if I have struggled for years? Yes. Rebuilding foundations and practising regularly can help you make progress. Should I use a calculator? Follow your curriculum and examination rules; learn the underlying method and practise mental or written calculations as required.
