How Can I Improve My Mathematics Skills?

Start by identifying the exact topic that is difficult: fractions, decimals, algebra, geometry, graphs or word problems. Strengthen prerequisite skills, study worked examples and explain the method in your own words. Practise a little every day instead of cramming before a test.

Practical steps and further guidance

Use a cycle of learn, practise, check and correct. Show every step, review mistakes and solve a similar problem without looking at the answer. Khan Academy provides free mathematics lessons and exercises. Teachers can reinforce learning with curriculum-aligned notes and practice questions.

International standards: expanded guidance

Identify the exact skill that needs work

Replace “I am bad at mathematics” with a specific diagnosis: fractions, decimals, percentages, algebra, geometry, measurement, graphs, statistics or word problems. Attempt a short diagnostic set and classify errors as concept gaps, weak prerequisites, calculation slips or misread instructions.

Rebuild foundations and use visual models

Many advanced topics depend on earlier concepts. Connect symbols with diagrams, number lines, tables, shapes and real examples. Understand why a method works before memorising a formula. For example, link fractions to equal parts and division, and link algebraic equations to the idea of maintaining balance.

Use worked examples and deliberate practice

Study a worked solution, explain each step, cover it and attempt a similar question independently. Gradually practise mixed questions where you must decide which method to use. Keep an error notebook and reattempt corrected questions after a gap.

Practise consistently and seek feedback

Short sessions across the week help learners revisit ideas and notice progress. Explain your reasoning aloud and ask a teacher to identify the first incorrect step. Use timed work after conceptual understanding is secure; speed should not replace accuracy and reasoning.

Free practice: Khan Academy Mathematics offers lessons and exercises across multiple levels; choose material that matches your curriculum.
