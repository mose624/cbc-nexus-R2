Undergraduate pathway

About this study guide

Data Science combines statistical reasoning, computing and subject knowledge to extract useful evidence from data. Artificial Intelligence develops computational systems that perform tasks involving prediction, perception, language, planning and decision support. This pathway develops the foundations needed to collect, clean, analyse, model, evaluate and communicate data responsibly.

The sixteen chapters are an illustrative course structure, not a claim that all universities use the same curriculum or award the same degree title. Institutions may place topics in different years, use different prerequisites and require additional mathematics or domain-specific modules. Always compare these notes with your university's approved programme and assessment requirements.

Chapter standard: Each chapter is intended to contain at least 5,000 substantive words, labelled and explained diagrams, mathematical derivations, worked data examples, practical coding tasks, revision material and structured assessment questions.

Programme learning outcomes

  • Use probability, statistics, linear algebra and calculus to reason about data and models.
  • Write reproducible analytical programs and use version control.
  • Acquire, clean, transform, store and validate structured and unstructured data.
  • Choose appropriate statistical and machine-learning methods and evaluate them rigorously.
  • Explain neural networks, deep learning and modern AI system architectures.
  • Build data pipelines and reason about scalable data platforms.
  • Communicate uncertainty, limitations and findings using appropriate visualisations.
  • Address privacy, fairness, transparency, security and accountability in AI systems.

Year 1 — Mathematical and Programming Foundations

Chapter 01

Introduction to Data Science and Artificial Intelligence

Data science lifecycle, AI history, types of data, problem formulation, data ethics, computational thinking and applications.

Chapter 02

Programming for Data Science with Python

Python syntax, control flow, functions, data structures, modules, NumPy, pandas, testing, debugging and reproducible notebooks.

Chapter 03

Mathematics for Data Science

Linear algebra, vectors, matrices, eigenvalues, calculus, gradients, optimisation, logarithms and numerical methods.

Chapter 04

Probability and Statistical Foundations

Probability rules, random variables, distributions, expectation, variance, sampling, estimation, confidence intervals and hypothesis testing.

Year 2 — Data Management and Statistical Learning

Chapter 05

Data Wrangling, Exploration and Visualisation

Data quality, missing values, outliers, transformations, exploratory analysis, visual encoding, dashboards and communication.

Chapter 06

Database Systems and SQL for Analytics

Relational modelling, SQL, joins, aggregation, normalization, transactions, indexing, analytical queries and data warehouses.

Chapter 07

Statistical Modelling and Inference

Linear models, regression assumptions, diagnostics, ANOVA, generalized linear models, Bayesian inference and uncertainty.

Chapter 08

Machine Learning Fundamentals

Supervised and unsupervised learning, train-validation-test splits, feature engineering, regularisation, model selection and evaluation.

Year 3 — Advanced AI and Data Engineering

Chapter 09

Data Engineering and Big Data Systems

ETL/ELT, distributed storage, batch and stream processing, data lakes, orchestration, quality monitoring and scalable pipelines.

Chapter 10

Deep Learning and Neural Networks

Perceptrons, backpropagation, activation functions, optimisation, CNNs, sequence models, regularisation and model deployment.

Chapter 11

Natural Language Processing

Text preprocessing, tokenisation, language models, embeddings, sequence learning, transformers, information retrieval and evaluation.

Chapter 12

Computer Vision and Image Analytics

Image representation, filtering, feature extraction, classification, object detection, segmentation, convolutional networks and metrics.

Year 4 — Responsible AI and Professional Practice

Chapter 13

Reinforcement Learning and Intelligent Agents

Markov decision processes, rewards, policies, value functions, dynamic programming, Q-learning and policy evaluation.

Chapter 14

Responsible AI, Privacy and Governance

Fairness, bias, explainability, privacy protection, security, model documentation, audit, risk management and governance.

Chapter 15

AI Deployment, MLOps and Cloud Platforms

Model serving, APIs, containers, pipelines, experiment tracking, monitoring, drift detection, CI/CD and lifecycle management.

Chapter 16

Research Methods and Final-Year Data Science Project

Research design, literature review, data collection, reproducibility, evaluation, technical writing, project governance and presentation.

Recommended practical toolkit

Students may encounter Python, Jupyter, NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, scikit-learn, SQL, Git, a deep-learning framework and cloud-based data tools. The choice of tools should follow the learning objective and institutional requirements; software names are not substitutes for understanding the underlying mathematics and assumptions.

How to study

  1. Review learning outcomes and mathematical prerequisites.
  2. Reproduce every worked calculation with a calculator or notebook.
  3. Run code on small datasets and inspect intermediate outputs.
  4. Compare model performance using appropriate metrics and validation methods.
  5. Document assumptions, data provenance, uncertainty and limitations.
  6. Attempt assessment questions independently before reviewing model answers.
