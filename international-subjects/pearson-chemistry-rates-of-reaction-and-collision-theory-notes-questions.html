5. Rates of reaction and collision theory

Particles must collide for a reaction to occur. A collision is successful only if particles have sufficient energy to overcome the activation energy and have a suitable orientation. Increasing temperature increases average kinetic energy and the proportion of particles with energy at or above activation energy.

Increasing concentration or gas pressure increases collision frequency. Increasing the surface area of a solid exposes more particles for collisions. A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy and is not used up overall.

Rates can be investigated by measuring gas volume produced, mass lost, or time taken for a visible change such as a precipitate to obscure a mark. Keep control variables constant and repeat measurements.

mean rate = change in measured quantity ÷ time taken

Further textbook notes: rate experiments

Rate of reaction can be measured by tracking loss of mass when a gas escapes, gas volume produced, or change in concentration. A graph of product formed against time is steepest when the reaction is fastest; the gradient at a particular point gives instantaneous rate. Keep the method, apparatus and measurement intervals consistent.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: Increasing temperature raises particle kinetic energy and the fraction of collisions with energy equal to or greater than activation energy, so more collisions are successful each second.
Exam-style question (3 marks)

Explain why increasing temperature increases reaction rate using collision theory.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Particles move faster/have greater kinetic energy (1); collide more frequently (1); a greater proportion have energy at least activation energy (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Simply saying particles collide harder is insufficient without the activation-energy idea.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

A reaction produces 60 cm³ of gas in 30 s. Calculate its average rate in cm³/s. State one way to increase rate without changing the reactants.

Show answer and mark scheme

Rate = volume/time = 60/30 = 2.0 cm³/s (2); increase temperature, concentration, pressure for gases, or surface area (1).
