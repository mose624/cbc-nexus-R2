6. Momentum and collisions

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity. It is a vector, so direction matters.

momentum p = mass m × velocity v

In a closed system with no resultant external force, total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after it. This is the principle of conservation of momentum.

Impulse

change in momentum = resultant force × time

Increasing the time taken to change momentum reduces the average force for the same momentum change. Crumple zones, airbags and padded sports equipment increase collision time and help reduce injury risk.

Worked example

A 0.15 kg ball moves at 20 m s⁻¹. Its momentum is 0.15 × 20 = 3.0 kg m s⁻¹. If it is brought to rest in 0.10 s, average resultant force magnitude = 3.0 ÷ 0.10 = 30 N.

Choose a positive direction before substituting velocities in collision questions. A velocity in the opposite direction must carry a negative sign.

Further textbook notes: momentum and impulse

Momentum: Momentum p = mv is a vector. Its direction is the direction of velocity. In a closed system, total momentum is conserved when the resultant external force or external impulse is negligible. Choose a positive direction before substituting values; objects moving in the opposite direction have negative velocity.

Impulse: Impulse equals change in momentum, Ft = Δp, for a constant resultant force. On a force–time graph, the area under the graph represents impulse. Increasing collision time for the same change in momentum reduces average force. This explains the use of airbags, seat belts, crumple zones and padded sports equipment.

Collision types: Momentum is conserved in both elastic and inelastic collisions in an isolated system. Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision; in an inelastic collision some kinetic energy is transferred to internal energy, sound or deformation.

Worked example: A 0.20 kg ball moving at 5 m/s is brought to rest. Change in momentum = 0−(0.20×5) = −1.0 kg m/s. If stopping takes 0.05 s, average force = −1.0/0.05 = −20 N; the negative sign indicates the force opposes the initial motion.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: A 0.5 kg ball moves at 8 m/s. Momentum p=mv=4 kg m/s in its direction of travel.
Exam-style question (3 marks)

A 0.15 kg ball moving at 10 m/s is stopped in 0.05 s. Calculate average resultant force.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Initial momentum=0.15×10=1.5 kg m/s (1); change in momentum=0−1.5=−1.5 kg m/s (1); F=Δp/Δt=−1.5/0.05=−30 N (1).

Examiner’s explanation: The negative sign means the force acts opposite to the initial motion. Magnitude is 30 N.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

A 0.20 kg ball moving at 10 m/s is brought to rest. Calculate its change in momentum and explain why increasing stopping time reduces force.

Show answer and mark scheme

Initial momentum = 0.20 × 10 = 2.0 kg m/s; final = 0, so change = −2.0 kg m/s (2); for the same momentum change, a longer time gives a smaller force (1).
