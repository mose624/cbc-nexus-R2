Chapter 1: Cell structure and organisation

Cells are the basic structural and functional units of living organisms. Eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; prokaryotic cells lack a nucleus and are generally smaller. Animal cells contain a cell-surface membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria and ribosomes. Plant cells also have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues and a permanent vacuole containing cell sap.

Core explanation

The nucleus contains DNA and controls cell activities. Mitochondria are the main site of aerobic respiration and release energy for cellular processes. Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy. The cell membrane controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.

Worked example / application

A specialised cell has adaptations for its function. A sperm cell has a flagellum and many mitochondria; a root hair cell has a long projection for absorption; a palisade cell contains many chloroplasts. Magnification = image size ÷ actual size, using the same units.

Further textbook notes: cell ultrastructure

The cell-surface membrane controls movement of substances into and out of the cell. The nucleus contains DNA and controls cell activities through gene expression. Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration; ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis. In plants, the cellulose cell wall provides support, the permanent vacuole contains cell sap and chloroplasts contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis.

Prokaryotic cells, such as bacteria, have no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles. Their DNA is free in the cytoplasm, often as a circular molecule, and they may contain plasmids. Eukaryotic cells include animal, plant and fungal cells. Microscope magnification = image size ÷ actual size. Convert units before calculating: 1 mm = 1000 μm.

Practise: Compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells and calculate actual size from an image scale bar.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: A cell image is 40 mm long at ×400 magnification. Actual length=40/400=0.10 mm=100 μm.
Exam-style question (2 marks)

State two differences between a prokaryotic cell and a typical animal cell.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Prokaryote has no nucleus (1); its DNA is free in cytoplasm/circular and it lacks membrane-bound organelles (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Use paired comparisons; bacteria are prokaryotes.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

A cell has a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm and a large permanent vacuole. Identify two features indicating it is a plant cell and state the function of the vacuole.

Show answer and mark scheme

Cell wall and large permanent vacuole (1 each); vacuole contains cell sap and helps maintain turgor/support (1).
