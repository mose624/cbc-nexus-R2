Chapter 7: Pythagoras and trigonometry

In a right-angled triangle, Pythagoras' theorem states that a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse. Trigonometric ratios relate an acute angle to side lengths: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent.

Core explanation

Choose the ratio using the sides known and required. Ensure the calculator is in degree mode when angles are in degrees. The sine rule and cosine rule apply to non-right-angled triangles where included information is known.

Worked example / application

Worked example: a right triangle has opposite side 6 cm and hypotenuse 10 cm. sin θ = 6/10 = 0.6, so θ ≈ 36.9°. A 3–4–5 triangle is a useful Pythagorean triple.

Further textbook notes: Pythagoras and trigonometry

In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Pythagoras states a²+b²=c². The trigonometric ratios are sinθ=opposite/hypotenuse, cosθ=adjacent/hypotenuse and tanθ=opposite/adjacent. Choose the ratio containing the known and required sides. Check calculator mode is degrees when angles are in degrees.

Worked example: If opposite=6 cm and hypotenuse=10 cm, sinθ=0.6, so θ=sin⁻¹(0.6)≈36.9°.

Three additional worked examples — different approaches

Study each method, compare the reasoning and use the final check to verify your answer. These are original teaching examples designed to build flexible problem-solving skills.

Worked example 1: Method 1 — Pythagoras to find a hypotenuse

Question: A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

Step-by-step solution: c² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225. Therefore c = √225 = 15 cm.

Check / exam insight: The hypotenuse must be the longest side.

Worked example 2: Method 2 — trigonometry to find a side

Question: A 10 m ladder makes an angle of 60° with level ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

Step-by-step solution: The height is opposite the angle and the ladder is the hypotenuse. sin 60° = h/10, so h = 10 sin 60° ≈ 8.66 m.

Check / exam insight: Draw and label the right-angled triangle before choosing the ratio.

Worked example 3: Method 3 — inverse trigonometry to find an angle

Question: A right-angled triangle has opposite side 7 cm and adjacent side 10 cm. Find the angle θ.

Step-by-step solution: tan θ = 7/10 = 0.7. Therefore θ = tan⁻¹(0.7) ≈ 35.0° (to 1 decimal place).

Check / exam insight: Check the calculator is in degree mode.

Method and working: cos θ=8/10=0.8; θ=cos⁻¹(0.8).

Method and working: tan θ=9/12=0.75; θ=tan⁻¹(0.75).

Worked question 9: A ramp rises 1.2 m over a horizontal distance of 5 m. Find its angle to horizontal.

Worked question 10: A 6 m ladder makes 70° with ground. Find vertical height.

Method and working: Height is opposite: h=6 sin70°.

Worked question 11: A kite string is 25 m and makes 40° with horizontal. Find vertical height.

Method and working: h=25 sin40°.

Worked question 12: A right triangle has hypotenuse 17 cm and one side 8 cm. Find the other side.

Method and working: √(17²−8²)=√(289−64)=√225.

Worked question 13: Find the angle whose sine is 0.5 between 0° and 90°.

Method and working: Use inverse sine: sin⁻¹(0.5).

Worked question 14: A 10 m wire is fixed to a point 6 m above ground. Find horizontal distance to anchor.

Worked question 15: A hill rises 50 m over horizontal distance 200 m. Find angle of elevation.

Method and working: tan θ=50/200=0.25; θ=tan⁻¹(0.25).

Worked question 16: A triangle has sides 7 cm and 9 cm enclosing 60°. Find its area.

Method and working: Area=½ab sin C=½×7×9×sin60°.

Worked question 17: Find the third side opposite 60° when adjacent sides are 5 cm and 8 cm.

Method and working: Cosine rule: c²=5²+8²−2×5×8×cos60=49.

Worked question 18: A bearing is measured clockwise from north. What is the reverse bearing of 065°?

Method and working: Add 180° because 065° is less than 180°.

Final answer: 245°

Worked question 19: A ship travels 8 km east and 6 km north. Find resultant distance.

Method and working: Use Pythagoras: √(8²+6²)=√100.

Final answer: 10 km

Worked question 20: For a 12 m ramp at 30° to horizontal, find vertical rise.

Method and working: Opposite=12 sin30°=12×0.5.

Final answer: 6 m

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: If opposite=5 and adjacent=12, tanθ=5/12; θ=tan⁻¹(5/12)≈22.6°.
Exam-style question (2 marks)

A ladder 5 m long reaches 4 m up a wall. Find its distance from the wall.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Use Pythagoras (1); √(5²−4²)=√9=3 m (1).

Examiner’s explanation: The ladder is the hypotenuse; identify the right angle.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Show answer and mark scheme
