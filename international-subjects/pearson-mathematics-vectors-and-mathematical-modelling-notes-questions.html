Chapter 8: Vectors and mathematical modelling

A vector has magnitude and direction. In two dimensions, a vector may be written as a column vector. Add vectors by adding corresponding components; subtract by subtracting components. A position vector describes a point relative to an origin.

Core explanation

A mathematical model represents a real situation using variables, equations, graphs or diagrams. State assumptions, define variables and interpret the result in context. Units and sensible rounding are essential when a mathematical answer describes a measurement.

Worked example / application

If a = (3,−2) and b = (−1,5), then a + b = (2,3). The magnitude of (6,8) is √(36 + 64) = 10.

Key learning point

Use precise scientific or mathematical vocabulary, show the reasoning behind each step, and connect the concept to the relevant principle rather than memorising isolated facts.

Further textbook notes: vectors and modelling

A vector has magnitude and direction; a scalar has magnitude only. Column vectors can be added by adding corresponding components. The magnitude of vector (a,b) is √(a²+b²). A position vector describes a point relative to an origin; vector AB is the position vector of B minus that of A. In modelling, define variables, state assumptions and check units and realism.

Worked example: Add vectors (3,−2) and (−1,5): result (2,3), with magnitude √13.

Practise: Find a displacement vector between two points and explain why direction matters.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: Add vectors (2,−1) and (−3,4) to obtain (−1,3). Magnitude=√10.
Exam-style question (2 marks)

A displacement is 6 m east and 8 m north. Find its magnitude.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Use Pythagoras (1); √(6²+8²)=10 m (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Magnitude is positive; direction is a separate part of a vector answer.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

Given vector a = (3, 2) and b = (−1, 4), find 2a + b.

Show answer and mark scheme

2a = (6, 4) (1); add b component-wise (1); result = (5, 8) (1).
