Chapter 7: Coordination, response and homeostasis

Coordination enables organisms to detect and respond to changes. The nervous system uses electrical impulses along neurones; hormonal coordination uses chemical messengers carried in blood. A reflex arc produces a rapid, automatic response.

Core explanation

A receptor detects a stimulus. Sensory neurones carry impulses to the central nervous system; relay neurones connect pathways; motor neurones carry impulses to effectors. A synapse is a junction where neurotransmitters transmit a signal between neurones.

Worked example / application

Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment. Negative feedback detects deviation from a set point and activates effectors that oppose the change. Thermoregulation involves sweating and vasodilation when hot, and shivering and vasoconstriction when cold.

Key learning point

Chapter self-check

Draw and label a reflex arc. Explain why a reflex is protective. Describe negative feedback in temperature regulation.

Further textbook notes: nervous and hormonal coordination

A stimulus is detected by receptors; sensory neurones carry impulses to the central nervous system, and motor neurones carry impulses to effectors. At a synapse, neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap and binds to receptors on the next neurone. Reflex actions are rapid and automatic, helping protect the body.

Hormones are chemical messengers carried in blood to target organs. They usually act more slowly than nerve impulses but their effects can last longer. Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment, including blood glucose, body temperature and water balance. Negative feedback reverses a deviation from a set point.

Practise: Label a reflex arc and explain how insulin and glucagon regulate blood glucose.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: A reflex arc: receptor→sensory neurone→CNS/relay neurone→motor neurone→effector. Insulin lowers blood glucose by promoting uptake and glycogen formation.
Exam-style question (4 marks)

Describe the pathway of a simple reflex action.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Receptor detects stimulus (1); sensory neurone carries impulse to CNS (1); relay neurone passes signal to motor neurone (1); motor neurone stimulates effector to respond (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Use the correct sequence and distinguish sensory from motor neurones.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

A person touches a hot object and quickly withdraws a hand. Name the pathway and explain why the response is rapid.

Show answer and mark scheme

Receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone in spinal cord → motor neurone → effector (2); it is a reflex action that is rapid and automatic, reducing injury (1).
