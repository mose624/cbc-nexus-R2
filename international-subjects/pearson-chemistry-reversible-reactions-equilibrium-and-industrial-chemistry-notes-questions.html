12. Reversible reactions, equilibrium and industrial chemistry

A reversible reaction can proceed in both directions. In a closed system at dynamic equilibrium, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal and concentrations remain constant, although reactions continue at particle level.

Changing concentration, pressure (for gaseous equilibria) or temperature can shift the equilibrium position. A catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally and helps equilibrium be reached faster, but does not change the equilibrium position.

For an exothermic forward reaction, increasing temperature favours the reverse direction; for an endothermic forward reaction, increasing temperature favours the forward direction. Increasing pressure favours the side with fewer moles of gas, where the number of gas particles differs.

Further textbook notes: equilibrium and industrial conditions

Dynamic equilibrium is established in a closed system when forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. Concentrations remain constant, but reactions continue. A change in concentration, pressure or temperature can shift the equilibrium position to oppose the change (Le Chatelier’s principle).

For a gaseous equilibrium, increasing pressure favours the side with fewer moles of gas; decreasing pressure favours the side with more gas. Increasing temperature favours the endothermic direction. A catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally and helps equilibrium establish faster, but does not change its position or yield at equilibrium.

Industrial conditions balance yield, rate, cost, safety and equipment. The Haber process uses nitrogen and hydrogen to make ammonia; the forward reaction is exothermic and produces fewer gas molecules, so lower temperature and higher pressure favour yield, while a moderate temperature and iron catalyst give a practical rate.

Practise: Predict the effect of changing temperature and pressure on a stated equilibrium and explain why industrial conditions are compromises.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: For an exothermic forward reaction, increasing temperature shifts equilibrium towards reactants. A catalyst speeds up both directions but does not change equilibrium position.
Exam-style question (2 marks)

For N₂+3H₂⇌2NH₃, predict the effect of increasing pressure and explain.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Equilibrium shifts to the right/towards ammonia (1); right side has fewer gas molecules (2 vs 4) (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Compare total gaseous mole numbers on both sides.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

For a reversible reaction at equilibrium, explain the effect of increasing pressure when the product side has fewer gas molecules.

Show answer and mark scheme

Equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules (1); therefore towards products (1); the forward reaction is favoured until a new dynamic equilibrium is established (1).
