3. Formulae, equations and the mole

The mole is the amount of substance containing the Avogadro number of specified particles. Relative formula mass is found by adding the relative atomic masses in a formula.

moles n = mass m ÷ molar mass M
mass = moles × molar mass

Use grams for mass and g mol⁻¹ for molar mass in the common school calculation. The balanced equation gives the mole ratio between reactants and products.

Worked example

Calculate the amount of substance in 10.0 g of calcium carbonate, CaCO₃. Mᵣ = 40.1 + 12.0 + (3 × 16.0) = 100.1. Moles = 10.0 ÷ 100.1 = 0.0999 mol (3 significant figures).

For a limiting-reactant question, calculate the available moles of each reactant, divide by its equation coefficient, and identify the smallest result. That reactant is used up first.

Further textbook notes: reacting masses and concentration

A balanced equation gives the mole ratio in which substances react. Convert mass to moles using n = m/Mr, use the equation ratio, then convert the required moles back to mass or volume. The limiting reactant is used up first and determines the maximum product formed. Percentage yield = actual yield/theoretical yield × 100%.

For solutions, concentration in mol/dm³ = moles/volume in dm³. Convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by 1000. In titration calculations, use the balanced equation ratio rather than assuming a 1:1 reaction.

Worked example: 0.20 mol of magnesium reacts with excess hydrochloric acid: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. The equation gives 1 mol Mg to 1 mol H₂, so 0.20 mol Mg produces 0.20 mol H₂ theoretically.

Practise: Calculate the mass of product from a balanced equation and identify the limiting reactant when two reactant amounts are supplied.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: For 2Mg+O₂→2MgO, 2 mol Mg produces 2 mol MgO. If 0.5 mol Mg reacts fully, it forms 0.5 mol MgO.
Exam-style question (2 marks)

Calculate moles in 9.0 g of water, Mr=18.

Show model answer and mark allocation

n=m/Mr (1); n=9.0/18=0.50 mol (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Show the formula and substitute mass and relative formula mass.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

Calculate the relative formula mass of CaCO₃. Then calculate the amount in moles in 10.0 g of CaCO₃. (Ar: Ca=40, C=12, O=16)

Show answer and mark scheme

Mr = 40 + 12 + (3 × 16) = 100 (2); moles = mass/Mr = 10.0/100 = 0.100 mol (1).
