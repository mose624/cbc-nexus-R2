Physics

Physical quantities, units and measurement

Worked example 1 A student measures a metal rod as 2.40 m. Convert this length to centimetres.
  1. 1 m = 100 cm
  2. Multiply 2.40 by 100

Final answer: 240 cm

Worked example 2 A balance reads 156 g. Express the mass in kilograms.
  1. 1 kg = 1000 g
  2. Divide 156 by 1000

Final answer: 0.156 kg

Worked example 3 A ruler has a smallest division of 1 mm. State a suitable reading uncertainty for one measurement.
  1. For an analogue scale, use about half the smallest division

Final answer: ±0.5 mm

Physics

Motion: distance, displacement, speed and acceleration

Worked example 1 A runner covers 150 m in 30 s. Calculate average speed.
  1. speed = distance ÷ time = 150 ÷ 30

Final answer: 5.0 m/s

Worked example 2 A car changes velocity from 4 m/s to 20 m/s in 8 s. Find acceleration.
  1. a = (v − u) ÷ t = (20 − 4) ÷ 8

Final answer: 2.0 m/s²

Worked example 3 A cyclist travels 60 m east then 20 m west. Find distance and displacement.
  1. Distance = 60 + 20 = 80 m
  2. Displacement = 60 − 20 = 40 m east

Final answer: 80 m distance; 40 m east displacement

Physics

Forces, mass, weight and Newton’s laws

Worked example 1 Find the weight of a 6 kg object where g = 10 N/kg.
  1. W = mg = 6 × 10

Final answer: 60 N

Worked example 2 A resultant force of 18 N acts on a 3 kg trolley. Find its acceleration.
  1. F = ma, so a = F ÷ m = 18 ÷ 3

Final answer: 6 m/s²

Worked example 3 A 12 N force acts right and a 5 N force left. Find the resultant force.
  1. Take right as positive: 12 − 5 = 7 N

Final answer: 7 N to the right

Physics

Moments, pressure and density

Worked example 1 A force of 20 N acts 0.30 m from a pivot. Calculate the moment.
  1. Moment = force × perpendicular distance = 20 × 0.30

Final answer: 6.0 N m

Worked example 2 A force of 400 N acts over 0.20 m². Find pressure.
  1. P = F ÷ A = 400 ÷ 0.20

Final answer: 2000 Pa

Worked example 3 A block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Find density.
  1. density = mass ÷ volume = 540 ÷ 200

Final answer: 2.7 g/cm³

Physics

Energy stores, transfers, work and power

Worked example 1 A 2 kg object is lifted 3 m. Calculate gain in GPE using g = 10 N/kg.
  1. ΔGPE = mgh = 2 × 10 × 3

Final answer: 60 J

Worked example 2 A 50 N force moves a box 4 m in the force direction. Find work done.
  1. W = Fd = 50 × 4

Final answer: 200 J

Worked example 3 A motor transfers 900 J in 15 s. Calculate power.
  1. P = E ÷ t = 900 ÷ 15

Final answer: 60 W

Physics

Momentum and collisions

Worked example 1 Find momentum of a 0.8 kg ball moving at 5 m/s.
  1. p = mv = 0.8 × 5

Final answer: 4.0 kg m/s

Worked example 2 A 2 kg trolley moving at 3 m/s stops. Find change in momentum.
  1. Initial p = 2 × 3 = 6; final p = 0
  2. Change = 0 − 6

Final answer: −6 kg m/s (magnitude 6)

Worked example 3 A 1 kg trolley at 4 m/s joins a stationary 3 kg trolley. Find their shared speed.
  1. Momentum before = 1×4 + 3×0 = 4
  2. Total mass = 4 kg
  3. v = 4÷4

Final answer: 1 m/s

Physics

Waves and the electromagnetic spectrum

Worked example 1 A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Find wave speed.
  1. v = fλ = 5 × 2

Final answer: 10 m/s

Worked example 2 A sound wave travels 680 m in 2 s. Find its speed.
  1. v = distance ÷ time = 680 ÷ 2

Final answer: 340 m/s

Worked example 3 A wave has period 0.02 s. Calculate frequency.
  1. f = 1 ÷ T = 1 ÷ 0.02

Final answer: 50 Hz

Physics

Electricity, charge and circuits

Worked example 1 A 12 V supply drives 3 A through a resistor. Find resistance.
  1. V = IR, so R = V ÷ I = 12 ÷ 3

Final answer: 4 Ω

Worked example 2 A 6 Ω resistor carries 2 A. Find potential difference.
  1. V = IR = 2 × 6

Final answer: 12 V

Worked example 3 A current of 0.5 A flows for 4 minutes. Find charge transferred.
  1. Q = It
  2. Convert 4 min to 240 s
  3. Q = 0.5 × 240

Final answer: 120 C

Physics

Magnetism and electromagnetism

Worked example 1 A transformer has 200 primary turns and 50 secondary turns. If input is 240 V, find output voltage.
  1. Vs/Vp = Ns/Np
  2. Vs = 240 × 50 ÷ 200

Final answer: 60 V

Worked example 2 A wire carries 3 A for 10 s. Find charge passing a point.
  1. Q = It = 3 × 10

Final answer: 30 C

Worked example 3 State how to increase the strength of an electromagnet.
  1. Increase current, add coil turns, or insert a soft-iron core

Final answer: Any one correctly explained method

Physics

Radioactivity and nuclear physics

Worked example 1 A sample has a half-life of 6 hours. What fraction remains after 18 hours?
  1. 18 ÷ 6 = 3 half-lives
  2. Remaining fraction = (1/2)³

Final answer: 1/8 (12.5%)

Worked example 2 A count rate falls from 800 to 100 counts/min. How many half-lives have passed?
  1. 800 → 400 → 200 → 100: three halvings

Final answer: 3 half-lives

Worked example 3 Compare alpha and gamma penetration.
  1. Alpha is strongly ionising and stopped by paper/skin; gamma is weakly ionising and highly penetrating, reduced by thick lead/concrete

Final answer: Correct comparison with both properties

Physics

Thermal physics, particle model and heat transfer

Worked example 1 Calculate energy to heat 0.5 kg water by 10°C. Use c = 4200 J/kg°C.
  1. Q = mcΔT = 0.5 × 4200 × 10

Final answer: 21 000 J

Worked example 2 A 2 kg metal cools by 5°C and releases 5000 J. Find its specific heat capacity.
  1. c = Q ÷ (mΔT) = 5000 ÷ (2×5)

Final answer: 500 J/kg°C

Worked example 3 Explain why a metal pan heats quickly.
  1. Mobile delocalised electrons transfer thermal energy rapidly through the metal, alongside lattice vibrations

Final answer: Electron and lattice explanation

Physics

Astrophysics and space

Worked example 1 A star is 4 light-years away. What does this distance mean?
  1. Light-year is the distance light travels in one year; the light observed left the star about four years ago

Final answer: Light has travelled for about four years

Worked example 2 A planet completes 3 orbits in 12 years. Find its orbital period.
  1. Period = total time ÷ number of orbits = 12 ÷ 3

Final answer: 4 years

Worked example 3 Explain why a more massive star can have a shorter stable lifetime.
  1. A high-mass star has a much greater fusion rate and uses its fuel more rapidly

Final answer: Higher fusion rate; faster fuel use

Chemistry

Particles, atoms and formulae

Worked example 1 An atom has 17 protons and 18 neutrons. Find its proton number and mass number.
  1. Proton number = protons = 17
  2. Mass number = protons + neutrons = 17 + 18

Final answer: Proton number 17; mass number 35

Worked example 2 Calculate the relative formula mass of MgCl₂. Ar: Mg=24, Cl=35.5.
  1. Mr = 24 + (2 × 35.5)

Final answer: 95

Worked example 3 An ion has 12 protons and 10 electrons. State its charge.
  1. Charge = 12 positive − 10 negative = +2

Final answer: 2+

Chemistry

Bonding and structure

Worked example 1 Explain why sodium chloride conducts when molten but not solid.
  1. In solid NaCl, ions are fixed in a lattice
  2. When molten, ions are mobile and carry charge

Final answer: Mobile ions only in molten state

Worked example 2 How many shared pairs form a double covalent bond?
  1. A double bond contains two shared pairs of electrons

Final answer: Two shared pairs

Worked example 3 Explain why graphite conducts electricity.
  1. Each carbon forms three covalent bonds; one electron per atom is delocalised and can move along layers

Final answer: Delocalised electrons carry charge

Chemistry

Formulae, equations and the mole

Worked example 1 Find moles in 12 g of carbon. Ar(C)=12.
  1. n = m ÷ Mr = 12 ÷ 12

Final answer: 1 mol

Worked example 2 Calculate mass of 0.25 mol CO₂. Mr=44.
  1. m = n × Mr = 0.25 × 44

Final answer: 11 g

Worked example 3 Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO.
  1. There are two oxygen atoms on the left, so make 2MgO; then balance magnesium with 2Mg

Final answer: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Chemistry

Acids, alkalis, salts and pH

Worked example 1 A solution changes universal indicator to red. What does this suggest?
  1. Red universal indicator corresponds to a strongly acidic solution

Final answer: Acidic; low pH

Worked example 2 Write the word equation for hydrochloric acid reacting with sodium hydroxide.
  1. Acid + alkali → salt + water
  2. The salt is sodium chloride

Final answer: Hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water

Worked example 3 25 cm³ acid is neutralised by 20 cm³ alkali. What is the total mixed volume?
  1. Assuming volumes are additive: 25 + 20

Final answer: 45 cm³

Chemistry

Rates of reaction and collision theory

Worked example 1 A reaction makes 60 cm³ gas in 30 s. Find average rate.
  1. Rate = volume ÷ time = 60 ÷ 30

Final answer: 2 cm³/s

Worked example 2 Explain why powdered calcium carbonate reacts faster than chips.
  1. Powder has a larger total surface area, so more acid particles collide successfully each second

Final answer: Greater surface area; more frequent successful collisions

Worked example 3 State the effect of increasing temperature on reaction rate.
  1. Particles have greater average kinetic energy; collisions are more frequent and a larger fraction exceed activation energy

Final answer: Rate increases

Chemistry

Energetics: exothermic and endothermic reactions

Worked example 1 A reaction releases 25 kJ. Is it exothermic or endothermic?
  1. Energy is transferred to surroundings; ΔH is negative

Final answer: Exothermic; ΔH = −25 kJ

Worked example 2 A fuel transfers 120 kJ usefully from 200 kJ supplied. Find efficiency.
  1. Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input × 100 = 120÷200×100

Final answer: 60%

Worked example 3 On an energy profile, products are lower than reactants. What is the sign of ΔH?
  1. Products have less chemical energy; energy has been released

Final answer: Negative ΔH

Chemistry

Electrolysis and redox

Worked example 1 During electrolysis of molten lead bromide, which ion moves to the cathode?
  1. Positive Pb²⁺ ions are attracted to the negative cathode

Final answer: Pb²⁺

Worked example 2 Define oxidation using electrons.
  1. Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons

Final answer: Loss of electrons

Worked example 3 How many electrons are needed to reduce one Cu²⁺ ion to copper?
  1. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
  2. Two electrons balance the +2 charge

Final answer: 2 electrons

Chemistry

The periodic table and chemical trends

Worked example 1 How many outer-shell electrons does a Group 7 element have?
  1. For main-group halogens, group position indicates seven outer-shell electrons

Final answer: 7

Worked example 2 Explain why Group 1 reactivity increases down the group.
  1. The outer electron is farther from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is lost more easily

Final answer: Lower attraction; easier electron loss

Worked example 3 State the trend in halogen reactivity down Group 7.
  1. Reactivity decreases down the group because gaining an electron becomes less favourable as atomic radius and shielding increase

Final answer: Decreases down the group

Chemistry

Extraction and uses of metals

Worked example 1 Why is aluminium extracted by electrolysis rather than carbon reduction?
  1. Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot readily reduce aluminium oxide

Final answer: Aluminium is above carbon in the reactivity series

Worked example 2 A metal oxide loses oxygen during extraction. What type of reaction is this?
  1. Removal of oxygen is reduction

Final answer: Reduction

Worked example 3 Give one reason copper is used in electrical wiring.
  1. Copper has high electrical conductivity and is ductile, so it can be drawn into wires

Final answer: High conductivity / ductility

Chemistry

Organic chemistry: crude oil, alkanes and alkenes

Worked example 1 Write the molecular formula of an alkane with 5 carbon atoms.
  1. Alkanes follow CnH2n+2
  2. For n=5, H=12

Final answer: C₅H₁₂

Worked example 2 How can bromine water distinguish an alkene from an alkane?
  1. An alkene decolourises orange bromine water because it adds bromine across the C=C bond; an alkane does not under these conditions

Final answer: Alkene: orange to colourless

Worked example 3 Name the process that breaks long-chain hydrocarbons into shorter molecules.
  1. Cracking uses heat and often a catalyst to break C–C bonds

Final answer: Cracking

Chemistry

Chemical analysis and practical skills

Worked example 1 A gas turns limewater milky. Identify it.
  1. Carbon dioxide reacts with calcium hydroxide to form insoluble calcium carbonate

Final answer: Carbon dioxide

Worked example 2 A blue precipitate forms when sodium hydroxide is added to a solution. Which common ion may be present?
  1. Copper(II) ions form blue copper(II) hydroxide precipitate

Final answer: Cu²⁺

Worked example 3 Why rinse a burette with the solution it will contain before a titration?
  1. Rinsing removes water that would dilute the titrant and change its concentration

Final answer: Prevents dilution

Chemistry

Reversible reactions, equilibrium and industrial chemistry

Worked example 1 At dynamic equilibrium, what is true of forward and reverse rates?
  1. In a closed system, both reactions continue at equal rates, so concentrations remain constant

Final answer: Forward rate = reverse rate

Worked example 2 For an exothermic forward reaction, what does lowering temperature do to equilibrium yield?
  1. Lower temperature favours the exothermic direction, so equilibrium shifts towards products

Final answer: Product yield increases

Worked example 3 In the Haber process, what is the effect of increasing pressure?
  1. The equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules; nitrogen + 3 hydrogen gives 2 ammonia

Final answer: Shifts towards ammonia

Biology

Cell structure and organisation

Worked example 1 A cell image is 40 mm long and the actual cell is 0.08 mm. Calculate magnification.
  1. Magnification = image size ÷ actual size = 40 ÷ 0.08

Final answer: ×500

Worked example 2 Name the organelle where aerobic respiration releases most ATP.
  1. Mitochondria are the main site of aerobic respiration

Final answer: Mitochondrion

Worked example 3 Explain why a root hair cell has a long extension.
  1. The extension increases surface area for absorption of water and mineral ions from soil

Final answer: Large surface area for absorption

Biology

Movement of substances

Worked example 1 Define diffusion.
  1. Net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration, down a concentration gradient

Final answer: Net movement down concentration gradient

Worked example 2 A plant cell is placed in a concentrated sugar solution. Predict the effect.
  1. Water leaves by osmosis through the partially permeable membrane; the cell becomes flaccid/plasmolysed

Final answer: Water leaves; cell loses turgor

Worked example 3 Explain why active transport needs energy.
  1. It moves substances against a concentration gradient using carrier proteins and energy from respiration

Final answer: ATP/energy for movement against gradient

Biology

Biological molecules and enzymes

Worked example 1 An enzyme works fastest at 37°C but slows at 70°C. Explain.
  1. High temperature disrupts bonds maintaining enzyme shape; the active site changes and substrate no longer fits

Final answer: Enzyme denatures

Worked example 2 A food test turns iodine solution blue-black. Which molecule is present?
  1. Iodine gives a blue-black colour with starch

Final answer: Starch

Worked example 3 Explain enzyme specificity.
  1. The active site has a complementary shape and chemical properties to its substrate

Final answer: Specific active-site/substrate fit

Biology

Nutrition, digestion and absorption

Worked example 1 Which enzyme breaks starch into maltose?
  1. Amylase hydrolyses starch into smaller sugars, including maltose

Final answer: Amylase

Worked example 2 Explain how villi are adapted for absorption.
  1. Villi provide large surface area, thin epithelium, rich blood supply and lacteals for lipid absorption

Final answer: Any two explained adaptations

Worked example 3 Why is bile important in fat digestion?
  1. Bile emulsifies large fat globules into smaller droplets, increasing surface area for lipase

Final answer: Emulsification; greater surface area

Biology

Respiration and gas exchange

Worked example 1 Write the word equation for aerobic respiration.
  1. Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

Final answer: Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

Worked example 2 Explain why alveoli have thin walls.
  1. A short diffusion distance allows oxygen and carbon dioxide to diffuse rapidly

Final answer: Short diffusion distance

Worked example 3 A person breathes 15 times per minute with tidal volume 0.5 dm³. Find minute ventilation.
  1. Minute ventilation = breathing rate × tidal volume = 15 × 0.5

Final answer: 7.5 dm³/min

Biology

Transport in plants and animals

Worked example 1 Which tissue transports water and mineral ions from roots to leaves?
  1. Xylem vessels conduct water and dissolved mineral ions upwards

Final answer: Xylem

Worked example 2 Explain why a plant wilts when water loss exceeds uptake.
  1. Cells lose water by osmosis, turgor pressure falls, and tissues become flaccid

Final answer: Loss of turgor

Worked example 3 Name the blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood from lungs to heart.
  1. Pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood to the left atrium

Final answer: Pulmonary vein

Biology

Coordination, response and homeostasis

Worked example 1 State the role of a receptor in a reflex arc.
  1. A receptor detects a stimulus and initiates a nerve impulse

Final answer: Detects stimulus

Worked example 2 Explain insulin's role after blood glucose rises.
  1. Pancreatic beta cells release insulin; body cells take up glucose and liver/muscles store glucose as glycogen

Final answer: Blood glucose falls

Worked example 3 Why does sweating cool the body?
  1. Evaporation of sweat requires energy, which is taken from the skin, lowering skin temperature

Final answer: Evaporation removes thermal energy

Biology

Inheritance, variation and evolution

Worked example 1 Cross two heterozygous tall plants Tt × Tt. Find the genotype ratio.
  1. Punnett outcomes: TT, Tt, Tt, tt

Final answer: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt

Worked example 2 If a recessive disorder allele is a, what genotype must an affected individual have?
  1. A recessive phenotype appears only when both alleles are recessive

Final answer: aa

Worked example 3 Explain natural selection in a population exposed to a new predator.
  1. Individuals with advantageous inherited variation survive and reproduce more; allele frequency rises over generations

Final answer: Differential survival and reproduction

Biology

Ecology, ecosystems and human impacts

Worked example 1 A food chain transfers 10 000 kJ to producers. If 10% passes to primary consumers, how much is transferred?
  1. 10% × 10 000 = 1000 kJ

Final answer: 1000 kJ

Worked example 2 Explain why energy is lost between trophic levels.
  1. Energy is lost in respiration as heat, movement, waste and uneaten material; not all biomass is consumed or digested

Final answer: Examples of energy loss

Worked example 3 A quadrat of 0.25 m² contains 8 daisies. Estimate density per m².
  1. Density = 8 ÷ 0.25

Final answer: 32 daisies/m²

Biology

Practical biology, data and scientific evaluation

Worked example 1 Why repeat measurements in an investigation?
  1. Repeats help identify anomalies and allow a mean to be calculated, improving reliability

Final answer: Identify anomalies; calculate mean

Worked example 2 A student records 12, 13, 12, 25 and 13. Identify the likely anomaly.
  1. 25 is far from the cluster 12–13 and should be checked before analysis

Final answer: 25

Worked example 3 State one way to improve validity when testing light intensity on photosynthesis.
  1. Keep temperature, carbon dioxide concentration, plant species and exposure time controlled while changing only light intensity

Final answer: Control relevant variables
