Chapter 2: Movement of substances

Substances cross cell membranes by diffusion, osmosis and active transport. Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, due to random motion.

Core explanation

Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane from a dilute solution (higher water potential) to a more concentrated solution (lower water potential). Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration and carrier proteins.

Worked example / application

Factors affecting diffusion include concentration gradient, temperature, surface area and diffusion distance. Large organisms need specialised exchange surfaces with a large surface area, thin barriers and good transport systems.

Key learning point

Use precise scientific or mathematical vocabulary, show the reasoning behind each step, and connect the concept to the relevant principle rather than memorising isolated facts.

Chapter self-check

Define diffusion and osmosis precisely. Explain why a large surface-area-to-volume ratio benefits exchange. Predict what happens to a plant cell in a concentrated salt solution.

Further textbook notes: membrane transport

Diffusion is the net movement of particles down a concentration gradient due to random motion. Its rate increases with a steeper gradient, higher temperature and larger surface area, and decreases with a longer diffusion distance. Osmosis is the net movement of water through a partially permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.

Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration and carrier proteins. It is important in mineral ion uptake by root hair cells and glucose absorption in certain tissues. Plant cells may become turgid, flaccid or plasmolysed depending on water movement.

Practise: Predict changes in plant and animal cells placed in dilute and concentrated solutions, and explain why root hair cells need active transport.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: Water moves by osmosis from a dilute solution with higher water potential to a more concentrated solution with lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane.
Exam-style question (3 marks)

A plant cell is placed in a concentrated sugar solution. Explain what happens.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Water leaves the cell by osmosis (1); vacuole/cytoplasm volume decreases (1); membrane pulls away from cell wall/plasmolysis may occur (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Name osmosis and state the direction of water movement.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

A red blood cell is placed in distilled water. Predict what happens and explain why.

Show answer and mark scheme

Water enters by osmosis (1); distilled water has a higher water potential than the cell cytoplasm (1); the cell swells and may burst because it has no cell wall (1).
