Chapter 6: Transport in plants and animals

Transport systems move materials between exchange surfaces and cells. Mammalian blood contains plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. Red blood cells contain haemoglobin, which binds oxygen reversibly.

Core explanation

The heart is a double pump. The right side sends deoxygenated blood to the lungs; the left side sends oxygenated blood to the body. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return blood, and capillaries allow exchange with tissues.

Worked example / application

In plants, xylem transports water and mineral ions from roots to leaves and also provides support. Phloem translocates assimilates such as sucrose between sources and sinks. Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from leaves, mainly through stomata.

Key learning point

Use precise scientific or mathematical vocabulary, show the reasoning behind each step, and connect the concept to the relevant principle rather than memorising isolated facts.

Chapter self-check

Compare arteries, veins and capillaries. Explain how haemoglobin supports oxygen transport. Distinguish xylem transport from phloem translocation.

Further textbook notes: transport in plants and mammals

Xylem vessels transport water and mineral ions from roots to leaves and provide support. Transpiration is evaporation of water from leaf surfaces followed by diffusion of water vapour through stomata. It creates a pull that draws water up the xylem. Transpiration rate is affected by light intensity, temperature, humidity and air movement.

Phloem translocates dissolved sugars and other organic substances between sources and sinks. Mammalian double circulation sends blood from the heart to lungs and back, then to the body and back. Red blood cells carry oxygen using haemoglobin; white blood cells defend against pathogens; platelets help blood clot.

Practise: Trace blood through the heart and explain how xylem and phloem differ in structure and function.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: Blood travels right atrium→right ventricle→pulmonary artery→lungs→pulmonary vein→left atrium→left ventricle→aorta. Xylem transports water; phloem transports assimilates.
Exam-style question (3 marks)

Describe the route of blood from the right ventricle to the left atrium.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Right ventricle pumps blood into pulmonary artery (1); blood passes through lung capillaries for gas exchange (1); returns through pulmonary vein to left atrium (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Do not confuse pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein; name is based on direction relative to heart.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

Explain how the structure of a xylem vessel supports its function.

Show answer and mark scheme

Hollow tube with no end walls allows continuous water flow (1); lignified walls strengthen and prevent collapse (1); pits allow sideways movement of water (1).
