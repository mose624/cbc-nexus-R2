Chapter 10: Practical biology, data and scientific evaluation

Biology practical work requires a clear question, hypothesis, independent variable, dependent variable and controlled variables. A valid method changes one independent variable while controlling other factors that could affect the result.

Core explanation

Use suitable apparatus, repeat measurements, calculate means and identify anomalous results using a stated rule. Tables need headings and units. Graphs should use appropriate scales, labelled axes and a best-fit line or curve where suitable. Conclusions must be supported by data.

Worked example / application

Reliability is improved by repetition and consistent technique. Validity depends on whether the method measures what it claims to measure and controls relevant variables. Evaluate limitations and suggest realistic improvements, such as a more precise instrument or a larger sample.

Key learning point

Use precise scientific or mathematical vocabulary, show the reasoning behind each step, and connect the concept to the relevant principle rather than memorising isolated facts.

Chapter self-check

Plan an investigation of light intensity and photosynthesis. Identify variables and safety precautions. Explain the difference between accuracy, reliability and validity.

Further textbook notes: practical planning and evaluation

A strong investigation begins with a focused question and a testable hypothesis. Identify the independent variable to change, dependent variable to measure and control variables to keep constant. Select suitable apparatus and a range of values; repeat each measurement to identify anomalies and calculate a mean when appropriate.

Reliability concerns consistency; validity concerns whether the method measures what it intends to measure. Accuracy concerns closeness to the true value. Improve reliability with repeats, validity by controlling variables and accuracy by using suitable calibrated equipment. Present data in a table with headings and units, then choose an appropriate graph.

Practise: Evaluate a practical method by identifying a limitation, its effect on results and a realistic improvement.

Worked example, exam-style question and marking guidance

Worked example: A valid investigation changes one independent variable, measures the dependent variable and controls other factors. Repeats help identify anomalies and calculate a mean.
Exam-style question (4 marks)

A student tests how light intensity affects photosynthesis. State one variable to measure, two controls and one way to improve reliability.

Show model answer and mark allocation

Measure oxygen bubbles/volume of oxygen per unit time (1); control temperature (1) and carbon dioxide concentration or plant species/length (1); repeat at each light intensity and calculate mean (1).

Examiner’s explanation: Bubbles are a less precise measure than collected gas volume; an improvement may mention a gas syringe or oxygen sensor.

Exam technique: Show each formula, substitution, calculation and unit. For “explain” questions, connect the scientific idea to the outcome; for “describe” questions, give the relevant sequence or features.

Extra chapter practice question (3 marks)

A student investigates the effect of light intensity on photosynthesis using pondweed. State the independent variable, dependent variable and two control variables.

Show answer and mark scheme

Independent variable = light intensity/distance from lamp (1); dependent = oxygen bubbles per minute/oxygen volume (1); controls include temperature and carbon dioxide concentration or pondweed length/species (1).
