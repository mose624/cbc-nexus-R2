International standards: expanded guidance

Understand ranking methodologies

QS and Times Higher Education publish influential global university comparisons, but their indicators and weightings differ. QS includes academic and employer reputation, research citations, faculty-student ratio, international engagement and sustainability. THE assesses teaching, research environment, research quality, international outlook and industry. Rankings can therefore place the same institution differently.

Compare by subject and programme

Check the specific department, course content, laboratory or clinical facilities, research groups, teaching approach and professional accreditation. A university’s overall rank does not guarantee that every programme is equally strong. For regulated careers, verify recognition by the relevant professional body in the country where you plan to work.

Verify recognition and admissions

Confirm the institution is recognised by the national higher-education authority and that the specific programme is approved where required. Review entry requirements, language tests, deadlines, portfolios, interviews and credit-transfer rules. Contact admissions offices for written clarification.

Compare cost, funding and student support

Calculate tuition, accommodation, insurance, travel, food, transport and visa costs. Review scholarships and assistantships. Compare academic advising, disability support, counselling, internships, graduate outcomes and international student services.

Build a balanced shortlist

Compare several universities using the same criteria: subject fit, recognition, total cost, funding, student experience and career outcomes. Record the ranking edition and methodology date, because tables change annually.

Current ranking sources: QS World University Rankings 2026, QS methodology and THE methodology.

Additional practical guidance - CBE Nexus

Why “best university” depends on your subject and goals

A university can be highly ranked overall but may not be the strongest option for your intended subject, budget, learning style or career plan. The best choice is an institution that offers a recognised programme, strong teaching and support in your field, affordable total costs and a realistic route to your goals. Compare the programme itself rather than choosing only by a famous name.

Understand the ranking systems

QS, Times Higher Education (THE) and the Academic Ranking of World Universities use different methods. QS considers indicators such as academic and employer reputation, citations, internationalisation, faculty-student ratio and sustainability. THE evaluates research-intensive universities across teaching, research environment, research quality, international outlook and industry. Because their weightings differ, the same institution can occupy different positions in different rankings. Read each ranking's methodology and subject tables before drawing conclusions.

Compare the actual course

Review compulsory modules, optional subjects, laboratory or studio facilities, research groups, internships, teaching methods, assessment and placement opportunities. Look at the academic staff and recent work in your area. For professional courses, confirm whether the programme is recognised by the professional body in the country where you intend to work. A high overall ranking does not mean every programme is accredited or suitable for every career.

Verify recognition and admission requirements

Confirm that the institution is legally recognised in its country and that the programme meets any professional requirements relevant to your career. Check admission grades, required subjects, language tests, portfolios, interviews and deadlines on the university's official website. Contact the admissions office when a requirement is unclear. Be cautious of agents who promise guaranteed admission or scholarships.

Calculate total costs and build a balanced shortlist

Include tuition, accommodation, food, transport, health insurance, books, visa fees and travel. Check whether international students pay different fees and whether tuition increases annually. Compare scholarships, research assistantships and external funding. Build a shortlist of ambitious, realistic and safer options. For each university, record the programme, recognition, admission requirements, total cost, funding, language of instruction and application deadline.

Official ranking sources: QS World University Rankings; QS methodology; THE methodology.
