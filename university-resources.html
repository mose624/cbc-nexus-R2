01 · Undergraduate Notes

Build strong foundations with structured topic notes, definitions, principles, worked examples, diagrams, practice exercises and revision questions. Resources should identify the expected level and prerequisite knowledge.

02 · Advanced Undergraduate Notes

Explore upper-level modules, mathematical derivations, laboratory and computational methods, case studies, proofs and complex problem-solving. Content should move beyond introductory summaries.

03 · Master's Degree Resources

Graduate resources should emphasise critical evaluation, advanced methods, specialist literature, independent analysis, applied projects and rigorous assessment tasks.

04 · Doctoral and PhD Resources

Doctoral learning support covers advanced theoretical frameworks, research methodology, scholarly argument, reproducibility, research ethics and original contribution. These resources support study and do not replace supervision.

05 · University Mathematics

Explore pure mathematics, calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, probability, statistics, numerical analysis, optimisation and applied or specialist mathematics.

06 · University Science

Develop university-level understanding in biology, chemistry, physics and related sciences through concept explanations, equations, laboratory principles, diagrams, data interpretation and problem sets.

07 · Engineering and Technology

Cover engineering mathematics, mechanics, materials, electrical and electronic systems, computing, programming, data systems, design, modelling and technology applications.

08 · Business and Economics

Provide structured learning in microeconomics, macroeconomics, accounting, corporate finance, marketing, management, econometrics, entrepreneurship and quantitative decision-making.

09 · Medicine and Health Sciences

Support foundational study in anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, microbiology, pharmacology principles, public health and health research. Clinical decisions and professional practice must follow accredited training and current clinical guidance.

10 · Law and Social Sciences

Include legal principles, jurisprudence, constitutional systems, international law, political science, sociology, psychology, anthropology and research methods. Jurisdiction-specific legal content must clearly identify the relevant country.

11 · University Examination Papers

Provide original, level-appropriate practice papers with clear instructions, marks, realistic time limits, answer space, worked solutions and marking guidance. Papers must not be presented as official past papers unless their source and permission are established.

12 · Research and Dissertation Resources

Cover research questions, literature reviews, research design, qualitative and quantitative methods, sampling, statistics, data visualisation, referencing, research ethics, thesis structure and academic presentation.

Academic quality and publishing principles

  • Write original academic notes rather than copying university textbooks.
  • Follow internationally recognised subject content and learning outcomes.
  • Clearly distinguish undergraduate, Master's and doctoral difficulty.
  • Include real calculations, diagrams, worked solutions and challenging assessments.
  • Avoid claiming that materials are officially endorsed by a university unless they are.
  • Make the resources accessible regardless of the learner's country.
Academic integrity: CBE Nexus learning materials are independent educational resources. They are not official university publications, and learners should consult their own institution's syllabus, lecturers and assessment rules.
