How to study a medical image

Identify modality→Orient the image→Describe objective findings→Relate to anatomy→Interpret in context→State limitations

Describe before diagnosing. Record the modality, plane, orientation, anatomical region, image quality and visible structures. Separate observations from interpretation. Correlate with clinical history and other investigations; a teaching image alone is not a patient diagnosis.

Image licensing and patient privacy

Do not copy images simply because they are visible online. Each image must have a verified licence or written permission that covers the intended use, including commercial use on CBE Nexus. Record the creator, title, source, licence, attribution text and any modification. CC BY-NC material is not suitable for commercial use without additional permission; CC BY-ND restricts adaptations. MedPix and other collections may restrict redistribution beyond personal or local teaching.

Never upload identifiable patient photographs, scans, pathology reports or metadata without appropriate lawful authority, consent where required, de-identification and institutional approval. Remove names, dates of birth, record numbers, facial identifiers and embedded DICOM metadata. Use synthetic or properly licensed educational cases wherever possible.

Image interpretation checklist

  1. Name the modality: photograph, gross specimen, H&E histology, special stain, radiograph, ultrasound, CT, MRI or another technique.
  2. State orientation, plane, anatomical region and relevant landmarks.
  3. Describe visible structures and abnormalities without jumping to a diagnosis.
  4. Use correct anatomical, histological and pathological terminology.
  5. Explain the biological mechanism that could produce the observed appearance.
  6. Correlate with symptoms, examination, laboratory data and differential diagnoses.
  7. Discuss image quality, artefacts, limitations and what cannot be concluded.
  8. Provide source attribution and licence information for every third-party image.

Academic reference and safety note

Use current peer-reviewed literature, standard textbooks, institutional teaching and national clinical guidance alongside these image collections. This atlas is for education and does not replace a radiologist’s report, histopathology diagnosis, clinical assessment or supervised medical training.
