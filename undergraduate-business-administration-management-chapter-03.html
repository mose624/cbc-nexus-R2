Chapter overview and learning outcomes

Demand and supply, markets, competition, macroeconomic indicators, trade, globalisation, policy and business cycles.

At the end of this chapter, a student should be able to define and explain the central terminology; describe the main principles and their relationships; apply an appropriate method to a structured problem; interpret evidence and communicate a reasoned conclusion; and recognise limitations, context and professional responsibilities.

  • Explain the foundational concepts and vocabulary of the subject.
  • Analyse how the main ideas connect to other areas of the programme.
  • Apply concepts to a realistic case, calculation, lesson, project or professional scenario.
  • Evaluate alternative approaches using appropriate evidence and stated assumptions.
  • Present findings clearly using suitable diagrams, tables, calculations or written arguments.

Worked learning example

Scenario: A student or professional team is asked to address a realistic problem related to business economics and the global business environment. Before selecting a solution, define the problem, identify the people or systems affected, gather relevant information and state the assumptions.

  1. Frame the task: Write a precise problem statement and identify the expected output.
  2. Select a method: Choose a theory, analytical procedure or planning framework from the chapter and justify the choice.
  3. Apply it: Show the stages of the reasoning or calculation. Keep units, definitions, data sources and intermediate steps visible where relevant.
  4. Check the result: Test consistency, consider an alternative explanation and identify uncertainty or missing information.
  5. Communicate: Present the conclusion, limitations and a practical recommendation.

Professional context: Business decisions should be supported by evidence, transparent assumptions, ethical conduct and awareness of local law and market conditions.

Practical activities and independent study

  1. Create a one-page concept map showing how the chapter's key terms connect.
  2. Prepare a glossary of at least 15 important terms, each with a definition and example.
  3. Find a credible case study or dataset relevant to this topic and summarise its context and limitations.
  4. Complete a small practical, analytical, observation or planning task and keep a record of your method.
  5. Write a reflective paragraph identifying what you understand well and what requires further reading or tutor guidance.

Revision and examination questions

Attempt these questions independently before consulting lecture materials. Support answers with definitions, examples, diagrams and clearly explained reasoning.

  1. Define the central concepts in business economics and the global business environment and explain why they matter in professional practice.
  2. Distinguish between the major theories, methods or systems introduced in this chapter.
  3. Explain the relationship between the chapter's key principles and the wider discipline.
  4. Describe the assumptions that should be stated before applying the methods in this chapter.
  5. Develop a labelled diagram or process map that explains one major concept.
  6. Work through a numerical, analytical or planning example relevant to this subject, showing each step.
  7. Identify common errors or misconceptions and explain how they can be prevented.
  8. Apply the chapter concepts to a realistic case from a different country or institutional context.
  9. Compare two approaches and evaluate their strengths, limitations and appropriate use cases.
  10. Explain how evidence, data or observations can be used to support a defensible conclusion.
  11. Discuss relevant ethical, legal, safety or professional responsibilities.
  12. Identify risks, uncertainties and limitations that may affect a real-world application.
  13. Propose a practical activity or investigation that could deepen understanding of this topic.
  14. Write a structured short essay connecting at least three major ideas from the chapter.
  15. Design a small project, case analysis or portfolio task demonstrating the learning outcomes.

Chapter summary and further reading

The central learning task is to connect accurate subject knowledge with sound reasoning and appropriate application. Review the chapter outcomes, revise unfamiliar terminology, practise the worked method and use feedback to improve your explanation. Consult the current prescribed textbook, university reading list, relevant professional standards and authoritative institutional guidance for deeper study.
