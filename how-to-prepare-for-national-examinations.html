How Do I Prepare for National Examinations?

Begin by mapping the official syllabus and examination format. Mark each topic as confident, developing or needing support. Create a weekly timetable with short study blocks, breaks and time for sleep. Use active recall: close your notes and explain a concept or answer questions from memory.

Practise and review

Practise legitimate past papers under timed conditions, show your working and review every error. Ask teachers for help with repeated difficulties and check the official examination body's current instructions. Avoid relying on rumours or leaked papers. Regular revision is more effective than last-minute cramming.

International standards: expanded guidance

Use the current official syllabus and examination guidance

Get the syllabus, assessment specification, candidate instructions and timetable from the responsible examination authority. Identify topics, practical components, question formats, permitted materials and any updates. Make sure practice papers match the current syllabus rather than an older examination cycle.

Diagnose strengths and plan revision

Attempt a short set of questions in each subject and classify topics as secure, developing or requiring support. Build a weekly timetable with short focused sessions, subject rotation, breaks and time for feedback. Give extra attention to foundational gaps and difficult topics instead of simply rereading everything.

Use evidence-informed study methods

Practise active recall by closing notes and explaining concepts from memory. Space revision over several days and mix older topics with new ones. Use worked examples, flashcards, diagrams and self-testing. Rereading and highlighting can support learning, but should not be the only study method.

Practise timed questions and learn from mistakes

Use official past papers and sample assessments when available. Practise interpreting command words, showing working and allocating time. Mark answers against official mark schemes where provided. Record recurring errors and redo similar questions after a gap. Do not rely on leaked papers, predicted topics or memorised answers alone.

Protect wellbeing and prepare for exam day

Keep a consistent sleep schedule, take movement breaks and avoid last-minute all-night cramming. Ask a teacher or counsellor for help if anxiety becomes overwhelming. On the day, read instructions carefully, plan time, check your work and follow the examination authority’s rules.

Official starting point for Kenya: Kenya National Examinations Council. Other countries should use their own examination board’s current guidance.
