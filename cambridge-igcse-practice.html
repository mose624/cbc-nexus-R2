Biology — Original Practice

1. Cell structure [4 marks]

(a) Name the cell structure that controls the activities of a cell. (1)
(b) State two structures found in a plant cell but not in a typical animal cell. (2)
(c) Explain the function of the cell membrane. (1)

Show answer and marking guidance

(a) Nucleus. (1)
(b) Any two: cell wall, chloroplast, large permanent vacuole. (1 each)
(c) Controls movement of substances into and out of the cell; it is selectively permeable. (1)

2. Enzymes [5 marks]

A student investigates the effect of temperature on an enzyme. Explain why the rate of enzyme activity increases as temperature rises towards the optimum, and why it decreases rapidly above the optimum.

Show answer and marking guidance

As temperature rises, particles gain kinetic energy and collide more frequently, so more enzyme-substrate complexes form. Above the optimum, bonds maintaining the enzyme's shape are disrupted; the active site changes shape (denaturation), so the substrate is less complementary and fewer complexes form. Award up to 5 marks for these linked points.

3. Photosynthesis [4 marks]

(a) Write the word equation for photosynthesis. (2)
(b) State two factors that can limit the rate of photosynthesis. (2)

Show answer and marking guidance

(a) Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, in the presence of light and chlorophyll. (2)
(b) Any two: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature, chlorophyll availability. (1 each)

Chemistry — Original Practice

1. Atomic structure [4 marks]

An atom has proton number 17 and nucleon number 35. Determine its number of (a) protons, (b) neutrons and (c) electrons, assuming the atom is neutral.

Show answer and marking guidance

(a) 17 protons. (1)
(b) 35 − 17 = 18 neutrons. (1)
(c) 17 electrons because a neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons. (2)

2. Bonding [4 marks]

Explain why sodium chloride has a high melting point and why it conducts electricity when molten but not when solid.

Show answer and marking guidance

It has a giant ionic lattice (1) with strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions (1), requiring much energy to overcome. When molten, ions are mobile and carry charge (1); in the solid lattice, ions are fixed in position and cannot move (1).

3. Acids and bases [4 marks]

Hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide. (a) Name the type of reaction. (1) (b) Write the balanced symbol equation. (2) (c) Name the salt formed. (1)

Show answer and marking guidance

(a) Neutralisation. (1)
(b) HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. (2)
(c) Sodium chloride. (1)

Physics — Original Practice

1. Speed and acceleration [4 marks]

A car increases its velocity uniformly from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 seconds. Calculate its acceleration and state the equation used.

Show answer and marking guidance

Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time (1). a = (25 − 5) ÷ 4 (1) = 5 m/s² (2).

2. Electrical circuits [4 marks]

A 6 Ω resistor is connected to a 12 V supply. Calculate the current through the resistor and the electrical power dissipated.

Show answer and marking guidance

Using V = IR: I = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A (2). Power P = VI = 12 × 2 = 24 W (2).

3. Density [3 marks]

A metal block has a mass of 540 g and a volume of 200 cm³. Calculate its density in g/cm³.

Show answer and marking guidance

Density = mass ÷ volume (1) = 540 ÷ 200 (1) = 2.7 g/cm³ (1).

Mathematics — Original Practice

1. Algebra [3 marks]

Solve the equation 3(2x − 1) = 4x + 9. Show your working.

Show answer and marking guidance

6x − 3 = 4x + 9 (1); 2x = 12 (1); x = 6 (1).

2. Percentage [3 marks]

A laptop costs KSh 48,000. Its price is reduced by 15%. Calculate the sale price.

Show answer and marking guidance

Discount = 0.15 × 48,000 = KSh 7,200 (2). Sale price = 48,000 − 7,200 = KSh 40,800 (1).

3. Pythagoras' theorem [3 marks]

A right-angled triangle has perpendicular sides of 6 cm and 8 cm. Calculate the length of the hypotenuse.

Show answer and marking guidance

c² = 6² + 8² (1) = 36 + 64 = 100 (1), so c = 10 cm (1).

Biology — 0610 · 21 syllabus areas

1. Characteristics and classification [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A microorganism has no nucleus and its genetic material is free in the cytoplasm. Identify its cell type and give one other characteristic.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

It is a prokaryote. It has no membrane-bound organelles / has a cell wall made of peptidoglycan / is generally smaller than eukaryotic cells. [2]

2. Organisation of the organism [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Arrange these levels of organisation from simplest to most complex: organ, cell, organ system, tissue. Define a tissue.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Cell → tissue → organ → organ system. A tissue is a group of similar cells working together to perform a particular function. [2]

3. Movement into and out of cells [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A plant cell is placed in a concentrated sucrose solution. Describe the net movement of water and explain the resulting change to the cell.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Water moves out by osmosis, from higher water potential inside to lower water potential outside, through the partially permeable cell membrane. The vacuole/cytoplasm shrinks and the membrane pulls away from the cell wall (plasmolysis). [3]

4. Biological molecules [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A food sample turns blue-black when iodine solution is added. Identify the nutrient and describe one biological role of it.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Starch. It is a carbohydrate used as an energy store in plants; it can be hydrolysed to glucose for respiration. [2]

5. Enzymes [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain why an enzyme-catalysed reaction may stop when the substrate concentration is increased no further but enzyme concentration remains constant.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

At sufficiently high substrate concentration, all enzyme active sites are occupied; enzyme concentration becomes the limiting factor and the rate reaches a maximum. [2]

6. Plant nutrition [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A plant is kept in darkness for 48 hours and then one leaf is partly covered before exposure to light. Describe the test and expected result for starch.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Boil leaf in water, heat in ethanol in a water bath to remove chlorophyll, rinse, then add iodine. Exposed regions turn blue-black; covered region remains yellow-brown, showing light is needed for starch production. [3]

7. Human nutrition [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain how the structure of a small-intestine villus is adapted for absorption.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Large surface area from villi/microvilli; epithelium one cell thick for short diffusion distance; rich capillary network maintains concentration gradient; lacteal absorbs fatty acids/glycerol. [3]

8. Transport in plants [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain how a high rate of transpiration can increase the movement of water from roots to leaves.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Evaporation from mesophyll and diffusion of water vapour out through stomata lowers water potential in leaves, creating tension in xylem; cohesion between water molecules maintains a continuous column pulled upwards. [3]

9. Transport in animals [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

State two differences between arteries and veins and explain how each difference relates to its function.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Arteries have thicker muscular/elastic walls to withstand high pressure; narrower lumen helps maintain pressure. Veins have a wider lumen and valves to reduce resistance/prevent backflow at low pressure. [3]

10. Diseases and immunity [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain how vaccination can reduce the spread of an infectious disease in a population.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

A harmless form/antigen stimulates lymphocytes to produce specific antibodies and memory cells. On later exposure, memory cells produce antibodies rapidly, reducing infection/transmission; high coverage reduces susceptible hosts. [3]

11. Gas exchange in humans [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain why the alveoli are efficient gas-exchange surfaces.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Many alveoli provide large surface area; walls are one cell thick; moist lining allows gases to dissolve; dense capillary supply and ventilation maintain steep concentration gradients. [3]

12. Respiration [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Compare aerobic respiration with anaerobic respiration in human muscle cells during vigorous exercise.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and completely breaks down glucose to carbon dioxide and water, releasing much energy. Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen, produces lactic acid and releases less energy. [3]

13. Excretion in humans [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Describe how the kidney helps maintain water balance when a person is dehydrated.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

More ADH is released; collecting ducts become more permeable to water; more water is reabsorbed into blood; a smaller volume of concentrated urine is produced. [3]

14. Coordination and response [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A person touches a hot surface and withdraws their hand. Outline the reflex arc.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Receptor detects stimulus; sensory neurone carries impulse to CNS; relay neurone connects to motor neurone; motor neurone carries impulse to effector muscle; muscle contracts and withdraws hand. [4]

15. Drugs [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain one harmful effect of smoking on gas exchange and one effect on the circulatory system.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Tar damages/cilia and alveolar walls, reducing surface area and gas exchange; carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin, reducing oxygen transport; nicotine increases heart rate/blood pressure. [2]

16. Reproduction [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain the roles of the placenta and umbilical cord during human pregnancy.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Placenta allows exchange of oxygen/nutrients from maternal blood to fetal blood and carbon dioxide/urea in the opposite direction without normally mixing the blood; umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood to fetus and arteries return deoxygenated blood. [3]

17. Inheritance [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

In a cross between two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa), determine the probability of offspring showing the recessive phenotype.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Genotypes: AA, Aa, Aa, aa. Only aa shows recessive phenotype, so probability is 1/4 or 25%. [2]

18. Variation and selection [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A population of insects contains individuals with different resistance to an insecticide. Explain how repeated insecticide use can increase the frequency of resistance.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Some insects have inherited resistance due to genetic variation; insecticide kills more non-resistant insects; resistant individuals survive and reproduce, passing alleles to offspring; resistance allele frequency rises over generations. [3]

19. Organisms and their environment [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain how energy is transferred and lost along a food chain, and why food chains are usually short.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Energy passes in biomass between trophic levels. Energy is lost through respiration/heat, movement, waste and uneaten material; less energy is available at each level, limiting chain length. [3]

20. Human influences on ecosystems [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Describe two ways deforestation can affect ecosystems and the carbon cycle.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Habitat loss reduces biodiversity; soil erosion increases; fewer trees remove carbon dioxide by photosynthesis, while burning/decay releases carbon dioxide, increasing atmospheric concentration. [3]

21. Biotechnology and genetic modification [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Outline the main stages used to transfer a useful human gene into bacteria to produce a protein.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Identify and isolate the gene; cut gene and bacterial plasmid with the same restriction enzyme; join using DNA ligase; insert recombinant plasmid into bacteria; select transformed bacteria and culture them to express the gene. [4]

Chemistry — 0620 · 12 syllabus areas

1. States of matter [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Use particle theory to explain why a gas can be compressed much more easily than a liquid.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Gas particles are far apart with large spaces between them, so they can be forced closer together. Liquid particles are already close-packed with little empty space. [2]

2. Atoms, elements and compounds [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

An ion has 13 protons and 10 electrons. State its charge and explain your answer.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

3+; it has three more protons than electrons, giving a net positive charge of three. [2]

3. Stoichiometry [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Calculate the relative formula mass of Ca(OH)₂. (Ar: Ca=40, O=16, H=1).

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Mr = 40 + 2(16 + 1) = 40 + 34 = 74. [2]

4. Electrochemistry [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

During electrolysis of molten lead(II) bromide, name the product at each electrode and explain how it forms.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Cathode: lead; Pb²⁺ gains two electrons (reduction). Anode: bromine; Br⁻ loses electrons (oxidation), forming Br₂. [3]

5. Chemical energetics [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A reaction mixture becomes colder during a reaction. State whether the reaction is endothermic or exothermic and explain the energy transfer.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Endothermic; the reaction takes in energy from the surroundings, so the surroundings/mixture temperature falls. [2]

6. Chemical reactions [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain how increasing temperature usually increases the rate of a reaction between particles.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Particles have greater kinetic energy; collisions occur more frequently and a greater proportion have energy equal to or above activation energy, so more successful collisions occur per second. [3]

7. Acids, bases and salts [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Describe how to prepare pure, dry crystals of copper(II) sulfate from dilute sulfuric acid and excess copper(II) oxide.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Warm acid; add copper(II) oxide until in excess; filter off unreacted solid; heat filtrate to concentrate; cool to crystallise; filter and dry crystals. [4]

8. The Periodic Table [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain why elements in the same group of the Periodic Table have similar chemical properties.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

They have the same number of electrons in their outer shell, so they have similar tendencies to lose, gain or share electrons and form similar ions/bonds. [2]

9. Metals [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain why aluminium is extracted by electrolysis rather than by reduction with carbon.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Aluminium is more reactive than carbon and its oxide is too stable to be reduced by carbon; electrolysis is used to remove oxygen from molten aluminium oxide. [2]

10. Chemistry of the environment [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain how sulfur dioxide released from burning fossil fuels can damage the environment.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

It dissolves in rainwater and forms acidic solutions, contributing to acid rain; this acidifies lakes/soils, harms aquatic organisms and damages vegetation/buildings. [2]

11. Organic chemistry [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Describe the test that distinguishes an alkene from an alkane using bromine water.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Shake each sample with bromine water. An alkene decolourises orange/brown bromine water to colourless by addition across the C=C bond; an alkane shows no change in these conditions. [2]

12. Experimental techniques and chemical analysis [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A student is given a mixture of sand and sodium chloride. Describe how to obtain dry sodium chloride from the mixture.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Add water to dissolve sodium chloride; filter to remove insoluble sand; evaporate some water; allow solution to crystallise; filter and dry crystals. [3]

Physics — 0625 · 6 syllabus areas

1. Motion, forces and energy [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A 1200 kg car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s. Calculate its acceleration and resultant force.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

a = (20−0)/10 = 2 m/s². F = ma = 1200 × 2 = 2400 N. [3]

2. Thermal physics [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain, using particle ideas, why a metal spoon becomes hot along its length when one end is placed in hot water.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

At the hot end, particles gain energy and free electrons transfer energy rapidly through the metal; collisions transfer energy to cooler regions, so thermal energy conducts along the spoon. [3]

3. Waves [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A wave has frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 0.8 m. Calculate its speed and state the equation.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

v = fλ = 50 × 0.8 = 40 m/s. [2]

4. Electricity and magnetism [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Two resistors of 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in parallel across a 12 V supply. Calculate the current in each branch and the total current.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Each branch has 12 V. I₁=12/3=4 A; I₂=12/6=2 A; total current = 4+2 = 6 A. [3]

5. Nuclear physics [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

A radioactive source has a half-life of 6 hours. Its activity is initially 800 counts/min. Calculate its activity after 18 hours.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

18 hours is three half-lives: 800 → 400 → 200 → 100 counts/min. [2]

6. Space physics [2 marks · 2026–2028 syllabus]

Explain why planets remain in orbit around the Sun rather than travelling in a straight line.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

The Sun's gravitational force provides the centripetal force, continually changing the direction of each planet's velocity and keeping it in orbit. [2]

Mathematics — 0580 · 9 syllabus areas

1. Number [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

A jacket is reduced by 12% to KSh 4,400. Calculate its original price.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

4,400 represents 88%. Original price = 4400 ÷ 0.88 = KSh 5,000. [2]

2. Algebra and graphs [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

Solve simultaneously: 2x + y = 11 and x − y = 1.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Add equations: 3x=12, so x=4. Substitute: 4−y=1, so y=3. [3]

3. Coordinate geometry [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

Find the gradient of the line through points (2, 3) and (6, 11).

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Gradient = (11−3)/(6−2) = 8/4 = 2. [2]

4. Geometry [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

The interior angles of a quadrilateral are x, 2x, 3x and 4x. Find the largest angle.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

x+2x+3x+4x=360°, so 10x=360°, x=36°. Largest angle = 4x = 144°. [3]

5. Mensuration [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Calculate its volume in terms of π.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

V=πr²h=π×3²×10=90π cm³. [2]

6. Trigonometry [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm and an angle of 30°. Calculate the side opposite the angle.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

sin 30° = opposite/13; opposite = 13 × 0.5 = 6.5 cm. [2]

7. Transformations and vectors [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

Point P(−2, 5) is translated by vector (4, −3). Find its image P′.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Add components: (−2+4, 5−3) = (2, 2). [2]

8. Probability [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

A bag contains 5 red, 3 blue and 2 green counters. One counter is selected at random. Find the probability it is not blue.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Not blue = 5+2=7 counters out of 10. Probability = 7/10. [2]

9. Statistics [2 marks · 2025–2027 syllabus]

The data are 4, 7, 7, 9, 13. Find the mean, median and mode.

Show worked answer and marking guidance

Mean = 40/5 = 8; median = 7; mode = 7. [3]
